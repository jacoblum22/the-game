# Squishes Rescale Numbers; Only Reworks Retire Content

World of Warcraft's twenty-year history supplies real, named, quantifiable prior art for Principle VI's demand for a "mandatory consolidation of aging content" mechanism — but the prior art is bifurcated in a way the constitution's current language doesn't yet distinguish: **stat/level squishes rescale numbers without retiring anything** (four events since 2014, each preserving relative power while cutting absolute numbers by roughly 90–96%), while **talent-system reworks and keystone removals actually retire and re-band content** (Cataclysm's point-count cut, Mists of Pandaria's tier flattening, Legion's seven-row simplification, Dragonflight's return to trees, and Path of Exile 2's folding of Acrobatics into base evasion) — and Blizzard's own internal research found that the second, real mechanism carries a measured cost ("rework after rework put off far more people than it turned on") that no LitRPG or TTRPG source in the first three research passes ever quantified. Separately, the computer-versus-tabletop complexity-ceiling research confirms and sharpens Pattern P3: the dividing line is not "how much state exists" but a hard, named categorical boundary between complexity a computer resolves silently (unlimited) and complexity it must present to a player (capped at roughly 3–5 simultaneous items while learning, 7±2 once familiar). Finally, Path of Exile's support-gem system surfaces a genuinely new primitive — deterministic, priority-ordered, mutually-exclusive behavioral tags (Pierce beats Fork beats Chain beats Return) — that solves the same "combination changes behavior" goal as Pattern P6 without requiring a bespoke named effect for every pairing, which does not scale the way P6 currently assumes. The net verdict below: Principle VI should be amended to add a lower-cost, PoE-league-style "provisional-by-default" consolidation path alongside its existing retirement/re-banding language; Pattern P3 should be amended to state the categorical boundary explicitly rather than leave it as a general risk statement; and P6 should gain a companion mechanism for combination effects that don't merit individual authorship.

## FFXIV, GW2, and EVE run three incompatible bets on where balance work goes

The three other major computer-driven MMORPGs each answer "how do you manage build diversity and balance at scale" with a structurally different bet, and none of them resembles WoW's talent-tree oscillation. Final Fantasy XIV bets on **architectural restriction**: each job gets exactly one weapon type, all abilities are learned by leveling and job quests, and the design goal is explicitly that "players who have progressed through similar levels remain on equal footing by default" ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/why-final-fantasy-xivs-job-system-is-more-restrictive-than-xi/)). This buys verifiable, near-perfect role parity — world-first raid clears show "every single job is viable for endgame content" ([FFXIV Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/39210/discussions/0/3195866053914644743)) — at a cost the community names precisely: players accuse the developers of having "prioritized perfect balance at the expense of job identity," citing Dark Knight losing MP management and Samurai losing its Kaiten resource button as casualties of the same tuning discipline ([FFXIV Forums](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/473154)). Director Naoki Yoshida has confirmed the mechanism is deliberate and admits it is compounding: "maintaining the uniqueness of job mechanics while balancing their numbers has become extremely taxing" as more jobs are added ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/ffxiv-dawntrail-interview-naoki-yoshida/)), and Patch 7.5's response was pure potency-slider tuning across jobs within role categories rather than structural redesign ([EpicCarry](https://epiccarry.com/blogs/ffxiv-patch-7-5-jobs-tier-list-job-balance-guide/)) — a scaling model that is linear in the number of jobs (each new job costs one more redesign-and-retune cycle) but caps the total permutation space by construction, since the weapon restriction eliminates most of the combinatorics WoW or PoE would have to balance.

Guild Wars 2 bets on **openness plus tolerance for convergence**. Every profession carries two weapon sets in combat, each contributing five skills determined by main-hand/off-hand/two-handed slotting rules, and elite specializations add whole new weapon types and trait lines on top of the base kit ([GW2 Wiki: Weapon swap](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Weapon_swap); [GW2 Wiki: Skill bar](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Skill_bar)). ArenaNet has published this philosophy explicitly as a "living document," with named goals of "encouraging build diversity and allowing players to express their mechanics mastery," implemented as "smaller overall scope in changes some months and major balance overhauls less frequently" ([MMORPG.com](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/guild-wars-2-releases-profession-balance-change-philosophy-policy-after-announce-2000126507); [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2022/10/29/guild-wars-2-makes-public-the-principles-guiding-its-current-balance-philosophy/)). This is a materially more transparent balance-communication practice than WoW's hotfix-notes-without-rationale model, and it is a candidate this project can copy directly: publish the *why*, not just the *what*, of a balance change. GW2 also removes the trinity itself — "every player [has] the simple means to defend themselves" through dodge/block mechanics rather than relying on a healer ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2019/03/28/massively-overthinking-thoughts-on-the-holy-trinity-in-mmos/)) — which structurally lightens the balance burden, since encounters no longer *require* specific roles. The tradeoff GW2 accepts openly is meta convergence: community threads explicitly discuss "the decline of build diversity" even under a design intended to prevent it ([MMORPG.com Forums](https://forums.mmorpg.com/discussion/459929/guild-wars-2-the-decline-of-build-diversity-mmorpg-com)), and the team's own stated response is incremental buffing of underused options rather than aggressive nerfs — a philosophy that anticipates PoE2's later, independently-arrived-at "buff, don't nerf" shift discussed below.

EVE Online bets on something neither WoW, FFXIV, nor GW2 attempts: **removing simultaneous choice entirely** by making specialization a function of elapsed real time rather than point allocation. Skills train in real time, even offline, at a rate set by attributes and a per-skill training-time multiplier, and training every skill in the game to maximum level would take roughly two decades of continuous training ([EVE University](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Skills_and_learning); [EVE Online Academy](https://www.eveonline.com/eve-academy/everyone-starts-somewhere)). Skill points, once trained, are never lost except through a costed Skill Extractor ([EVE Support](https://support.eveonline.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406388028178-Skill-Plans-and-How-They-Work)). This is the one system among the four that solves "everyone converges on the meta build" structurally rather than through balance patches: a character *cannot* be built optimally across every role because training time itself is the scarce resource, so specialization emerges from the passage of time rather than from a designer's point-cost audit. This is directly relevant to this project's own P11 concern (naive shared point-buy requires "an eagle-eyed GM" to police, which an AI DM cannot provide by default) — EVE's model sidesteps the audit problem category entirely rather than solving it, by making the currency time instead of points. The three systems scale differently with content growth, too: FFXIV scales linearly (each job costs one tuning cycle, but permutations are capped by the weapon restriction); GW2 scales closer to quadratically (each new weapon/trait/elite-spec multiplies combinations, managed only by accepting slow convergence); EVE scales logarithmically against its own total skill space, because at any given moment most players haven't trained most of the tree, so the "unbalanced" corner of the space is simply unreached by most players rather than balanced away.

| Dimension | WoW | FFXIV | GW2 | EVE Online | PoE / PoE2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core diversity lever | Talent trees (oscillating structure) | Job + weapon restriction | Weapon swap + traits + elite specs | Time-gated skill training | Skill gem + support gem + passive tree |
| Balance philosophy | Squish + hotfix + rework | Tight, verifiable role parity | "Living document," incremental | Emergent via economy + gradual patches | Buff-lean, seasonal reset |
| Primary cost of the bet | Rework fatigue, squish disruption | Mechanical homogenization | Meta convergence despite intent | Slow onboarding, months to pivot roles | Jewel/keystone power spikes |
| Documented mitigation | Talent tree redesigns (Dragonflight) | Potency-slider tuning by role | Buff underused skills, not nerf leaders | Skill extractors (costed respec) | Buff over nerf; league-scoped experiments |

## PoE's gem-priority chains show behavior-changing composition without bespoke names

Path of Exile's skill gem plus support gem system is the clearest available example in any of the four research passes of abilities that **chain and fundamentally change behavior when combined**, rather than merely stacking bigger numbers, and it does so through a mechanism this project's constitution has not yet catalogued: deterministic, priority-ordered, mutually-exclusive behavioral tags. A projectile skill's terminal behavior is decided by which of four support-gem tags it carries — Pierce (pass through and continue), Fork (split into two projectiles at ±60°), Chain (redirect to a new target on collision), or Return — and these resolve in a fixed order, Pierce first, then Fork, then Chain, so "a piercing, forking, chaining projectile will first pierce its maximum, then fork, then chain" ([PoE2 Stratlore Guide](https://poe2.stratlore.com/en/guides/projectile-pierce-fork-chain-return/); [PoE Wiki: Fork](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Fork)). No pairing gets an authored name — there is no "Piercing Fork" effect card — the behavior simply falls out of applying two mechanical rules in a fixed sequence. This is a distinct primitive from Pattern P6 (Named Composite Bonuses), which assumes combining two aligned primitives should surface a bespoke, individually-authored effect (Stormlight's "Three Lashings," Mistborn's Compounding). PoE's model instead defines a small number of composable behavioral verbs and a total order for resolving conflicts between them — which scales combinatorially without requiring a human designer to author every pairwise interaction by hand, exactly the property P6 lacks once the primitive count grows past a handful.

The system also demonstrates genuine behavior-restructuring, not scalar bonus stacking, through its penalty design: Multiple Projectiles support fires two additional projectiles but imposes a flat 20% less projectile damage and 20% less attack/cast speed in PoE2 (26% less damage for four extra projectiles in PoE1's Greater Multiple Projectiles) ([Fextralife](https://pathofexile2.wiki.fextralife.com/Multiple_Projectiles); [PoE Wiki: GMP](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Greater_Multiple_Projectiles_Support)) — a single-target skill becomes an area spreader at the explicit cost of per-hit power, and the "less damage" modifier notably does not apply to damage-over-time effects like Poison, making the same support gem read as a pure upgrade on one build archetype and a genuine tradeoff on another ([exile-tools](https://exile-tools.com/gemplanner/gem_info?gem_name=Greater+Multiple+Projectiles+Support&from_cache_id=e974c2bf97d30dfd81c47628d92aa33c)). PoE2's architectural shift — moving support-gem sockets from gear (PoE1, requiring players to hunt for correctly-linked items) directly onto the skill gem itself, capped at five supports per skill with a hard rule that only one copy of any given support gem may appear in a player's entire loadout ([Gamerant](https://gamerant.com/path-of-exile-2-equip-support-gems-guide-poe-2-support-gem-system/); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/path-of-exile-2-skill-gems-explained/)) — is itself a consolidation move: it retires the entire "gear-socket hunting" subsystem in favor of direct character-bound customization, and the one-copy-per-loadout rule is a structural anti-stacking guardrail that prevents the additive-bloat failure mode Principle VI already worries about, achieved through a hard cap rather than an audit process.

The passive tree contributes a second, independent finding relevant to Principle VI directly: PoE2 did not just add nodes on top of PoE1's roughly 2,831-node tree — it deliberately *removed* categories of content. Life nodes were eliminated entirely, pushing survivability onto gear ([Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/poe-2-passive-tree-and-dual-specialization)); Masteries were removed outright ([PoECurrency](https://www.poecurrency.com/news/poe-2-what-are-the-changes-coming-to-passive-tree)); and the Acrobatics keystone — previously an all-or-nothing dodge-for-defense-penalty trade — was deleted as a distinct choice and its core mechanic (evasion applying to all hits, not just some) was folded directly into the base evasion system ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/path-of-exile-2-acrobatics-keystone-removed/)). This is a clean, concrete instance of exactly what Principle VI asks for: an old, cliff-edge power spike (Acrobatics) was retired by redistributing its effect into the base system rather than either leaving it to rot unused or bolting a bigger version on top. Ascendancy classes add a fourth, largely independent progression vector on top of base class, skill gems, and passive tree — 12 base classes each branching into 3 ascendancies for 36 effective subclasses — unlocked through campaign trials rather than PoE1's endgame-gated Labyrinth, and swappable later without touching the base class choice ([Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/poe-2-ascendancy-overview); [Dving](https://dving.net/guides/path-of-exile-2-guides/ascendancies-overview)). This "swap the specialization, keep the identity" design is a second concrete precedent worth flagging alongside GW2's elite-spec model for how a system can offer major respec flexibility without touching the player's foundational choice — directly relevant to Pattern P4's "trade-offs locked at creation" language, since PoE2 shows one axis (ascendancy) can stay flexible while another (base class) stays locked, rather than treating "locked at creation" as all-or-nothing.

GGG's balance-communication practice supplies the last concrete, transferable finding here. The studio runs a **seasonal league structure**: each three-month league introduces exactly one new mechanic, tested live, and afterward GGG either integrates it into the permanent game, reworks it, or discards it entirely ([arpgseasons.com](https://www.arpgseasons.com/en/guides/poe-league-cycle)). This is a fundamentally different answer to "how do you add content without permanent bloat" than anything in the first three research passes: rather than adding content and later having to retire it (WoW's costly, backlash-prone path), PoE never grants new content permanent status in the first place until it has been observed in live play. Recent league reviews credit this model with a genuine build-diversity success — "almost every ascendancy and class [has] multiple playable options," attributed explicitly to GGG "focusing on balancing through buffs rather than heavy nerfs" ([MMOexp](https://www.mmoexp.com/News/path-of-exile-2-league-review-build-diversity-endgame-freedom-economy-problems-and-the-road-to-1-0.html)) — while also surfacing a live cautionary tale: jewels became powerful enough in the current league that "if every build simply collects as many jewel sockets as possible, the game risks losing one of its greatest strengths: creativity" ([MMOexp](https://www.mmoexp.com/News/path-of-exile-2-jewels-are-too-powerful-why-the-current-jewel-meta-is-destroying-build-diversity.html)) — a single layer of a four-layer system (class/ascendancy/gems/tree/jewels) overwhelming the others, confirming P3's warning that granularity needs active reconciliation even inside a computer-driven system with no human-GM bottleneck at all.

## The complexity ceiling sits at player comprehension, confirming and sharpening Pattern P3

The dedicated complexity-ceiling research answers this pass's cross-cutting question directly, and the answer refines rather than simply confirms Pattern P3. The dividing line between "computers can handle unlimited complexity" and "even ambitious computer games still simplify" is not fuzzy — it is a **named categorical boundary** in game-design literature between comprehension complexity (understanding the rules — harmful if excessive), tracking complexity (mentally managing multiple simultaneous elements — harmful if excessive), and depth (difficulty of finding the best move — valuable and worth maximizing) ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/design-101-complexity-vs-depth)). Below that line, background-resolved complexity is empirically unlimited in live, shipped MMORPGs. WoW's threat system layers a baseline (1 damage = 1 threat) with class-specific multipliers (a 5.0x threat modifier for tank abilities like Righteous Fury), per-ability bonus threat on top of the damage-derived baseline, and a distinct halved-and-divided formula for healing threat — an opaque enough calculation that until Wrath of the Lich King, threat was entirely hidden and reverse-engineered by the community through trial-and-error add-ons ([Wowpedia: Threat](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Threat); [Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/threat-guide-reference-table/)). Crowd-control diminishing returns (each successive application halving duration down to 1/16th before a 16-second reset) and stat diminishing returns (introduced in Shadowlands specifically to punish over-stacking a single secondary stat past 30%) run continuously, invisibly, for every player in every pull ([Maxroll: CC DR](https://maxroll.gg/wow/resources/crowd-control-diminishing-returns); [Maxroll: Stat DR](https://maxroll.gg/wow/resources/stat-diminishing-returns)). Path of Exile's own damage pipeline is dense enough that Path of Building, a third-party calculator, is needed to compute DPS "by factoring in auras, buffs, charges, curses, monster resistances and more" ([Path of Building Community](https://pathofbuilding.community/)) — none of this is computationally constrained; it is kept invisible because a player cannot meaningfully "optimize the threat table," only follow rules the designer has already resolved.

At the line, the same games hit a hard, documented wall the moment complexity becomes player-facing. WoW's default UI caps visible debuff icons at 16 even though up to 40 can exist simultaneously on a raid boss — a pure UI comprehension limit players had to route around with third-party add-ons like BigDebuffs ([WoW Forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/debuff-limit-and-target-frame/1094410)). The Global Cooldown itself is a deliberately engineered comprehension throttle, not a technical limitation: Game Director Ion Hazzikostas stated explicitly that the point of the GCD is to give "individual abilities more significance again" by preventing a player from stacking multiple simultaneous effects into a single decision-free action — "without the global cooldown, the decision is easy because there is none" ([Gamesear](https://www.gamesear.com/news/world-of-warcraft-will-soon-be-reverting-the-rather-controversial-global-cooldown-change)). And Path of Exile — the one game in this pass with genuinely no human GM anywhere in the loop — did not escape the ceiling either; it simply moved it. Its passive tree runs to over 1,200–1,500 meaningfully distinct nodes ([VULKK](https://vulkk.com/2025/02/08/path-of-exile-2-passive-tree-guide/)), and the game went years without an official build planner, relying entirely on the third-party Path of Building until GGG eventually built an official equivalent and formally supported it ([pathofbuilding.net](https://pathofbuilding.net/path-of-exile-passive-skill-tree-calculator/); [PoE Build Planner](https://poe.com/Build-Planner)). Removing the human-GM bottleneck did not remove the complexity ceiling; it relocated the ceiling from "the GM can't track forty calculations" to "the player can't hold fifteen hundred passive nodes in working memory," and the fix GGG converged on — officially blessing an external, queryable optimization tool rather than simplifying the tree — is exactly Pattern P7's "player-facing reference layer" prescription, independently arrived at by a live commercial studio under real player pressure.

The categorical boundary sits at a specific, citable number, not a vague sense of "too much": working-memory research puts the practical limit at roughly **3 items when a player is actively learning a new system, expanding to 5±2 once the task is familiar** ([Celia Hodent, GDC](https://celiahodent.com/gamers-brain-ux-onboarding/)), which is the empirical basis behind the general "7±2" ceiling cited across game-UI design guidance for how many simultaneous choices or information elements a designer should ever surface at once ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/advice/0/how-do-you-reduce-cognitive-load-game-design)). This gives Pattern P3 something it currently lacks: a testable number, not just a directional claim. It also surfaces a second nuance P3's current text doesn't capture — that the correct amount of player-facing complexity is not a one-time design decision but requires live calibration in both directions. WoW's Global Cooldown was expanded in Battle for Azeroth to cover more offensive-buff abilities specifically to force more meaningful sequencing, but the change backfired: "affected abilities didn't change in any significant way, so players still had to go through exactly the same rotations as before, just with far more stop-gaps sprinkled throughout," and Blizzard reversed most of the change in Shadowlands after sustained complaint ([Gamesear](https://www.gamesear.com/news/world-of-warcraft-will-soon-be-reverting-the-rather-controversial-global-cooldown-change); [MMO-Champion](https://www.mmo-champion.com/threads/2389509-New-Global-Cooldown/page13)). The lesson is that Blizzard confused *pace reduction* with *comprehension reduction* — slowing the game down is not the same intervention as lowering the number of things a player must simultaneously track — and got the calibration wrong in a direction P3 doesn't currently warn against: over-simplifying can be its own live-service mistake, correctable only by watching real play and reversing course, exactly the same reconciliation discipline P3 already demands for granular state, now shown to apply symmetrically to the simplification side of the ledger too.

## What this pass changes in the constitution

Principle VI is confirmed as directionally correct and gains its first quantified prior art, but the WoW evidence shows the constitution's current single mechanism ("retiring, replacing, or re-banding") conflates two things that behave very differently in practice, and the PoE evidence supplies a third mechanism that is strictly better where it applies. **Squishes are not consolidation.** The four WoW stat/level squishes (2014's ~96% stat and ~92% health reduction demonstrated at BlizzCon before shipping; 2018's stat-and-itemlevel-only pass; 2020's unprecedented character-level squish from 120 to 50, the first level squish in sixteen years of live service; 2024's stat-and-itemlevel repeat) all preserve relative power exactly — Blizzard's own framing that a Fireball dealing 15% of a target's health continues dealing 15% after the squish is mechanically accurate ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Stat_squish); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/world-warcraft-patch-60-warlords-draenor/)) — but they retire nothing; they only rescale the numbers old and new content already produce. The recurrence interval (2014, 2018, 2020, 2024 — every four to six expansions) is itself the tell: squishing is a **postponement mechanism for arithmetic bloat**, not a solution to it, and every cycle proves the underlying growth resumes. **Talent reworks are consolidation, and they are measurably costly.** Cataclysm's cut from roughly 71 to 41 talent points, Mists of Pandaria's collapse from 80+ choices to a discrete 18 (six tiers of three), Legion's seven-row single-choice structure, and Dragonflight's 2022 return to branching trees are each an explicit removal or restructuring of prior content, not an addition on top of it ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2022/10/31/wow-cataclysm-talent-trees/); [Wowpedia](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Talents_(history))) — and Blizzard's own player research, cited as the direct justification for the Dragonflight redesign, found that "large-scale reworks of classes, especially rework after rework, put off far more people than it turned on" ([AlwaysNintendo](https://alwaysnintendo.com/the-most-controversial-class-changes-blizzard-ever-implemented-and-the-player-backlash/)). The Survival Hunter melee conversion in Legion (2016) is the sharpest documented case study of what this costs in player reaction: a specialization's core identity changed with no reversion path, splitting the community into "revert it" and "refine it" camps for at least two full expansions, with Blizzard's own lead systems designer conceding by 2017 that "Survival Hunter execution needs work" while still declining to reverse the change ([Eyes of the Beast](https://eyesofthebeast.com/2015/08/legion-the-melee-hunter-returns/); [MMO-Champion](https://www.mmo-champion.com/threads/2341568-Thoughts-on-Survival-Hunter-Changes/page2)). This is the first quantified evidence in any of the four research passes that retirement/re-banding has a real, empirically-observed cost ceiling, not just a design-purity cost — and Principle VI's current text does not acknowledge that cost or tell a future feature spec how to budget for it.

PoE's seasonal league structure supplies a third mechanism this project should adopt as the *preferred* path precisely because it sidesteps that cost. Rather than granting new content permanent status and later having to retire an entrenched player favorite (WoW's expensive path), a league-scoped mechanic is explicitly provisional from the moment it ships — three months of live data, then integrate, rework, or discard ([arpgseasons.com](https://www.arpgseasons.com/en/guides/poe-league-cycle)) — so retirement of a *failed* mechanic never triggers the sunk-cost backlash retirement of an *entrenched* one does, because players were told up front it might not stay. This is a genuinely new consolidation mechanism beyond anything the first three research passes found (Dungeon Crawler Carl's specialization-forking, currently Principle VI's only cited clean precedent, only handles the retirement of a skill's own growth path, not new system-level content generally), and it converts the hard problem "how do we take something away that players love" into the much easier problem "how do we decide whether to keep something we always flagged as trial." The recommended amendment:

| | Current text | Proposed amendment |
|---|---|---|
| Principle VI | "The system MUST include an explicit, telegraphed mechanism for retiring, replacing, or re-banding old content as new content is added... A feature spec that adds progression content MUST state how older content is consolidated, re-banded, or retired as part of that content, not leave it for a later cleanup pass." | Add: "New progression content SHOULD default to explicitly provisional status (a bounded trial window, analogous to Path of Exile's three-month league cycle) before being granted permanent status, rather than launching as permanent and requiring later retirement; this converts the harder problem of removing entrenched content into the easier problem of declining to promote untested content. Where content has already been granted permanent status and must later be retired or re-banded regardless, the spec MUST budget for the documented cost of that retirement (WoW's Survival Hunter rework and Blizzard's own finding that repeated large-scale reworks measurably reduce player retention are the reference case) by bundling retirement into infrequent, clearly-telegraphed boundary points rather than continuous piecemeal changes, and by distinguishing pure numeric rescaling (a squish, which preserves relative power but retires nothing) from structural retirement (removing or folding a mechanic, which is the actual consolidation this principle requires) — a spec MUST NOT claim a squish-style rescaling satisfies this principle's retirement requirement." |

Pattern P3 is confirmed by the complexity-ceiling research about as cleanly as any claim in this project's constitution has been confirmed, and it should be sharpened with the specific boundary the research names. The current text says the risk is "narration drifting out of sync with ground-truth state," which is correct but incomplete — the complexity-ceiling research shows the risk category is broader than narration-desync alone; it is *any* player-facing presentation of state that exceeds roughly 3–5 simultaneous items during learning (7±2 once familiar), whether that presentation is prose narration, a UI list, or an ability menu. The GCD-expansion-and-reversal case additionally shows the failure runs in both directions: over-simplifying (removing decision points under the banner of "reducing complexity") is its own live-service mistake requiring the same reconciliation-and-correction discipline P3 already demands for over-granular state. Recommended amendment:

| | Current text | Proposed amendment |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern P3 | "...the actual adjudication risk is narration drifting out of sync with ground-truth state... not the volume of tracked numbers." | Add: "This risk generalizes beyond narration specifically: the fourth research pass confirms a named categorical boundary in computer-driven games between background-resolved complexity (state the system computes but never surfaces to the player — WoW's threat tables and diminishing-returns curves, PoE's full damage-modifier stack — which can be arbitrarily granular with zero risk) and player-facing complexity (state the player must hold in working memory to make a decision — abilities, active effects, build choices), which hits a hard, citable comprehension wall at roughly 3–5 simultaneous items while a mechanic is being learned and 7±2 once familiar. A feature spec MUST classify each piece of tracked state as background-resolved or player-facing and MUST NOT budget player-facing complexity past this wall on the assumption that computation is cheap — computation being cheap is exactly why background-resolved state is safe and irrelevant to whether player-facing state is safe. Separately, simplification itself is not risk-free: WoW's Global Cooldown expansion-then-reversal shows that removing decision points can be miscalibrated in the same way over-granular state can, so any deliberate simplification MUST be treated as a hypothesis to verify against real play, not a one-time design decision."|

Two further, smaller candidate mechanics from this pass are worth flagging against Pattern P6 specifically, since P6 currently assumes every meaningful combination gets a bespoke authored name, which does not scale past a handful of primitives. PoE's Pierce-beats-Fork-beats-Chain-beats-Return priority order shows an alternative that P6 should adopt as a companion mechanism rather than a replacement: define a small closed set of composable behavioral tags plus a fixed total resolution order among them, and let combination-driven behavior change fall out of applying that order deterministically, reserving bespoke named effects (P6 as currently written) for the smaller set of combinations distinctive enough to deserve one. This is directly reusable for this project's own ability system without requiring the design team to hand-author every pairwise interaction as new primitives are added. WoW's asymmetric global-cooldown-based class differentiation (agility-melee classes at a 1.0-second base GCD versus 1.5 seconds for everyone else, with haste explicitly not affecting the 1.0-second tier) is a second new, low-overhead primitive worth naming separately: it creates a durable, structural feel-difference between class groups using a single cadence parameter rather than a unique ability list, at essentially zero additional balance-audit surface ([Wowpedia: Cooldown](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Cooldown)). Finally, EVE's training-time-enforced specialization is worth recording as a named alternative to point-buy for any future decision under P11 or P4: rather than auditing every option's point cost (the discipline P11 already demands and flags as high-maintenance), specialization can instead be enforced by making broad access take proportionally longer to acquire, sidestepping the audit-everything requirement by construction rather than by policing it.

## Conclusion

The deepest shift this pass produces is not a new fact about any single game but a reframing of what "prior art" means for Principle VI. The first three research passes searched fiction and tabletop systems and found none that solved consolidation cleanly; this pass finds that even the best-resourced live computer game in the genre — twenty years of continuous iteration, a dedicated balance team, direct telemetry on every player's rotation — has *also* not solved it cleanly, only bought two different, imperfect coping mechanisms (postponement via squish, costly-but-real retirement via rework) plus, in Path of Exile's separate lineage, a third mechanism that avoids the cost by never granting permanence prematurely. That third mechanism is the one genuinely new idea this pass contributes to Principle VI, and it is a stronger fit for an AI-driven, no-human-GM project than either WoW mechanism, because it requires no player-facing "we are taking this away" moment at all — only a "we are deciding whether to keep this" moment, framed as such from day one. On Pattern P3, the finding is cleaner still: every category of evidence this pass gathered — threat tables, diminishing-returns curves, PoE's damage pipeline, debuff-icon caps, the Global Cooldown's whole design history, Path of Building's near-mandatory adoption — lands on the same side of the same line, which is about as strong a confirmation as an unproven pattern can get from external research, and the line itself (background-resolved vs. player-facing, with a citable 3–5/7±2 numeric wall) is exactly the kind of concrete, testable criterion this project's constitution should prefer over a directional intuition.
