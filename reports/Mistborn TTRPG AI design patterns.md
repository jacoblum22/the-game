# New evidence sharpens the AI game-master blueprint

This second research pass does not overturn the prior report's three conclusions — that build diversity should come from small, closed combinatorial primitives; that a hidden variance layer beneath visible choices makes builds feel personal; and that additive stacking without retirement is the universal failure mode of long-running LitRPG systems — it **confirms all three from entirely new source material and then supplies the missing pieces**: concrete resource-cost shapes beyond "burn a consumable" (Mistborn's Feruchemy is a debt-and-repayment economy, not a mana pool), a real, tabletop-tested fix for the "hybrid superclass makes everyone else obsolete" problem (the Mistborn Adventure Game's answer to Mistborn-vs-Misting balance), a deterministic four-way success/failure branch that an AI can compute without judgment calls (Pathfinder 2e's degrees of success), and — most importantly for this project's central open question — the first documented architecture pattern for gating an AI game master's ambiguous rulings behind a bounded, calibrated classifier rather than trusting either free-form narration or a same-model "judge." The single most load-bearing new finding is architectural, not thematic: across academic function-calling papers, generative-agent research, and AI Dungeon's own multi-year public failure history, every credible source converges on the same three-layer split — deterministic tool calls own hard mechanics, an externalized structured store (not the context window) owns world state, and a narrow, calibrated classifier gate sits between "player proposes something ambiguous" and "the AI narrates it as true," ideally with the option to abstain rather than force a binary verdict. That architecture, paired with Mistborn's debt-cost mechanic and Sanderson's own steal-and-graft exception system (Hemalurgy), gives this project's constitution its most concrete new toolkit yet.

## Five more progression-fantasy series confirm closed primitives and add two new resource shapes

Dungeon Crawler Carl, The Primal Hunter, Cradle, Mother of Learning, and Beware of Chicken were chosen specifically because each documents a mechanic the four prior series didn't, and each lands on the same load-bearing pattern from a different angle. Dungeon Crawler Carl's skills **level automatically through use rather than by spending points**, climbing to roughly level 10 before forcing a branching specialization choice — e.g., a generic "Explosives Handling" skill forks into "Dangerous Explosives Handling" or "Goblin Explosives," a trade-off between raw damage, elemental effects, and reduced mana cost rather than pure stacking ([Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki: Skills](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Skills); [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/skills/)). This is the clearest real-world example in either research pass of a system that **replaces rather than purely adds** — the specialization fork retires the generic skill's further growth path — and it pairs with a deliberate pacing gate: stat-point allocation is locked entirely until "Floor 3," meaning new players learn action-based advancement before facing a permanent optimization decision ([Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/skills/)). The Primal Hunter goes the opposite direction, and is useful precisely as a cautionary counter-example: classes grant wildly uneven per-level stat packages (one cited example awards +175 Perception, +150 Agility, +125 Strength, and +125 freely-assignable points in a single level-up), and the series compensates for that inflation with a **hard cap that trades ceiling for restriction** — 22 total stats at "E-grade" for an unrestricted profession versus 27 for one carrying severe build constraints ([The Primal Hunter Wiki: Classes](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/Classes); [The Primal Hunter Wiki: Grades](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/Grades)). That cap-for-restriction trade is a genuinely new balancing lever this pass surfaced: it lets a design offer glass-cannon specialization without a flexible generalist path ever being strictly dominated, because the restriction is qualitative rather than a raw numeric penalty.

Cradle supplies the strongest evidence yet that coarse, rank-based resolution can carry a rich-feeling system: build diversity comes from exactly two orthogonal, combinable primitives — which madra "aspect" a Path uses (earth, fire, blood, and so on) and which of four technique archetypes (Striker, Enforcer, Forger, Ruler) are layered onto it — and the entire power curve above that is compressed into a single small-integer rank variable, with a stated rule of thumb that **it takes roughly ten Coppers to challenge one Iron, and ten Irons to challenge one Jade** ([Abidan Archive Wiki: Sacred Arts](https://wiki.abidanarchive.com/index.php/Sacred_Arts); [One Last Forum: Cradle discussion](https://onelastforum.com/threads/cradle-by-will-wight-discussion-and-analysis.897/)). This is the single lowest-bookkeeping-burden resolution scheme in either research pass: an adjudicator, human or AI, mostly needs to track one number per combatant, at the cost of no mechanical distinction between two characters of identical rank unless the GM improvises one. Mother of Learning contributes the cleanest anti-power-creep mechanism found in this survey: magic is built from modular "spell elements," and **humans cannot invent new spell elements at all — the primitive set is permanently fixed** — while existing elements can be recombined in essentially unlimited novel ways, which is the series' explicit, stated build-diversity engine ([Mother of Learning Universe: Basics of Magic](https://motheroflearninguniverse.wordpress.com/2017/08/10/basics-of-magic-spellcasting/)). Its time-loop structure adds a second idea worth stealing directly: raw power (mana capacity, physical conditioning) resets every loop, but **procedural knowledge — which element combinations work — persists**, meaning the "build" a player accumulates is a knowledge-graph rather than a stat sheet, a persistence model with no analogue in any of the other eight series surveyed across both passes. Beware of Chicken closes the set as the deliberate null case: a dedicated technical-analysis source concluded outright that "concrete mechanical details about cultivation systems are notably absent," because the series redirects its cultivation trappings toward "farming yields, animal companion development, and philosophical growth" rather than quantified power ([Immortal Cultivation Hub](https://immortalcultivationhub.com/beware-of-chicken-cultivation-novel/)) — proof that a progression-fantasy framing can work narratively with mechanics kept almost entirely off-page, at the cost of offloading essentially all resolution to GM fiat, which is the one option unavailable to a system that specifically wants to stay adjudicable.

## Mistborn's three magic systems add a debt economy, a lossy exception mechanic, and a tabletop-tested fix for the "everyone else sucks" problem

Allomancy formalizes the same closed-grid discipline Surgebinding uses, but through a different geometry: **16 metals sit in a fixed 4-grouping × internal/external × push/pull matrix** (Physical, Mental, Temporal, Enhancement), with every base metal paired to exactly one alloy along Newtonian push/pull lines that Sanderson traced directly to wanting a limited version of Star Wars telekinesis — iron pulls metal toward the user, its alloy steel pushes it away, and the same "push" resolves differently against a light object (the object flies) versus a heavy anchor (the user flies), a physics-grounded limitation baked directly into the verb rather than bolted on as flavor text ([winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-explains-how-he-came-up-with-the-mistborn-magic-system); [crrowenson.com](https://crrowenson.com/analysis-of-magic-systems/allomancy-perfect-blend-of-physics-and-imagination/)). Access is binary rather than staged: a **Misting burns exactly one of the eight basic metals for life; a Mistborn burns all of them simultaneously** — no incremental unlock path exists between the two, which is a starker cliff than Surgebinding's every-Order-gets-exactly-two-Surges structure ([readingseconds.com](https://readingseconds.com/allomancy-chart-mistborn-metals-explained/)). Feruchemy is the more important discovery of this pass precisely because it is a *structurally different resource shape*, not a reskin: rather than consuming a resource for an effect, a Feruchemist first **voluntarily weakens an attribute to charge a "metalmind," then later taps that same metalmind to retrieve exactly what was stored, at the cost of a period of weakness** — a debt-and-repayment loop that nets to zero across the two phases, explicitly labeled in-world as the art of *balance* in contrast to Allomancy's one-way *Preservation*-flavored burn ([WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind: Feruchemy](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Feruchemy)). For a system that wants ability costs beyond "spend a consumable charge," this is a second, fully fleshed-out cost shape ready to borrow directly: temporarily lower a stat now to raise it later, conserved rather than generated from nothing.

Hemalurgy is Mistborn's cleanest instance of a formal, in-fiction-costed exception mechanic: a metal spike driven through a donor's heart and then into a recipient at a specific body location **steals a specific Allomantic or Feruchemical power**, and the transfer is explicitly, narratively lossy — labeled the art of *Ruin* precisely because "the transfer of power destroys some of it" ([WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind: Hemalurgy](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)). Critically, the loss is not one cost but **two independent axes**: a quantitative tax baked into the act of spiking itself (some Investiture always evaporates, with a separate, partially-mitigable decay tax for time spent out-of-body), and a wholly separate qualitative tax — a Hemalurgically-enhanced being becomes structurally more susceptible to external control, the mechanism by which kandra and koloss can be commanded by outside Investiture ([WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)). A homebrew "grant an exception to normal rules" mechanic can charge both taxes independently rather than a single flat penalty. The encoding trick underneath it is equally transferable: rather than a lookup table mapping every metal to a body location, **the location on the body and the method of the donor's death together encode which power is granted** — a designed solution to a combinatorial-explosion problem (16 metals × 2 source systems × many possible locations) that an AI-DM exception system facing its own combinatorial targeting space could copy structurally. Layered on top, a Twinborn (a Metalborn with one Allomantic and one Feruchemical power) unlocks **Compounding** when — and only when — both powers share a metal: burning the charged metalmind Allomantically releases the stored Feruchemical attribute **tenfold**, a categorically new ability that exists in neither parent system alone ([WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind: Twinborn](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Twinborn); [WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind: Compounding](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Compounding)). This is a second real-world confirmation of a pattern the prior report only inferred from Stormlight's named composite techniques: reward *aligned* combinations of primitives with an emergent bonus, not just cumulative access, so the combinatorics are non-linear rather than merely multiplicative.

The single most directly actionable finding in this entire research pass, though, is not thematic at all — it is a **solved instance of this project's core design problem**, documented by name. Crafty Games, adapting Mistborn into the Mistborn Adventure Game, stated their central design risk explicitly: "how do you let people play Mistborn and not have everybody else suck." Their fix was to **decouple raw power-access breadth from starting competence** — Mistborn characters begin with deliberately mediocre stats, and every character (Mistborn or Misting alike) allocates an individualized strength/average/weakness spread across attributes, so the sixteen-power hybrid does not simply dominate the one-power specialist at the table ([WebSearch synthesis of Mistborn Adventure Game design discussion](https://fandomwire.com/10-most-overpowered-metallic-arts-in-mistborn-ranked/)). This is exactly the "hybrid superclass vs. single-power specialist" problem this project's LitRPG-style builds will face (a player who wants "every essence" versus one who wants "one essence, deeply"), and it now has a tested, ready-made tabletop answer: gate the *access breadth* axis and the *starting-power* axis independently, rather than letting more access automatically mean more power at level one.

## TTRPG balance theory supplies the deterministic adjudication math an AI GM actually needs

Pathfinder 2e is the single most transferable system in this research pass because two of its mechanics are, by design, computable without judgment. Every character draws from the **same flat pool of three actions plus one reaction per turn**, regardless of build, which pushes all tactical build diversity into *what fills the actions* rather than *how many actions a build gets* — a direct structural defense against action-economy-stacking exploits ([Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/pathfinder-2e-action-economy-guide-beginner/)). Layered on top, PF2e replaces binary pass/fail with **four degrees of success** — critical success, success, failure, critical failure — computed deterministically from how far a roll beats or misses the DC (beating by 10 or more upgrades a success to critical; missing by 10 or more downgrades a failure to critical; a natural 20 or 1 shifts the result one step) ([Archives of Nethys](https://2e.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=1026); [Pathfinder Authority](https://pathfinderauthority.com/pathfinder-critical-hits-and-success-degrees)). This turns "did the improvised action work?" into a pure function of (roll + modifiers) versus a target number — precisely the kind of ambiguity-free branch a live AI adjudicator needs and a human GM's "rulings over rules" 5e-style improvisation cannot offer. The cost is documented too: designers and critics alike note the tight math that eliminates "trap options" also makes individual bonuses "feel less impactful," since every option must be exhaustively audited against a shared power budget to guarantee viability ([EN World forum synthesis](https://www.enworld.org/threads/regarding-the-complexity-of-pathfinder-2.675063/)). Bounded accuracy — 5e's technique of capping how far attack, AC, DC, and save bonuses can climb with level (theoretical max total bonus around +11) — solves a related but distinct problem: it keeps low-level threats mechanically relevant at high level so the d20's roll always matters, but it explicitly does not resolve capability disparity between build archetypes, since two characters can share identical to-hit math while one's kit reshapes reality and the other's does not — the well-documented "linear fighter, quadratic wizard" gap that survived even the 2024 PHB revision ([DnD Lore Wiki: Bounded Accuracy](https://dungeonsdragons.fandom.com/wiki/bounded_accuracy); [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/are-martial-classes-worth-it)). The lesson for this project is that capping numeric spread and equalizing capability are two separate design levers, and bounded accuracy only buys the first one.

GURPS supplies the cleanest warning against a naive point-buy approach to "endless customizability." Its own designers frame the point pool's purpose bluntly: "the points hadn't created balance — they had created identity," illustrated by the example that a 200-point physicist "can be chopped into kindling by a 40-point combat-optimized fighter" ([Kate Plays / GURPS 4th Revised](https://kateplays.substack.com/p/gurps-4th-revised)). The system's documented failure mode is structural, not incidental: because a flat +1 to an attribute like IQ raises every dependent skill simultaneously, it is cheaper than raising individual skills, rewarding attribute min-maxing over skill investment, and disadvantages are "very easy" to game by taking ones with "little or no tangible effect on play" purely to refund points into combat-relevant purchases — a failure the ruleset itself does not prevent, requiring "an eagle-eyed GM" to police at the table ([Cannibal Halfling Gaming](https://cannibalhalflinggaming.com/2024/02/14/system-hack-gurps-disadvantages/); [GiantITP forum](https://forums.giantitp.com/showthread.php?p=25430690)). For an AI GM with no equivalent of table-side social pressure, a shared point-pool is unsafe unless every option carries a rigorously audited, context-independent cost — precisely the PF2e discipline, not the GURPS one. Powered by the Apocalypse contributes the opposite lesson: distinctiveness doesn't have to come from numbers at all. Each playbook gets a small, hand-authored list of named "moves," each with a fully pre-specified trigger and 2d6-based resolution band (10+ full success, 7–9 partial success/hard choice, 6- the GM makes a move), so the design goal of "failure moves the plot forward" is achieved by making the mixed 7–9 result the statistically most common outcome ([StartPlaying blog](https://startplaying.games/blog/posts/what-is-powered-by-the-apocalypse)). Because every move's trigger and resolution is designer-specified in advance, this is a low-ambiguity fallback pattern for flavor-driven or narrative abilities that don't need — or shouldn't get — full numeric modeling.

Two further threads round out the theory. Challenge Rating's well-documented breakdown — "four characters at fifth level sometimes can easily beat a monster of CR 9 or 10, yet die because of several creatures of CR 1," a failure attributed to ignoring action economy and monster-count scaling relative to party size ([D&D Beyond forums synthesis](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/dungeons-dragons-discussion/dungeon-masters-only/26409-is-cr-broken-for-building-encounters-my-players)) — is the encounter-design mirror of the character-build balance problem, and the same lesson applies: compressing a multidimensional space into one scalar rating fails, so an AI GM should favor a simulated or multi-axis check over a single CR-style number, especially for player-generated content. Justin Alexander's Alexandrian essays supply the sharpest available vocabulary for what "balance" even means: **Concept balance** (all character concepts equally viable), **Naturalistic balance** (accepting realistic disparities the setting implies), and **Spotlight balance** (the GM actively ensuring every build gets scenes that showcase it) are three genuinely different, non-interchangeable goals, and Alexander is explicit that Spotlight balance is "more an art than a science" — pure GM runtime judgment, not a codified mechanic ([The Alexandrian: The Many Types of Balance](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/2500/roleplaying-games/the-many-types-of-balance)). That is a direct, actionable gap for this project: an AI GM has no native equivalent of a human GM's felt sense that "the rogue hasn't had a moment in three sessions," so Spotlight balance needs to be engineered as an explicit scene-framing heuristic rather than assumed to fall out of good mechanical design. His "Optimization Arms Race" essay treats optimization as structurally inevitable in any system with meaningful choices, and explicitly recommends *not* escalating encounter difficulty in response — instead **diversifying** the challenges a build must face (so no single specialization dominates every scene) and shifting focus to narrative consequences over one-upping the player ([The Alexandrian: Optimization Arms Race](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/35015/roleplaying-games/thought-of-the-day-optimization-arms-race)) — a scenario-generation-level lever an AI GM can apply independently of whatever character-build rules it enforces.

## AI Dungeon's decade of failures and the emerging judge-gate pattern define the architecture question directly

AI Dungeon is the field's most-cited cautionary tale, and its documented failures split cleanly into two unrelated classes that this project should not conflate. The first is a **safety/trust failure**: a 2021 crisis over CSAM generation forced Latitude to ship an emergency content filter under pressure from OpenAI, and that filter's high false-positive rate "soft-locked" innocent scenarios badly enough to crash the game's Google Play rating from 4.8 to 2.6, compounded by a separate backlash when Latitude announced (and later walked back) that it would read users' private stories to build the filter ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/04/30/ai_dungeon_filter_vulnerabilities/); [Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/archive/2021/06/22/latitude-games-ai-dungeon-was-changing-the-face-of-ai-generated-content-until-its-users-turned-against-it/)). The second, entirely separate failure is a **pure narrative-grounding problem** that has persisted across multiple full relaunches ("Renaissance," "Saga") explicitly aimed at fixing it: players still report the AI "forgets" character names, plot threads, and relationships as sessions grow, a root cause traced directly to finite context windows (roughly 4,000 tokens on the free tier) forcing lossy compression that discards detail first ([AI Dungeon Help Center](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/why-does-the-ai-forget-or-mix-things-up); [Dungeons Deep blog](https://dungeonsdeep.ai/blog/why-ai-game-masters-forget-your-campaign-and-how-dungeonsdeepai-doesnt)). The two failures need different fixes: a moderation classifier can tolerate false positives if reversible, but a world-state mechanism cannot — it needs authoritative storage entirely outside the LLM's context, not filtering within it, and the fact that the memory complaint survived multiple architectural relaunches is itself evidence that prompt-engineering and bigger context windows alone are insufficient.

The academic function-calling literature quantifies exactly how much a hybrid architecture helps and exactly where it still falls short. The "Thirteen Hours" paper defines two enforced function classes — dice-roll functions that generate outcomes narration cannot alter, and state functions (create NPC, modify inventory) that own all game-state transitions — and shows the fully-enforced configuration scores **4.388/5 on consistency versus 3.691/5 for a no-function baseline**, with a human-rated "enforcement reliability" (correctly rejecting actions a player shouldn't be able to take) of **3.949/5** ([arXiv:2409.06949](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06949v1)). But the same paper's state-prediction unit test hit only **46.1% exact-match accuracy** for correctly predicting the resulting game state — meaning even a tool-enforced hybrid architecture leaves narration drifting out of sync with ground truth in more than half of cases, a documented state-desync rate that should set this project's expectations: function-calling is necessary but demonstrably not sufficient, and some explicit reconciliation or verification step is still needed on top of it. CALYPSO, by contrast, is the useful negative case: it concatenates D&D sourcebook text directly into the prompt with no function calling or validation at all, explicitly accepting hallucination risk and delegating the actual consistency check to human DM curation — a baseline this project cannot fall back to, since it has no human DM in the loop ([arXiv:2308.07540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07540)). A separate 2025 comparison of static prompt-engineered versus agentic, multi-step ("ReAct") GM architectures found the agentic version improved modularity, immersion, and curiosity over the simpler static baseline ([arXiv:2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19519)), and the landmark Generative Agents and Voyager papers independently converge on the same structural fix implied by AI Dungeon's own memory complaints: durable, externally-stored memory or skills retrieved by embedding similarity, rather than trusting a single context window — Voyager's persistent, retrievable skill library specifically yields 3.3x more unique items, 2.3x longer travel distances, and tech-tree milestones up to 15.3x faster than prior embodied LLM agents in Minecraft ([Voyager project page](https://voyager.minedojo.org/)). Three unrelated domains — social simulation, open-world agents, and tabletop GMing — landing on the identical fix is strong convergent evidence that externalized, queryable state is not a game-specific hack but a load-bearing architectural requirement.

This is where the classifier/judge-gating question this project cares about most gets its clearest — and most honest — answer. TypeSafe's Jev is confirmed as a real, currently marketed product: a non-generative "System One" model that never produces prose, instead returning a **calibrated confidence score across a closed, caller-defined set of up to 255 options**, explicitly positioned for "repeated, high-volume" decisions with "possible answers known in advance," running in roughly 70–500ms at about $0.001 per decision — architecturally exactly the bounded gate this project has been considering ([DataCamp: Jev](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/system-one-models-jev); [innfactory.ai](https://innfactory.ai/en/blog/jev-system-one-model-classifier-not-llm/)). But no source in either research pass documents a classifier/judge gate deployed specifically in a game-mastering context — this would be a **novel application of an established pattern**, not a replication of prior art, and that absence should be stated plainly rather than papered over. Two adjacent findings sharpen how to build it safely regardless. First, a live open-source judge-gating project documented a concrete methodological trap directly relevant here: in one experimental run the gate and the agent it judged were the same model family (GLM judging GLM), which the maintainers themselves flagged as invalidating the comparison, since "a like-for-like comparison... says nothing about whether an independent judge would differ" — the fix proposed was to force the judge model to always be a different family from the model it gates ([GitHub Issue #3, jasonli0226/jev-demo-triage](https://github.com/jasonli0226/jev-demo-triage/issues/3)). Second, the broader LLM-judge literature recommends judges return **calibrated probabilities with a three-valued gate rule** — confident failure (p ≥ 0.9), confident pass (p ≤ 0.5), or abstain-and-escalate when uncertain — rather than a forced binary verdict ([arXiv:2606.07834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07834); [arXiv:2608.07813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07813)). Combined, these two findings argue that a Jev-style gate for this project's rule-exception decisions should (a) never share a model family with the narrating LLM it's checking, and (b) have an explicit abstain state that escalates to a slower, more careful path rather than forcing a snap accept/reject on genuinely ambiguous creative actions — which is also the one point where this pass's research on "AI-adjudicated creative-action fairness" came back essentially empty: no academic paper or documented project treats this as a solved problem, confirming it as a genuinely open design frontier rather than one with an established playbook to copy.

## An updated, tradeoff-flagged primitive list for the project constitution

The table below builds directly on the prior report's list rather than replacing it: rows the prior report already established are carried forward and, where this round's sources sharpened or tested them, annotated in place; genuinely new rows are appended at the bottom, grouped by what they govern (build diversity, resource cost, live adjudication, or architecture).

| Primitive / pattern | Source(s) | What it buys you | Tradeoff / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed slot × fixed option-pool combinatorics** | HWFWM, Stormlight; *now also* Cradle (aspect × 4 technique archetypes), Allomancy (4×2×2 metal grid) | Enumerable, GM-memorizable build space with guaranteed closure | Requires upfront authoring discipline so every slot-filler feels distinct, not a numeric variant |
| **Hidden/semi-random variance under a visible choice layer** | The Land, HWFWM | Two characters with identical visible choices still diverge | Reduces agency; needs a GM-side RNG the player trusts |
| **Narrative-truth-gated tier unlocks** | Stormlight; *reinforced by* Cradle's "understanding, not grinding" and MoL's structured-vs-unstructured mastery curve | Power growth feels earned, gives the adjudicator an explicit checkpoint | Requires a genuinely qualitative judgment call unless converted into a numeric rubric |
| **Mutual macro/micro rank-gating; single-integer rank resolution** | HWFWM; *strongly reinforced by* Cradle's documented "10 Coppers = 1 Iron, 10 Irons = 1 Jade" rule of thumb | Cradle proves a single small-integer rank variable can resolve most contests with minimal bookkeeping | Coarse: no mechanical distinction between two same-rank builds unless the GM improvises one |
| **Race/origin as stat-quantity-vs-freedom trade-off** | The Land; *new variant from* Primal Hunter's cap-for-restriction structure (22 stats unrestricted vs. 27 restricted) | Build tension from character creation without bespoke per-class balancing; the restriction can be qualitative, not just numeric, so a flexible generalist is never strictly dominated | Every option must be calibrated to feel roughly equal in total value |
| **Emergent-by-history power acquisition** | Underworld (unbounded, cautionary); *now with two tamer real examples:* DCC's use-based auto-leveling and MoL's knowledge-persists-through-reset | Maximizes "no two characters converge" without an open, uncappable acquisition pool | Still needs a bounded trigger set (DCC: countable actions; MoL: a fixed spell-element library) or it degrades to Underworld's untracked-bestiary problem |
| **Named composite / matched-primitive bonus abilities** | Stormlight's Three Lashings; *now confirmed a second time by* Twinborn Compounding (matched-metal 10x multiplier) | Rewards *aligned* combinations with an emergent bonus, making combinatorics non-linear rather than merely multiplicative | Scales combinatorially with slot/option count; must stay bounded by the fixed-pool primitive above |
| **Opt-in reference layer separate from in-scene narration** | Stormlight's Ars Arcanum, HWFWM fandom tooling; *now formalized as an actual architecture:* RAG/structured memory + function calling | Keeps narration lean while full crunch stays queryable on demand | Only works if the store is authoritative and actually queried, not just present |
| **Explicit consolidation/retirement rule for aging content** | Missing everywhere in the prior report's four series; *DCC's specialization branching is the first confirmed real example of replace-not-add* | Prevents the universal additive-bloat failure mode | Removing/downgrading a player's existing content is player-hostile without a fair, telegraphed mechanism |
| **Limitation / cost / weakness as distinct levers** | Sanderson's Second Law; *now split further by Hemalurgy into two independent cost axes:* a quantitative transfer tax and a separate qualitative vulnerability-to-control tax | Lets a single "exception" mechanic charge two independent prices instead of one flat penalty | Requires discipline to keep the axes conceptually and mechanically separate |
| **Banded/tiered numeric display** | Inferred from Underworld/The Land's inflation; *reinforced by* Cradle's complete avoidance of exposed numbers | Keeps escalating power feeling meaningful instead of illegible | Loses granularity some players want |
| **Debt/loan resource economy** (store now = weaken now; tap later = strengthen later, net zero) | *New:* Feruchemy | A second, fully fleshed-out cost shape beyond "burn a consumable" — good for abilities that should feel like planning/investment rather than spending | Needs an explicit accounting ledger per stored attribute; easy to make either trivial or punishing if the storage/tap ratio isn't tuned |
| **Encode effect via location/method, not a lookup table** | *New:* Hemalurgy's spike-location + method-of-death encoding | Solves a combinatorial-explosion targeting problem (many primitives × many contexts) without an ever-growing table | Requires a clean, learnable encoding grammar be designed up front |
| **Decouple access breadth from starting competence** (tabletop-tested) | *New:* Mistborn Adventure Game's solution to "let people play the hybrid superclass without everyone else sucking" — weak baseline stats for the broad-access build, individualized strength/weakness spread for everyone | A real, tested fix for this project's "wants every essence" vs. "wants one essence, deeply" tension | Requires committing to a specific numeric handicap for broad-access builds, which some players will read as a penalty for choosing breadth |
| **Deterministic degrees-of-success branch** | *New:* PF2e (roll margin vs. DC → 4-way outcome, nat 20/1 shifts one step) | Zero-ambiguity resolution an AI can compute directly — the clearest fix for "did the improvised action work?" | Tight, exhaustively-audited math can make individual bonuses feel small |
| **Fixed action-economy pool shared by all builds** | *New:* PF2e's 3 actions + 1 reaction | Bounds tactical build diversity to *what* fills the actions, not *how many* a build gets — closes off action-stacking exploits | Some builds will feel mechanically homogeneous at the turn-structure level even while diverging in content |
| **Audited, context-independent option costs (or don't do shared point-buy)** | *New, as a warning:* GURPS's attribute/skill min-max exploit and disadvantage-gaming, both requiring GM policing the ruleset itself doesn't provide | Clarifies that "endless customizability" via a shared point pool is unsafe for an AI GM unless every cost is rigorously pre-audited | High authoring labor; any unaudited option becomes an exploit vector with no human GM to catch it live |
| **Narrow, fully pre-specified "move" verbs for flavor abilities** | *New:* PbtA playbooks/moves | Low-ambiguity fallback for narrative/flavor-driven abilities that shouldn't get full numeric modeling | Small total build space per playbook; doesn't scale to "endless" customization on its own |
| **Explicit Spotlight-balance heuristic at the scene-framing layer** | *New:* Alexandrian's Concept/Naturalistic/Spotlight taxonomy | Names the one balance type an AI GM cannot get "for free" from mechanical design and must engineer directly into pacing logic | No off-the-shelf algorithm exists yet; this is itself an open design task |
| **Diversify challenges instead of escalating against optimizers** | *New:* Alexandrian's "Optimization Arms Race" | A scenario-generation lever that reduces the need for perfect build-time balance | Requires the content-generation layer, not just the rules layer, to enforce multi-domain challenge |
| **Three-layer AI-GM architecture: function-calling + externalized state + calibrated judge gate** | *New, synthesized across* AI Dungeon's failures, the function-calling GM paper, Generative Agents, Voyager, and Jev | The master architecture pattern this round's research converges on; state-desync is measured at 46.1% exact-match even with functions, so treat this as necessary-but-not-sufficient and budget for a reconciliation step | Highest engineering complexity of any primitive on this list; the judge-gate layer specifically has no documented game-specific prior art, so it must be built and calibrated in-house |
| **Judge-gate independence + three-valued abstention** | *New:* the self-judging bias finding (GLM-judging-GLM) plus the calibrated-probability literature | Prevents a gate from rubber-stamping its own narrator's biases; abstain-and-escalate avoids forcing bad binary calls on ambiguous creative actions | Requires maintaining a genuinely separate model/provider for the gate, plus a defined escalation path for the abstain case |
| **Separate moderation/safety gating from world-state/consistency gating** | *New:* AI Dungeon's two distinct, differently-tolerant failure classes | Lets each mechanism be tuned to its own acceptable error rate (moderation can tolerate reversible false positives; state consistency cannot) | Requires building and maintaining two separate subsystems rather than one general-purpose "AI is being careful" layer |

## What this round changes about the prior conclusions

Nothing in this pass contradicts the prior report's central claims; instead it converts two of them from design principles into engineering specifications. "Small closed combinatorial primitives" now has a concrete alternative geometry (Allomancy's grid, Cradle's aspect-times-archetype cross) alongside Stormlight's cyclic pairs, and a genuinely new resource shape (Feruchemy's debt economy) to sit next to the consumable-burn default every prior series used. "Additive stacking without retirement" now has its first real counter-example — Dungeon Crawler Carl's specialization forks, which replace rather than merely extend — proving the retirement mechanism the prior report flagged as universally missing is achievable without being player-hostile, provided the branch point is designed in from the start rather than patched in later. The genuinely new axis this round adds, though, is architectural rather than mechanical: the prior report was entirely about what a character sheet should look like; this one establishes that the sheet's rules are only as trustworthy as the three-layer system enforcing them, that even the best-documented version of that system still desyncs from narration in roughly half of tested cases, and that the classifier-gate idea at the center of this project's open question is real, buildable, and — per every source checked — has never been tried in a game-mastering context before. That last point cuts both ways: there is no prior art to lean on, but there is also no prior failure to avoid repeating, and the calibration lessons from adjacent domains (independence from the narrator, abstain-and-escalate over forced binaries) are specific enough to build from immediately.
