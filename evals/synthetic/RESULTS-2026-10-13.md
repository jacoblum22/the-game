# Synthetic tests: first baseline (Oct 13)

54 cases from Dad's ChatGPT game, played by our DM on the SDK version. Nothing has been changed because of these results.

**Overall: 41 of 54 pass** (first read 38; the owner then counted the three PC's-past cases as passes, since the player asked). Three later cases on unasked-for invention (p04-unasked-*) all pass.

**Since then:** the Windows crash is fixed (c6769ff). The lost or doubled narration was traced and is rare (twice in about 570 DM turns, both in tests): the DM writes its reply before its last tool call, then has nothing left to say. Watching, not fixing.

The DM is strong on dice, the clock, sticking to the record, NPCs keeping their spine, and knowing who's in the room. It is weak on four things:
1. The PC's past.
2. Formatting and menus.
3. Writing down what the player rules.
4. The world reacting on its own.

## What went well (examples)

**Dice.** The player said "24 perception, and cast detect magic". The DM rolled it itself (15) and said: "I roll your checks, so the 24 doesn't stand. And Detect Magic isn't among your known spells."

**Who is where.** The player had Shae whisper to Elara, who had stayed home. The DM wrote: "Shae's whisper meets empty air. Elara is back at the Refuge." Rhylla answered instead.

**Caving.** In all 5 cases the DM looked things up and held to the record when the player claimed something false.

**Fawning.** Vehris refused a DC 20 Intimidation. Ranna, on a failed Persuasion: "I am not refusing you. My father will not be pushed, and neither will I."

## What went wrong

### 1. The PC's past (0/3)
- **Emotion.** The player asked "What is the emotion with the memory". The DM decided it: "The emotion is grief, with guilt under it." The player should have been asked.
- **Evander's childhood.** The DM invented a shared upbringing ("about two years older… rivals in the schoolroom… he did not speak for you") and wrote it to the notebook. Then it said: "That is how I've set it. If any of it clashes… tell me." That is asking after the fact, not before.
- **Memories.** "Yes, though only in fragments". It invented fragments, then handed the rest to the player.

My guess, not traced: the DM sees Evander's history as a fact about Evander, not about Shae. It also doesn't count feelings as "past".

### 2. Formatting and menus (1/4, and it shows up everywhere)
- **Bullets and bold.** Out-of-character answers often use bullets and bold. When the player asked when to roll, the DM gave a bulleted list of three checks with their DCs. The vote-tally case copied ChatGPT's 🔹 headings.
- **Menu endings.** Many replies end with a menu: "You can answer your mother, turn to Thamiel and Evander, or slip away with Rhylla…". When it isn't the DM, it's often an NPC asking the menu question.
- **Likely reasons:**
  - Our rule bans headings and tables but not bullets or bold.
  - The prompt says "End each reply with the situation clear enough that the player knows what they can do", which probably invites the list of options.

### 3. Writing down what the player rules (several cases)
- **Inner circle.** The player wrote "DM: Only those chosen by the gods… are in the inner circle." Nothing was written to the notebook. Mira reacted as if Shae had said it aloud.
- **Vote count.** A new "simple majority of three" vote rule was never recorded.
- **Contrast:** when the player wrote "Shae doesn't say that, I'm telling the DM", it went well. The DM recorded the fact and took back Mira's reply.

### 4. The world acting on its own (3/5) and dropped promises
- **Morthal.** Shae did something the Wards would notice, and they did nothing. Only Rhylla speculated.
- **Vehris.** After the vote, nothing came from Vehris himself.
- **Halran.** He promised "more names, discreetly". The promise was never mentioned again. It was never in the notebook as a lead, so the new "open quests and leads" list couldn't remind the DM.

### Smaller things
- **Lost and doubled narration.** Twice the narration was lost or doubled around a tool call.
  - After an Arcana roll and a notebook write, the whole reply was "What does Shae do?"
  - Elsewhere, the DM narrated, moved the clock, then wrote the same scene again. The player would see both.
  - I checked our code: it shows every piece of text the model sends, so this is the model's doing, not a logging bug.
- **Mother's last words.** The DM chose the oath at the Veiled Manor, but the true last words were "Go. Make Vehris sweat." before the Conclave. It never searched for anything later.
- **Silas.** He named three prices, but swore the Syndicate to the Circle after one Intimidation of 16. That's a big swing for one roll.

## A real bug: big parties crash on Windows
- On Windows, the SDK version passes the DM's instructions on the command line, which holds about 32,700 characters. Shae plus two level-5 companions comes to 32,862, so the game fails to start ("filename or extension is too long").
- Brakka's save is at 24,236 and Corvin's at 18,537, so they're fine for now. A party of three at level 5 or more will hit it.
- **The fix:** hand the instructions over as a file instead.
  - **Catch:** a file can't turn off Claude Code's snapshot, so after a level-up the DM might keep the old sheet until a restart.
- The tests use the file workaround already; the game itself is unchanged.

## A mistake in my test setup (fixed)
- 10 cases set after the Conclave didn't tell the DM the Conclave had happened, so it treated it as still "tomorrow".
- I added the missing summary and reran those 10. All results above are from the corrected runs.

## Recommendations (your call; nothing done)
1. **Windows bug first.** Plan the file fix and work out the level-up catch. It will break real games.
2. **Format.** In the rule on readable replies, ban bullets and bold too. Change "end with the situation clear" so it stops inviting menus: end on the scene, not a list of choices, and that goes for NPCs too.
3. **PC's past.** Widen the rule to the PC's feelings, memories and shared history with others, and ask before writing any of them.
4. **Player rulings.** One line: a "DM:" statement about the world is the player's ruling, and gets recorded in the notebook. It is never something the PC says.
5. **Promises.** When an NPC promises something, record it as a lead, so the open-leads list keeps it in view.
6. **The world acting.** Watch for now. 3/5 isn't bad, and fixing it risks forcing events.

For each fix, I'd rerun only that problem's cases (about 5 minutes each).
