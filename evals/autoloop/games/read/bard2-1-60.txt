Marrowford sits where the slow brown Marrow River bends under a stone bridge, a town of grain barges, tallow-chandlers and market stalls that never quite close. The lands around it are old and quarrelsome. Minor noble houses hold the river-farms, trade in favours, and pay well for stories that make their lineages look older than they are.

Wren Ashgrove is nineteen, half-elf, a bard with a quick grin and a sharper tongue. She was raised by Corvin Tallowmere, who taught her to spin tales and sell them. A few weeks ago she slipped away from his caravan at a river crossing in the night. She took his ledger of forged tales, its pages now hidden in the binding of her road-psalter, a few of his best forgeries, and a head start. She also carries a brass lantern-shaped reliquary of the Lantern-Bearer, the gentle road-god of travelers and tale-tellers, with a curl of old parchment inside that may be a true blessing or just another forgery.

Dusk settles over Marrowford's square. Lamps are being lit along the stalls, and the smell of river fish and woodsmoke hangs in the air. At the square's far edge, half in shadow, stands a wagon painted with a shrine-lantern sigil you know too well. Corvin's. There's no sign of the man himself.

A weathered driver lounges on the box, picking his teeth. Before him a young page in a minor noble's livery, pale and sweating, is pressing a sealed letter into his hand and glancing over his shoulder. The wax seal is one you've seen in the ledger. On the tailboard behind them sits a locked, iron-bound strongbox, begging to be poked at.

The crowd is thin enough that you could slip closer unseen, march up with a grin, or wait and watch. What does Wren do?
