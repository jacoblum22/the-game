DM: "Everyone up two steps, in case I'm wrong," you say. Kit takes Brann by the elbow and they climb, and Kit's crossbow stays on the stair. You seat the reliquary's foot in the lantern plate, it fits with a click, and you turn it once to the left, for the road.

Deep in the iron something shifts, slow and heavy, like a key in a very old lock. The ring of Conjuration in the flagstones stays quiet and dark. The door breathes out a smell of cold stone and dry paper, swings inward on a hinge that makes no sound, and does not flood the cellar.

Beyond it is a small stone cell, dry as a bone, no bigger than a cupboard. On a shelf cut into the wall is a lead-lined tube, a little brass lantern the size of a thumb, and a folded letter. The tube has a faint ward on it that your Detect Magic, still running, reads as Abjuration. Nothing else is in the room, and nothing moves.

The letter is in your mother's tilted hand:

*My wren, if the door opened to the road, then you are who I hoped, and you are kind enough not to have drowned us both. In the tube is a leaf I cut from the Thornhallow register, which an old chaplain gave me because he knew a man would one day come to burn it or to use it. One side is a baptism, Aldous Verrel the younger, a servant's child, his father consenting. The other side is the child's burial, three years on. The boy is dead, and he has been dead thirty years. A man who comes to you wearing that name wears a dead child's grave. He is not what he says. He first came to me as a patron of our shrines, and then asked for the lamp. Do not let him make you his. Burn the leaf if you must, keep it if you can, and whatever you choose, choose it as yourself. Light the road, Wren. —E.*

You read the leaf by Brann's candle: the baptism on one side, and the burial on the other, in the same cramped chaplain's hand. It matches the register copy you carry, and the note about the missing page in Brother Osric's book. It proves the Grey Cloak's story false. It also proves nothing about who he really is.

You take the leaf, the letter, and the little brass lantern (Elowen's last gift is a thumb-sized lamp with a wick the size of a whisker). You take the foot back out of the plate and shut the iron door. Ten minutes, then twenty, pass in the cellar as you read and think. It is nearly half past six in the morning, and the lamp upstairs is dark. The tide is still rising. The guard boat waits on the water, and Dunn is yawning on the landing.

You've got the proof. You've got your mother's words. The reliquary's foot goes back in the lamp if you want the light burning, or stays with you if you want to carry the key. And the Grey Cloak will come for the leaf, if he's learned it's yours. What does Wren do?
