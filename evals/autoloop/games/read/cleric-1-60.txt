The mountain roads of the hill country are old dwarf-cut routes, and they carry more grief than trade these days. Rain loosens the slopes, and the small shrines along them are the only mercy for many miles.

Brunhilde Stonehearth, called Brun, grew up in one of those shrines. Father Dorrin Ashbeard found her as a half-starved child in a collapsed mine shaft and raised her in the Morninglord's light. His prayer book now rides in her pack, and she has come down from the hills to carry his blessing to those who need it.

Late afternoon, cold rain hammering the slate roof of the crossroads waystation. You have barely shaken the water from your braid when a roar of sliding stone tears across the pass. Beyond the inn's yard, a fresh rockslide has swallowed part of the road. Under it, muffled tapping and a thin voice calling for help: a wagon's worth of travelers, a wounded merchant, a child, a mule-driver.

At the edge of the rubble three strangers are already shouting. A nervous caravan guard wants to dig now. A sullen hunter, arms folded, says the slope above is still groaning and will bury them all.
