The night passes in cold and murmuring. Small hands are busy at the back of the Row, and you hear the faint scrape of iron on iron. At the dawn bell, a little before six, Hask rises from his stool and begins the count, walking the rows with a tally-board and touching each blanket with the butt of a short rod. Ninna coughs. A few of the others pick it up, and a small boy moans your name in a convincing wail.

But your act doesn't hold. Your breathing is too even and your color too good for a man at death's door, and Hask's flat eyes pause on you for a long moment. He stops three paces short of the end of your chain, well outside your reach, and doesn't come closer. "Eight hours, and the fever's gone," he says, dry as a ledger. "Interesting." He nudges a bowl of gruel along the floor toward you with the rod. "Eat. The Foreman will see you at noon." He turns his head. "Door. Two of you inside, spears out. No one stands near that one."

Two spear-bearing guards come in and plant themselves between you and the rest of the Row. Hask goes back to his stool. The ring of keys is in his lap again, twelve feet away. The chain is the length of your arm and a little over.

It is nearly six in the morning on the nineteenth. At noon you are summoned to the Foreman, and tomorrow is the move. What does Korvash do?
