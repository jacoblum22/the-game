You whisper it to Ninna: the goat that sings is your sister, the little ones are to stay quiet until her song, the smallest should try the chute bolts if they can reach them, and at the count they should cough and fuss over you. She passes it down the rows in a chain of nods. Then you let yourself go slack and grey, and the night does what the Foreman's bandages could not.

Nobody comes. No one drags you out, and no one asks questions. The lamp burns down, the draft sighs from the back, and the children's breathing slows. By some mercy you sleep, truly, and your body mends itself the way a dragonborn's does. When the dawn bell tolls at the cliff top you are whole: 14 of 14 hit points, your rage and your breath both banked in your chest. Nobody knows it but you.

It is a few minutes before six. Hask rises from his stool, keys at his belt, and begins the dawn count down the rows with a slate. Behind him a small boy coughs and moans, and a girl wails that the big one is dying. Ninna's voice is the loudest of all. Hask scowls, pauses, and comes down the aisle to you. He looks at your grey face and your shallow breath. He believes it. Setting down his slate, he kneels by the chained prisoner the Foreman wants alive and reaches for your throat to feel for a pulse. The key ring swings at his belt, a hand's breadth from your fingers.

The four wall-guards are outside the barred door, which is shut. Hask has not noticed that you are awake, and he's within your reach.
