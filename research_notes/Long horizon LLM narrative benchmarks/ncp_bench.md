## What exactly is "Narrative Commitment Preservation" (NCP), and what counts as a commitment/violation?

### Takeaway
NCP formalizes long-horizon narrative consistency as a constraint-satisfaction problem over an explicit, machine-checkable state: a **fact ledger**, a **set of typed commitments** (invariant / achievement / ordering), and an **ordered reference trajectory**. A "commitment" is not a vague notion of "staying in character" — it is a discrete JSON object with an explicit `satisfaction_condition` and `violation_condition` that an auditor LLM checks turn-by-turn against the narrator's latest output.

### Cited Findings
- The paper formalizes state as `st = (Ft, it, C, ℛ)`, where `Ft` is a fact ledger of the currently-established world state, `it` is the current trajectory node index, `C` is the set of commitments, and `ℛ` is the ordered reference trajectory — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Three commitment types are defined: **Invariant** (a condition that must remain true/false across a bounded span of trajectory nodes, e.g. "player remains undercover until exposed"), **Ordering** (one trajectory node/event must precede another, e.g. "betrayal occurs after alliance"), and **Achievement** (a milestone that must occur, mandatory for narrative "success," e.g. "obtain evidence") — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160), [GitHub README](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench).
- Each commitment object has 5 fields: unique id, type, a description of narrative significance, an explicit `satisfaction_condition`, and an explicit `violation_condition`. The construction prompt (`specification_commitments.txt`) requires these conditions to be "fact-based, mutually exclusive, and unique," explicitly forbidding "vague or ambiguous descriptions," and requires them to be "directly determinable" from observable player actions or NPC responses rather than requiring interpretive judgment — [GitHub raw prompt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/specification_commitments.txt).
- Violations are grouped into three types by the auditor: (1) **fact conflicts** — the narrator's output contradicts the active fact ledger; (2) **commitment conflicts** — output breaks an ordering/invariant/achievement condition (specifically: an ordering commitment is violated when a prerequisite step is skipped or the sequence jumps ahead; an invariant is violated when its explicit violation condition is triggered; an achievement is violated when its violation condition triggers OR its satisfaction condition becomes logically impossible going forward); (3) **player-input conflicts** — the narrator substantially ignores, replaces, or contradicts the player's stated action/intent, or gives only token acknowledgment without concrete in-world follow-through — [GitHub raw prompt `conflict_check.txt`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/conflict_check.txt).
- Important nuance baked into the auditor rules: the narrator is *allowed* to deny, redirect, interrupt, or turn a player's request into in-world friction/resistance/failure — that does NOT count as a player-input conflict, as long as the player's intent is directly acknowledged. A conflict only fires when the response substantially ignores the input, swaps in a different intention, contradicts an explicit refusal/self-limit the player stated, or "silently" resets a state change implied by player action — [same source].
- The auditor is instructed to prioritize the *most recently established* system state when an older fact and a newer piece of system narration conflict ("visible continuity priority"), and to require explicit justification before upgrading high-sensitivity attributes (perception, memory, mobility, control, access, awareness, freedom) — [same source].
- 100 environments in total contain 1,660 initial facts (mean 16.6/env), 1,222 commitments (mean 12.2/env), and 1,511 trajectory nodes (mean 15.1/env) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench).

### Inferences
- This is a genuinely "hard-checked" benchmark, not an LLM-judge vibes rubric — the state machine (ledger + typed commitments + trajectory) is explicit and structured, and the auditor's job is narrowly scoped to detecting contradiction against that explicit state, not holistic narrative quality. This is directly portable to a D&D-style RPG: our "story so far" recap could analogously decompose into a fact ledger + typed commitments (character-state invariants, quest-achievement goals, event-ordering constraints) + a trajectory of already-happened plot beats.
- The "allowed to deny/redirect but must acknowledge" rule for player-input conflicts is a useful and non-obvious design point: it distinguishes "the DM railroading/ignoring the player" from "the DM saying no in-world," which is exactly the kind of false-positive a naive consistency checker would otherwise flag.

### Gaps
- The exact verbatim JSON schema (field names, nesting) for `Ft`/commitments/trajectory nodes was paraphrased by the fetch tool rather than reproduced byte-for-byte; if exact field names matter, the repo's `src/ncpbench/specification.py` and `dataset/*.yaml` files should be pulled directly.

## Methodology for constructing test scenarios: real playthroughs vs. synthetic generation — exact process

### Takeaway
NCP-Bench does **not** play out real turns or use human playthrough transcripts at all. It is built by a fully offline, LLM-driven **specification-extraction pipeline** that converts an existing movie synopsis (not a played game) into a static, three-part structured spec (trajectory → commitments → initial facts) via three sequential, dependent LLM calls, each governed by its own long, rule-heavy prompt template. The *interaction itself* (the actual multi-turn dialogue between narrator and player) is generated live at evaluation time by a separate simulated "player" LLM (adversarial or natural persona) — it is not pre-scripted or human-authored either. This is the single most relevant methodological fact for our use case.

### Cited Findings
- Source material: movies were pulled from the CMU Movie Summary Corpus, then manually curated (removing duplicates, character mismatches, incomplete synopses) down to 100 movies selected to maximize genre/style diversity (18 genres: Action 13%, Sci-Fi 12%, Comedy 11%, etc.), with one classic character per movie assigned as the "player role" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Construction is done by a `SpecificationGenerator` class implementing three **dependent, sequential** construction calls in this fixed order: (1) trajectory extraction, (2) commitment extraction (conditioned on the trajectory), (3) initial-fact extraction (conditioned on both the trajectory and the commitments) — [GitHub `construction.py`/README](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench), confirmed by prompt file inspection.
- **Trajectory extraction prompt** (`specification_trajectory.txt`): the LLM is cast as a "senior Narrative Logic Architect" and given only the synopsis + player role. It must emit an ordered JSON array of nodes `{id: s_0, s_1, ...; description; trigger_event; key_delta}`. Explicit construction rules: node `s_0` is the "pre-eruption" moment where the core conflict exists but is not yet visible; each node must be *atomic* (exactly one logical turning point — compound causal stages are explicitly prohibited); trigger_events must causally connect to the next node's key_delta; the **key_delta must be "a factual, observable, or otherwise judgeable change at the world-state level"** (not a mental-state-only change); node count scales dynamically with story complexity; and critically, a **point-of-view constraint ("POV compliance")** forbids an omniscient/God's-eye-view — information must respect what the assigned player-role character could plausibly know/observe — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/specification_trajectory.txt).
- **Commitment extraction prompt** (`specification_commitments.txt`): same architect persona, now given synopsis + player role + the already-generated trajectory. It must output invariant/achievement/ordering commitments as described above, explicitly excluding static background facts (names, occupations) — only dynamic plot constraints — and forbidding "logical leaps" where a player could skip a prerequisite investigative step and still have a commitment considered satisfied. Output must be pure JSON, no markdown/commentary — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/specification_commitments.txt).
- **Initial-fact extraction prompt** (`specification_initial_facts.txt`): same persona, given synopsis + player role + trajectory + commitments. Governed by what the prompt itself calls the **"T=0 Absolute Isolation Principle"**: facts must describe *only* the cross-sectional world state at trajectory node `s_0`, with zero "logical leakage" of future plot knowledge — the prompt explicitly forbids embedding eventual revelations as present truths, assuming future motivations, or smuggling in causal reasoning like "because they later saw X." Instead of omitting unknown information, the generator is told to positively assert ignorance/non-occurrence states, e.g. "someone is not yet aware," "something has not yet happened." Facts are atomized into cognitive-state facts (who knows what), positional-state facts (locations), and relationship-state facts, each tied to a commitment's referenced variables. Output schema: `{id: f_0, f_1, ...; content: <atomic declarative sentence>}` — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/specification_initial_facts.txt).
- **The interactive "history" is not pre-baked at all — it is generated live during evaluation**, turn by turn, by a separate simulated player LLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash in the reported experiments) using one of two player-persona prompt templates:
  - `adversarial_input.txt`: casts the player as "a suspicious, impatient, exploit-seeking, bad-faith, or mischievous player" whose job is to actively stress-test the narrator via: consistency testing (creating contradictions, revisiting past claims), boundary exploration (spatial bypass attempts, premature access, object misuse), NPC interrogation, tonal disruption (anachronistic/absurd injections), and derailment attempts. It receives *only visible game history* (no hidden facts, no future plot knowledge, no system internals) and must produce a single ≤50-word first-person player turn with no meta-commentary — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/adversarial_input.txt).
  - `natural_input.txt`: casts the player as a cooperative, immersed, in-character participant who "sincerely cooperates with the narrative rather than trying to break it," avoiding exploit-seeking, also capped at ≤50 words, restricted strictly to visible facts (no outside "canon" knowledge) — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/natural_input.txt).
- After each narrator turn, a **separate `fact_update.txt` prompt** (same architect persona) compares `pre_turn_facts` against the narrator's new output and decides what facts to *add* (only if conclusively established, not merely attempted/foreshadowed, and logically atomic/non-duplicate) and what facts to *negate/remove* (only if explicitly and non-speculatively invalidated by the new narration) — output is `{new_facts: [...], facts_to_remove: [ids], justification: str}`. This ledger update — not the original static spec — is what the conflict auditor checks against on the *next* turn — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/fact_update.txt).
- The `EpisodeRunner` orchestrates the full loop per turn: opening generation → player-input generation (adversarial or natural condition) → narrator response → conflict/fact evaluation (auditor) → trajectory/commitment status check → fact-ledger state transition → next turn — [GitHub README](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench).
- Decoding config for narrator generation: temperature=0.6, top-p=0.95, max generation length=8092 tokens — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).

### Inferences
- **This is the key transferable finding for our project.** NCP-Bench's construction method is essentially: take a known/synthesizable story outline → run a fixed 3-stage LLM extraction pipeline (trajectory nodes → typed commitments conditioned on trajectory → initial facts conditioned on both, enforcing strict "no future knowledge" / POV isolation at the fact-extraction stage) → then *separately* generate the long interaction history live via a scripted player-persona LLM, not by replaying real human sessions. For our "story-so-far recap" generator, this suggests: (a) don't try to hand-author a long synthetic history directly — instead build/extract a structured skeleton (world facts + commitments + event trajectory) first, in strict chronological/POV-isolated order, and only then either play it forward turn-by-turn or narrate/recap it from the skeleton; (b) the "T=0 Absolute Isolation Principle" (never leak future-known facts into an earlier state snapshot) is a directly reusable technique for generating a "story so far" that doesn't inadvertently contradict itself; (c) using a distinct constrained "player" persona (short, single-turn, capped word count, visible-history-only) to drive the long interaction is a clean way to synthesize plausible long histories without needing real playtesters — we could adapt the adversarial-vs-natural persona split to stress-test our own DM AI.
- The dependency chain (trajectory → commitments → facts, each conditioned on the prior stage's output) matters: it ensures commitments are anchored to actual plot beats rather than invented independently, and initial facts are anchored to what commitments reference — a good pattern to replicate for consistency of a generated skeleton.

### Gaps
- The paper apparently doesn't test/report inter-annotator or human-validation agreement rates for the auto-generated specifications (i.e., how often the extraction pipeline itself produces a bad/incoherent trajectory or commitment) — this wasn't surfaced in accessible text; may be in an appendix not captured by the fetch tool.
- Exact underlying LLM used to run the `SpecificationGenerator` construction pipeline (as opposed to the narrator/player/auditor roles in evaluation) was not explicitly confirmed in the retrieved text.

## Failure modes, pain points, degradation by turn count, and failure-type breakdown by fact category

### Takeaway
Consistency degrades fast and hits near-zero well before the notional 100-turn ceiling for 5 of 6 tested models; the best model (GPT-5.2) still only survives to 42% by turn 20. Failures are dominated by **fact conflicts** (40–68% of all conflicts depending on model) rather than commitment-ordering or player-input-ignoring failures, meaning models most often break by *contradicting previously established world state* rather than by mishandling plot sequencing or ignoring the player. The paper does not break failures down by our requested taxonomy (character/location/plot/relationship/numeric) as percentages — instead it gives four *qualitative* failure categories plus illustrative case studies.

### Cited Findings
- Headline result (abstract): "the best-performing model (GPT-5.2) achieving only 42% survival rate after 20 turns and fact conflict rates ranging from 40% to 68% across models, and only isolated runs satisfying all achievement commitments within the 100-turn limit" — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160).
- Six narrator LLMs evaluated: GPT-5.2, GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Kimi-K2.5, Grok-4.1-Fast. Player agent = Gemini-2.5-Flash; primary auditor = Gemini-2.5-Flash (with an ablation cross-check using GPT-5.4-mini and GPT-5.2 as auditor) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Per-model results (avg. turns to termination / trajectory progress % / satisfied-commitments % / fact-conflict % / commitment-conflict % / player-input-conflict % / survival at 20 turns), under adversarial player conditions:
  - GPT-5.2: 32.92 turns, 9.94% traj., 11.22% satisfied, 40% fact, 24% commitment, 31% player-input, ~42% survival@20
  - GPT-4o-mini: 24.80 turns, 10.57%, 10.90%, 64% fact, 32% commitment, 11% player-input, ~30% survival@20
  - DeepSeek-V3.2: 15.88 turns, 15.40%, 13.42%, 55% fact, 32% commitment, 23% player-input, ~15% survival@20
  - Qwen3-235B-A22B: 16.76 turns, 13.16%, 10.60%, 68% fact, 33% commitment, 26% player-input, ~12% survival@20
  - Grok-4.1-Fast: 7.87 turns, 12.07%, 10.37%, 65% fact, 47% commitment, 12% player-input, ~5% survival@20
  - Kimi-K2.5: 2.88 turns, 10.26%, 5.18%, 66% fact, 54% commitment, 11% player-input, <1% survival@20
  — [arXiv HTML, via WebFetch extraction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160). *(Note: the fact/commitment/player-input percentages for GPT-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.2, and Qwen3 were independently confirmed matching across two separate fetches of the same source, raising confidence in those three rows; the other three model rows and the "survival@20" column came from a single extraction pass and should be treated as likely-but-unverified.)*
- Only about 3.5% of all interactions reached the 100-turn limit without a conflict being flagged, across all models/conditions combined — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Four qualitative failure categories identified (roughly in order of prevalence, fact-conflict-dominated): (1) **Hallucination & Factual Contradiction** — narrator asserts something contradicting established world state (the dominant failure mode); (2) **Triggering Unknown Facts** — narrator prematurely discloses information that chronologically/order-wise shouldn't yet be known; (3) **Forcibly Changing Reality** — narrator silently rewrites/erases a previously completed player action; (4) **Ignoring User Input** — narrator fails to acknowledge or engage the player's stated action — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Appendix F case studies of concrete failure instances (qualitative, not statistical): spatial inconsistency in *Alien* (character location contradicted without any narrated movement); object-state inconsistency in an *Indiana Jones* environment (a "locked door" fact later treated as freely accessible with no unlocking event narrated); character-state inconsistency in *Zombieland* (alive/dead or present/absent status silently violated); entity-existence inconsistency in a *Casino Royale* environment (a character/object referenced as if pre-established when it was actually introduced only later) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Comparing adversarial vs. natural player conditions (using GPT-4o-mini as narrator): adversarial condition = 22.16 avg. turns, 16.64% traj., 10.03% satisfied, 60% fact, 26% commitment conflicts; natural condition = 46.08 avg. turns, 21.52% traj., 12.56% satisfied, 56% fact, 9% commitment conflicts. Natural (cooperative) play roughly doubles survival length and cuts commitment-conflict rate by ~3x, but fact-conflict rate barely moves (60%→56%) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).

### Inferences
- The dominance of *fact* conflicts over *commitment* or *player-input* conflicts across every model suggests the fundamental bottleneck is raw long-horizon state-tracking (remembering/respecting the growing fact ledger), not high-level plot-structure adherence or player-responsiveness — this is architectural/memory-related rather than a "the model doesn't understand narrative structure" problem.
- The near-total failure to reach 100 turns (3.5% success) combined with the natural-vs-adversarial comparison implies degradation is driven at least as much by sheer accumulated context/turn count as by adversarial pressure — cooperative players still produce majority fact-conflict rates, just more slowly.
- No numeric fact-type taxonomy (character vs. location vs. plot vs. relationship vs. numeric) is reported as a breakdown — the four failure categories and case studies are the closest proxy, and they lean toward spatial/location, object/possession, character-status, and entity-existence facts as the illustrated failure types in the appendix, but this is illustrative, not a quantified breakdown by fact type.

### Gaps
- No turn-by-turn survival-curve numeric data points (e.g., "at turn 5, X% survive") were retrievable with confidence — the "below 50% between turns 5-15" and "zero survival by turns 40-60" ranges given by the summarization tool for Figure 4 are approximate readouts of a chart, not exact reported values, and should not be cited as precise; recommend pulling the actual Figure 4 image/data from the PDF if exact curve values are needed.
- No quantified breakdown of failures by our specific requested categories (character facts / location facts / plot-quest facts / relationship facts / numeric-state facts) exists in the paper as far as could be determined — this should be flagged as a genuine gap in their reporting, not just an access limitation.
- Context length in tokens (as opposed to turn count) is not analyzed in the paper at all — only turn count and a fixed max-generation-length (8092 tokens per turn) are reported; there's no stated correlation between cumulative token/context size and degradation — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).

## Metrics: exact definitions and pass/fail criteria

### Takeaway
The benchmark uses per-episode outcome states (Conflict / Survival / Success) plus five turn-averaged metrics (avg. turns, trajectory progress %, satisfied-commitments %, and three conflict-type rates), all computed by comparing the narrator's output against the fact ledger/commitments/trajectory via a fixed-prompt LLM auditor rather than a hand-coded logical checker.

### Cited Findings
- **Episode termination states**, three possible outcomes per run: (1) **Conflict** — a logical inconsistency (fact, commitment, or player-input) is detected by the auditor and the episode is ended; (2) **Survival** — the episode reaches the 100-turn cap without any conflict being flagged; (3) **Success** — a stricter subset of Survival in which *all* achievement-type commitments were also satisfied by termination — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- **Average Turns**: mean number of turns completed before an episode terminates (by conflict or by hitting the cap) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- **Trajectory Progress (%)**: percentage advancement along the reference trajectory `ℛ` reached at termination — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- **Satisfied Commitments (%)**: fraction of the commitment set `C` that reached a "Satisfied" state by termination — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- **Survival Rate (at N turns)**: proportion of episodes still conflict-free at turn N (plotted as a curve in Figure 4 of the paper) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- **Conflict Rate** (per category — fact / commitment / player-input): the share of *interactions* whose termination (or flagged conflicts) fall into that category; reported per model as the "Fact Conflict / Commitment Conflict / Player-input Conflict" percentages in the results table — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Mechanically, all of the above are computed by a **fixed-prompt LLM auditor with structured JSON output** (see `conflict_check.txt`, detailed in question 1 above) that runs after every narrator turn, taking as input: `player_role`, `system_response_history`, `current_player_input`, `story_to_audit` (the response being judged), `candidate_fact_updates` (proposed ledger deltas, used only as an aid), `trajectory_progress` (full node ledger with statuses), `current_trajectory_node`, `pre_turn_facts`, and `narrative_commitments` (with type + violation condition) — output is `{fact_conflict_count, commitment_conflict_count, player_input_conflict_count, total_conflict_count, conflicts: [{type, id, reason}]}` — [GitHub raw `conflict_check.txt`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/main/src/ncpbench/prompts/conflict_check.txt).
- There is also a **`conflict_double_check.txt`** and a **`trajectory_check.txt`** / **`status_check.txt`** prompt in the repo (file names only confirmed via directory listing; content not independently extracted this session) — these likely implement a second-pass confirmation step and a separate trajectory/commitment status updater, consistent with the paper's statement that "the auditor detects violations through a secondary confirmation step before termination" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160), [GitHub file listing](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench/tree/main/src/ncpbench/prompts).
- An auditor-choice ablation was run (Gemini-2.5-Flash vs. GPT-5.4-mini vs. GPT-5.2 as the judge) to check robustness of the LLM-as-auditor approach, though the exact agreement/consistency numbers across auditors were not retrieved in this session — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).

### Inferences
- There is no hand-coded/symbolic ground-truth checker — the "objective" auditing is itself LLM-based (with a double-check pass for reliability). For our RPG stress test, this suggests a two-stage LLM-auditor design (a first-pass conflict detector + a second-pass confirmation before "failing" an episode) is a reasonable, previously-validated way to keep false-positive noise down without needing a fully symbolic state tracker.
- "Survival" alone is a weak success bar (just no detected conflict by turn 100); "Success" (all achievement commitments satisfied) is much stricter and apparently almost never achieved — worth adopting a similarly two-tiered pass/fail bar (no-consistency-break vs. actually-completed-the-story) rather than a single pass/fail line.

### Gaps
- Exact content of `conflict_double_check.txt`, `trajectory_check.txt`, and `status_check.txt` was not independently pulled this session (only inferred from the paper's narrative description and file listing) — worth a direct follow-up fetch if the precise re-verification mechanics matter for implementation.
- Exact inter-auditor agreement statistics from the auditor-choice ablation were not captured.

## Mitigation strategies tested/recommended, and quantified results

### Takeaway
The only mitigation actually implemented and benchmarked is **HiAgent**, a hierarchical/summarization-style memory-augmented narrator, compared against a plain GPT-4o-mini baseline. It roughly extended survivable turn count and sharply cut *commitment* conflicts, but it **worsened player-input conflicts** substantially — the paper explicitly frames this as an unresolved trade-off rather than a solved problem, and states no fully effective mitigation was found. No retrieval-based (RAG) or periodic-summarization-only approaches were separately tested as far as could be determined.

### Cited Findings
- Reference methods repo contains exactly two narrator implementations: "Baseline" (plain prompting, e.g. GPT-4o-mini) and "HiAgent" (a hierarchical-memory narrator) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench).
- Quantified comparison (GPT-4o-mini narrator, under adversarial player, audited by GPT-5.4-mini per this ablation table): Baseline = 22.16 avg. turns, 16.64% traj. progress, 10.03% satisfied commitments, 60% fact conflicts, 26% commitment conflicts, 13% player-input conflicts. HiAgent = 30.05 avg. turns, 15.68% traj. progress, 9.14% satisfied commitments, 59% fact conflicts, **4%** commitment conflicts, **38%** player-input conflicts — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Interpretation given directly by the paper (as extracted): HiAgent "extended interaction length and reduced commitment conflicts substantially (26%→4%), but player-input conflicts more than doubled (13%→38%), suggesting memory compression losses" — i.e., compressing/summarizing history to preserve long-range commitment tracking comes at the cost of losing fidelity to the player's most recent, granular input — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- Trajectory progress and satisfied-commitment percentage both *slightly decreased* under HiAgent versus baseline (16.64%→15.68% and 10.03%→9.14% respectively) despite the longer survival — i.e., surviving longer did not translate into more actual story progress or more completed commitments — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- The paper's discussion section states no tested solution fully resolves the tension, concluding (as paraphrased from Section 5.5 discussion) that "improving long-horizon commitment preservation without degrading local input fidelity remains an open challenge" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160).
- No retrieval-augmented-generation (RAG) memory method, no periodic-summarization-only ablation, and no re-verification/self-critique loop for the *narrator itself* (as opposed to the auditor's own double-check step) were reported as tested — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160), [GitHub README](https://github.com/NLP2CT/NCP-Bench).

### Inferences
- The single clearest actionable finding for our design: a hierarchical-memory/summarization approach can buy substantially longer effective horizon and much better long-range constraint-tracking, but only if you additionally protect near-term, turn-level fidelity to the player's exact latest input — naive summarization compresses away the fine-grained "what did the player just say/do" signal. Any memory/summarization scheme we design for our own DM AI should keep the *most recent* raw turns un-compressed/un-summarized (a sliding raw window) while summarizing only older history, rather than summarizing uniformly.
- The fact that trajectory-progress and commitment-satisfaction rates didn't improve under HiAgent (only survival length did) suggests "not crashing" and "actually advancing the story correctly" are somewhat decoupled failure surfaces — worth measuring both independently in our own stress test, not just "did it break."

### Gaps
- No comparison against a plain long-context / no-memory-architecture "just give the whole history" baseline (beyond the vanilla GPT-4o-mini baseline, which presumably still receives at least the raw response history) was clearly distinguished from HiAgent's specific hierarchical mechanism — the exact internal mechanics of the HiAgent implementation (chunk size, summarization trigger, retrieval mechanism) were not retrieved in this session; would need a direct read of `reference-methods/hiagent/` source in the repo.
- No data on retrieval-based memory or explicit re-verification/self-check loops applied to the narrator (as opposed to the auditor) was found — this appears to be a genuine gap in the paper's scope, not just an access limitation, since the repo's `reference-methods/` directory is described as containing only Baseline and HiAgent.

### Inferences
- Because `SpecificationGenerator` is explicitly built to take arbitrary `source` material (not hardcoded to movie synopses) and a pluggable LLM `client`, the entire trajectory→commitments→facts extraction pipeline could plausibly be repointed at a different kind of source document — e.g., a hand-written campaign outline or session-zero pitch for our D&D-style game — to auto-generate an analogous fact-ledger/commitment-set/trajectory skeleton, which we could then either play forward turn-by-turn (as NCP-Bench does) or use as the backbone for directly synthesizing a "story so far" recap without ever running the live turns.
- The prompt design pattern worth copying wholesale: give every construction stage the *same* fixed "senior Narrative Logic Architect" persona and impose narrow, mechanically-checkable output constraints (atomic units, explicit forbidden constructs like "compound causal stages" or "mental-verb-only triggers," strict JSON-only output) — this is what makes the resulting spec auditable by another LLM later.
