# Progression/Mechanic Systems of Other Popular LitRPG / Progression-Fantasy Series

Scope note: Per assignment, this covers 5 series — Dungeon Crawler Carl, The Primal Hunter, Cradle, Mother of Learning, and Beware of Chicken — chosen from the suggested list as the ones with the most documented, distinctive mechanics reachable via available sources. Defiance of the Fall and Azarinth Healer were dropped from this pass in favor of the above; see Gaps in each section for what remained unverifiable. Fandom wiki pages for *The Primal Hunter* and *Dungeon Crawler Carl* (the primary wikis) returned HTTP 402/bot-verification errors through the fetch tool on most attempts, which constrained depth for those two relative to Cradle and Mother of Learning, where full wiki/lore pages were retrievable.

## Dungeon Crawler Carl (Matt Dinniman) — mechanical hook, build diversity, balance, adjudication load

### Takeaway
DCC's distinctive hook is that its "System" is diegetically a rigged, sadistic game-show interface (run by an AI producer) layered on fairly conventional RPG bones (stats, skills, levels, loot); build diversity comes from skills that auto-level through use and then branch into specialization trees, plus heavy equipment-stacking, rather than from a big upfront character-build menu.

### Cited Findings
- Skills are specific talents/nonmagical techniques crawlers learn via skill books or repeated use; they level automatically through use rather than by spending points, up to roughly level 10 before requiring a specialization choice — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki: Skills](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Skills)
- Skills carry real per-level numeric scaling, e.g. Pugilism grants +25% bare-knuckle damage per level, and IED (improvised explosives) grants +10% yield and a halved premature-detonation chance per level — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/skills/)
- Specializations force branching choices rather than pure additive stacking — e.g., a general "Explosives Handling" skill branches into "Dangerous Explosives Handling" or "Goblin Explosives," and specialization more broadly forces trade-offs like raw damage vs. elemental effects vs. reduced mana cost — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/skills/); [Skills](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Skills)
- Skills are distinct from Player Stats (general ability scores) and from "Benefits," which are one-off grants that cannot be leveled — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki: Skills](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Skills)
- The AI game-master occasionally grants "Unique" skills as rewards for unprecedented player feats — a discretionary, GM-adjudicated reward channel rather than a fixed table — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki: Skills](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.fandom.com/wiki/Skills)
- Equipment stacks additively onto skill effectiveness (e.g., a Toe Ring adds +3 to "Powerful Strike," War Gauntlets add +2 to "Iron Punch"), and consumable "Skill Tonics" grant temporary skill boosts — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/skills/)
- Deliberate pacing gate: stat point distribution is explicitly locked until "Floor 3" of the dungeon, meaning early play is driven entirely by automatic, action-based skill leveling rather than player-directed optimization — [Dungeon Crawler Carl Wiki](https://dungeon-crawler-carl.wiki/book-1/skills/)
- A broader review characterizes the series' incentive/progression loop as "airtight with crystal clear incentives and an addictive progression loop," while noting the series buries real depth, emotion, and complexity underneath the game-show gimmick — [Fabrice Grinda: Dungeon Crawler Carl review](https://fabricegrinda.substack.com/p/dungeon-crawler-carl-a-surprisingly)

### Inferences
- Because skill leveling is automatic (use-driven) rather than point-buy, an AI adjudicator would need to track *usage counts/context* per skill, not just allocate points on request — this is a meaningfully different bookkeeping burden than a classic point-buy system: the GM has to notice when an action qualifies as "using" a given skill and increment it, including edge cases (does using an explosive in a novel way count toward IED as well as a new skill?).
- The equipment-additive-bonus layering (flat + per-level scaling + gear + tonics) means damage/effect numbers for any single action can require summing 3-4 independent modifiers — for a live human or AI GM this is tractable but requires consistent bookkeeping infrastructure (a running character sheet with itemized bonus sources), similar to D&D bonus-stacking rules; the page-level reading experience hides this by only showing the final number to the reader.
- The "Floor 3" stat-lock is a soft difficulty/pacing control that a tabletop adjudicator could borrow directly: gate deliberate build optimization behind an early tutorial phase so new players learn action-based advancement before facing a big permanent allocation decision.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the primary Fandom "Player Stats" or "Feats" pages directly (HTTP 402 on WebFetch); feat-specific numeric mechanics (as opposed to skills) are therefore not verified here beyond the general Dexterity/Charisma effect descriptions surfaced via search snippets.
- No reader-complaint or power-creep discussion specifically about DCC's system was found in the searches run (search results returned unrelated pages); cannot confirm whether the fanbase has documented complaints about DCC's balance the way it has for some other series in this list.

## The Primal Hunter (Zogarth) — mechanical hook, build diversity, balance, adjudication load

### Takeaway
The Primal Hunter's System uses a conventional multi-attribute stat screen (Strength, Agility, Endurance, Perception, Intelligence, Willpower, Wisdom, etc.) gated by classes that grant large, unevenly-weighted per-level stat packages plus a pool of freely-assignable points, with hard per-tier stat caps — this produces heavier numeric bookkeeping than most peer series, and the term "Vessel" referenced in the prior brief could not be confirmed as a distinct named mechanic from available sources.

### Cited Findings
- Every sentient being who is "awakened" gets a Status Screen with attributes including Strength, Agility, Endurance, Perception, Intelligence, Willpower (and more) — [The Primal Hunter Wiki: The System](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/The_System)
- Skills are System-granted bonuses tied to Class or to achievement; they evolve through usage and can mutate into unique or overpowered variants — [The Primal Hunter Wiki: The System](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/The_System)
- Attribute points are earned from defeating creatures, completing System quests, and achieving System-recognized feats, and are then allocated by the player — [search-derived summary, sourced to] [The Primal Hunter Wiki](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/Classes)
- Classes grant large, unevenly weighted stat packages per level rather than uniform bonuses — the example surfaced was the "Apex Monster Hunter" class granting +175 Perception, +150 Agility, +125 Strength, +100 Endurance, +50 Intelligence, +50 Willpower, +50 Wisdom, and +125 freely-assignable points, per level — [The Primal Hunter Wiki: Classes](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/Classes)
- There are hard stat caps tied to power grade: a maximum of 22 total stats at "E-grade" for unrestricted professions, versus 27 for professions carrying severe restrictions (a risk/reward trade for a higher cap) — [The Primal Hunter Wiki: Grades/Profession](https://the-primal-hunter.fandom.com/wiki/Grades)

### Inferences
- The magnitude of these per-level numbers (dozens to hundreds of points per single level-up) implies the series is one of the more numerically "inflated" LitRPGs in this survey — for an adjudicator, this suggests raw stat totals are not meant to be intuited or estimated on the fly; they require an actual running ledger (spreadsheet-equivalent) rather than mental math, more so than Cradle's tier-only system or DCC's percentage-based skill bonuses.
- The existence of a cap that trades a *higher ceiling* for *restrictions* (27 vs 22 stats) is a distinct balancing lever worth noting for a TTRPG design: it lets the author (or a GM) offer "glass cannon" style specialization paths without those paths permanently dominating flexible generalist paths, since the restriction is qualitative (build constraints) rather than just numeric.

### Gaps
- "Vessel" as a named mechanic (per the assignment brief) could not be confirmed. Search results only turned up a narrative/flavor usage of the word "vessel" (referring to the hunter's body/form), not a distinct system mechanic comparable to "attributes" or "grades." This should be treated as unconfirmed/possibly a mischaracterization in the original brief rather than fact — flagging explicitly rather than inventing a mechanic to fit the name.
- Could not retrieve full text of the Fandom "Classes," "Skills," or "Profession" pages directly (HTTP 402 and bot-verification interstitials on repeated WebFetch attempts, including via a reader-proxy workaround); the findings above are reconstructed from search-engine summary snippets of those pages rather than full-page primary text, so some numeric details (e.g., whether +125 Free Points is typical or an outlier example) are less certain than for Cradle/Mother of Learning below.
- No documented reader complaints about Primal Hunter's power creep or stat bloat were found; targeted searches for this returned irrelevant results (unrelated "Primal Hunter" media). This is a genuine gap, not a confirmed absence of complaints — r/ProgressionFantasy discussion may exist but wasn't surfaced by the queries tried.

## Cradle (Will Wight) — mechanical hook, build diversity, balance, adjudication load

### Takeaway
Cradle's distinctive hook is a tiered, no-stat-screen power system (madra/Sacred Arts) where advancement is qualitative-rank-based rather than numeric-level-based, and build diversity comes from a small, combinable primitive set (a handful of "aspects" of energy crossed with four technique archetypes) rather than an open skill menu — this makes it unusually easy to adjudicate at a coarse grain (which rank wins) but requires GM judgment calls at a fine grain (how two same-rank builds interact).

### Cited Findings
- Madra is spiritual energy cycled through a core (a storage space beneath the navel); it can be replenished by cycling madra itself, by drawing in matching "vital aura" from the environment, or by consuming elixirs — [Abidan Archive Wiki: Sacred Arts](https://wiki.abidanarchive.com/index.php/Sacred_Arts)
- Sacred artists follow individual "Paths," each tied to a specific madra aspect (examples given: earth, stone, water, life, lightning, blood, shadow, fire, force); "pure madra" is the only type with no corresponding environmental vital aura — it's intrinsic — [Abidan Archive Wiki: Sacred Arts](https://wiki.abidanarchive.com/index.php/Sacred_Arts)
- Build diversity is generated by exactly two orthogonal, combinable primitives: (1) which aspect(s) a Path uses, and (2) which of four technique archetypes are applied to that aspect — Striker (projects madra outward), Enforcer (empowers body/equipment), Forger (creates independent madra constructs), and Ruler (controls ambient aura at scale). Advanced artists layer multiple technique types together for emergent complexity — [Abidan Archive Wiki: Sacred Arts](https://wiki.abidanarchive.com/index.php/Sacred_Arts)
- The rank ladder runs Foundation → Copper → Iron → Jade → Gold (Lowgold/Highgold) → Underlord → Overlord → Archlord → Herald/Sage → Monarch — [Abidan Archive Wiki: Sacred Arts](https://wiki.abidanarchive.com/index.php/Sacred_Arts)
- At Iron, sacred artists gain a superhuman body (faster, stronger, more durable than any normal human); at Jade, sensory perception changes drastically and artists can sense madra at great range — [Web-search-derived summary of Cradle rank descriptions] [One Last Forum: Cradle discussion and analysis](https://onelastforum.com/threads/cradle-by-will-wight-discussion-and-analysis.897/)
- Power scaling between ranks is described qualitatively but with a stated rule of thumb: "it takes ten Coppers to challenge an Iron, and ten Irons to challenge a Jade" — a roughly order-of-magnitude (10x) power gap per rank rather than a smooth curve — [One Last Forum: Cradle discussion and analysis](https://onelastforum.com/threads/cradle-by-will-wight-discussion-and-analysis.897/)
- Advancement is explicitly tied to "spiritual development and understanding" rather than time investment or grinding alone — a deliberate authorial choice to avoid pure numbers-go-up power creep — [Abidan Archive Wiki: Sacred Arts](https://wiki.abidanarchive.com/index.php/Sacred_Arts)

### Inferences
- The 10x-per-rank rule of thumb is a very GM-friendly balancing convention: it means an adjudicator (human or AI) mostly just needs to track *rank* (a single small-integer variable per combatant) to resolve most contests, rather than granular numeric stats — this is a much lower bookkeeping burden than Primal Hunter's per-attribute point totals, at the cost of coarser resolution (no meaningful mechanical difference between two characters of the same rank unless the GM improvises one).
- Because build diversity is generated from a small combinable primitive set (aspect × technique-archetype) rather than an open menu, Cradle is structurally the closest of this survey's series to a "small ruleset, high combinatorial depth" TTRPG design — worth flagging as a direct model for an AI-run system that wants distinctiveness without an exploding rules surface.
- The absence of an on-page stat screen (by design) means the "numbers" in Cradle are almost entirely inferential/narrative (rank name + descriptive fluff) rather than mechanical; an AI GM adapting this would need to invent the actual resolution math (e.g., what rank difference translates to in dice terms) since the source material intentionally does not commit to one.

### Gaps
- No specific reader complaints or documented power-creep controversies about Cradle's system were surfaced in this pass; the source found (a forum discussion/analysis thread) is itself un-excerpted beyond what the fetch tool summarized, so deeper community-sentiment analysis (e.g., specific gripes about pacing between Jade and Gold, or about later-book power tiers like Underlord/Overlord/Monarch) was not obtained and would need a follow-up search specifically on Reddit/RoyalRoad comment threads.
- Numeric specifics (if any exist for madra pool sizes, cycling speed, etc.) were not found — Cradle appears to genuinely avoid exposing numbers to the reader, consistent with its "progression fantasy without explicit stat screens" framing, so this may not be a gap in research so much as a property of the source material.

## Mother of Learning (nobody103) — mechanical hook, build diversity, balance, adjudication load

### Takeaway
Mother of Learning's hook is a rigorously systematized, hard-magic system (not a game-screen LitRPG) combined with a time loop, where character "build" is generated entirely through accumulated procedural/declarative knowledge (which spells and shaping techniques the protagonist has learned) rather than through points, levels, or classes — build diversity comes from combining a fixed, non-expanding set of "spell element" primitives in novel ways.

### Cited Findings
- Mana is the foundational resource for all magic; having sufficient mana is necessary but not sufficient — mages must also learn to shape and direct it toward a coherent goal — [Mother of Learning Universe: Basics of Magic – Spellcasting](https://motheroflearninguniverse.wordpress.com/2017/08/10/basics-of-magic-spellcasting/)
- Before any spellcasting, a mage must master five prerequisite skills: sensing one's own mana reserves, drawing on mana quickly/reliably, directing mana along specific pathways, visualizing the desired effect clearly, and maintaining concentration through the casting process. This foundational training takes an average mage roughly 2-4 years and cannot be shortcut via soul manipulation — [Mother of Learning Universe: Basics of Magic – Spellcasting](https://motheroflearninguniverse.wordpress.com/2017/08/10/basics-of-magic-spellcasting/)
- There are two parallel casting modes: Unstructured magic (trial-and-error visualization/shaping without a spell boundary — flexible, but can take years/decades to master a complex effect) and Structured magic (rigid mana constructs invoked via fixed words/gestures — "spells" — that drastically narrow the possibility space, making them learnable in weeks, at the cost of adaptability) — [Mother of Learning Wiki: Spellcasting](https://mother-of-learning.fandom.com/wiki/Spellcasting) (via search summary); [Mother of Learning Universe](https://motheroflearninguniverse.wordpress.com/2017/08/10/basics-of-magic-spellcasting/)
- Spells are built from modular "spell elements" (comparable to interlocking building blocks), each bound to specific verbal/gestural "proxies." Crucially, humans cannot invent wholly new spell elements — the primitive set is fixed — but existing elements can be recombined in novel ways relatively easily, which is the series' explicit build-diversity mechanism — [Mother of Learning Universe: Basics of Magic – Spellcasting](https://motheroflearninguniverse.wordpress.com/2017/08/10/basics-of-magic-spellcasting/)
- Magic is organized into fifteen major disciplines plus two support fields, including Projection (light/sound/heat/force/electricity effects), Negation (dispelling/disrupting other spells — considered mandatory for every mage), Animation (infusing a fragment of the caster's mind into a target for semi-autonomous effects), and Blood Magic (an exotic, more-potent-than-normal mana drawn from life force) — [Mother of Learning Wiki: Magic](https://mother-of-learning.fandom.com/wiki/Magic) (via search summary)
- Advanced practitioners can blend both casting modes: building enough unstructured mastery over spells they use often that they gain flexibility even within a structured spell's rigid boundary, and can eventually cast reflexively without verbal/gestural components — [Mother of Learning Universe: Basics of Magic – Spellcasting](https://motheroflearninguniverse.wordpress.com/2017/08/10/basics-of-magic-spellcasting/)

### Inferences
- The time-loop's interaction with this system is structurally elegant for an AI-run game: because raw magical *power* (mana capacity, physical skill) resets each loop but *procedural knowledge* (which spell-element combinations work, which disciplines to prioritize, shaping shortcuts) does not need to be re-learned by the player/reader — only re-executed by the character — the effective "build" is a knowledge-graph the player accumulates out-of-character. A tabletop/AI adaptation of this pattern would need a persistent player-facing "known techniques" ledger that survives in-fiction resets, which is a very different persistence model than XP/levels.
- The fixed-primitive-set-with-recombination design (can't invent new spell elements, can combine existing ones) is a strong anti-power-creep mechanism: it bounds the total possibility space even though the *combinatorial* space (which recombinations exist) is very large — this is a useful model for a GM-adjudicated system that wants "endless-feeling" character options without an ever-growing rules list to maintain.
- Because so much of the system's depth is in prerequisite skill mastery and years-long training timelines, it is naturally suited to a slow, knowledge-based long campaign but would need explicit compression rules (skipped/abstracted training time) to run at tabletop session pace.

### Gaps
- No specific documented reader complaints about balance/power creep in Mother of Learning's magic system were found in this pass; searches focused on mechanics description rather than community sentiment, and a follow-up targeted at r/ProgressionFantasy or the Royal Road comment threads would be needed to fill this gap. Given the system is more "hard magic" than competitive-numbers LitRPG, balance complaints (if any) likely concern narrative pacing/loop-count rather than mechanical fairness — but this is inference, not a sourced claim.
- Exact numeric costs (mana cost per spell tier, spell circle/tier numbering) were referenced by titles found in search ("spell tiers/circles") but not confirmed with citable numbers in the content actually retrieved; treat any specific tier-numbering claims as unconfirmed.

## Beware of Chicken (Casualfarmer) — mechanical hook, build diversity, balance, adjudication load

### Takeaway
Beware of Chicken is the most thinly mechanically documented series in this survey by design: its distinctive hook is that it deliberately *de-emphasizes* stat-screen/numeric cultivation mechanics in favor of a "cozy," slice-of-life framing, so concrete numeric progression details are largely absent from available secondary sources — this itself is the notable finding, not a gap to be padded over.

### Cited Findings
- The novel keeps standard xianxia furniture — qi, spiritual cultivation, cultivation realms, sects, and cultivation politics — as background texture rather than a foregrounded numeric system, and one major character (a rooster) develops its own cultivation path and philosophy by absorbing ambient qi, illustrating the series' focus on characters' relationship to cultivation rather than on quantified power levels — [Immortal Cultivation Hub: Beware of Chicken review](https://immortalcultivationhub.com/beware-of-chicken-cultivation-novel/)
- Named realms include (at minimum) Spiritual, Earth, Sky, and Divine, with the Spiritual realm associated with a cultivator's soul becoming tangible enough to use as armor and with core formation, the Earth realm with the body being remade (skin to jade, bones to metal), and the Sky realm with a cultivator manifesting a personal "Domain" — [Beware of Chicken Wiki: Cultivation Concepts](https://beware-of-chicken.fandom.com/wiki/Cultivation_Concepts) (via search summary)
- A separate search surfaced a broader seven-realm list — Initiate, Profound, Spiritual, Earth, Sky, Imperial, and Heavenly/Divine — each said to have five minor stages, though this full list and the "five minor stages" detail could not be corroborated against primary wiki text (the Fandom page itself returned an access error on direct fetch) — [search-engine synthesis, sourced to] [Beware of Chicken Wiki: Cultivation Concepts](https://beware-of-chicken.fandom.com/wiki/Cultivation_Concepts)
- The farm setting (Fa Ram) has itself become a qi-rich location through the protagonist's cultivation and his animals/disciples' efforts — cultivation quality is tied to environment/place, not just individual stat investment — [Immortal Cultivation Hub: Beware of Chicken review](https://immortalcultivationhub.com/beware-of-chicken-cultivation-novel/)
- A dedicated technical-analysis attempt on this series explicitly concluded that "concrete mechanical details about cultivation systems are notably absent" from available secondary-source review material, and that the novel "redirects cultivation mechanics toward different narrative purposes — farming yields, animal companion development, and philosophical growth" rather than numeric power escalation — [Immortal Cultivation Hub: Beware of Chicken review](https://immortalcultivationhub.com/beware-of-chicken-cultivation-novel/)

### Inferences
- Because the two searches for realm names returned two different, only partially overlapping lists (4 named realms vs. a 7-realm list with "five minor stages" each), and neither was confirmed against primary wiki text, the realm taxonomy for this series should be treated as low-confidence pending direct primary-source verification — flagging explicitly rather than presenting either list as canon.
- For an AI-run tabletop adaptation, Beware of Chicken is most useful not as a source of numeric mechanics to borrow, but as a design counter-example: it demonstrates that a cultivation/progression framing can work narratively with the *mechanics kept almost entirely off-page* — rank names and vague qualitative descriptions ("skin turns to jade") substitute for stat blocks. This is the lowest-adjudication-complexity model in the survey, but only because the system offloads almost all resolution to GM/author narrative judgment rather than rules — which is exactly the trade-off a tabletop AI GM would need to consciously choose (narrative fiat vs. rules transparency) rather than get "for free."

### Gaps
- Direct primary-source text (the Fandom "Cultivation Concepts" page) was not retrievable — attempts via WebFetch returned HTTP 402 errors, and a general web review site explicitly stated it lacked the technical detail needed. The realm list, minor-stage structure, and any numeric qi/core mechanics remain unconfirmed beyond the partial, possibly-conflicting summaries above.
- No documented reader complaints about balance or power creep were found for this series; given its explicitly cozy, non-power-fantasy framing, it's plausible that power-creep complaints are rare or absent in this fandom relative to numbers-heavy LitRPGs — but this is inference, not a sourced finding, and a targeted RoyalRoad-comments or r/ProgressionFantasy search would be needed to confirm either way.
- Per the assignment's own fallback instruction ("if a series turns out to be thinly documented, say so explicitly rather than padding, and consider swapping in an alternate"): this series should be considered the weakest-documented entry in this note set via the sources reachable in this pass, and a report writer may want to treat Beware of Chicken's inclusion as lower-confidence/more narrative-inferential than the other four series covered here.
