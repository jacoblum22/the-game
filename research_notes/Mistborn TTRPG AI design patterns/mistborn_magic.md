# Mistborn's Magic Systems (Allomancy, Feruchemy, Hemalurgy) for TTRPG/AI-DM Design

*Scope note: per assignment, this pass does not re-research Stormlight/Surgebinding in depth — see the existing `stormlight_archive_magic.md` note for that system. Surgebinding facts referenced here for comparison are carried over from that note, not re-verified in this pass.*

## How does Allomancy work — the 16 metals, the Physical/Mental × Internal/External × Pushing/Pulling grid, and Mistings vs. Mistborn?

### Takeaway
Allomancy is a "chemistry-set" magic system: 16 metals are organized into a strict 2×2×2×2-style grid (four thematic groupings — Physical, Mental, Temporal, Enhancement — each split into Internal/External and Pushing/Pulling, with each base metal paired to one alloy), and a character's access to this grid is binary and discrete — a **Misting** burns exactly one of the 8 basic metals for one fixed effect for as long as they live, while a **Mistborn** can burn all 16 (or at least all "basic" ones, per some source variance) simultaneously, making Mistborn a full-grid unlock rather than an incremental one.

### Cited Findings
- There are 16 Allomantic metals total: "8 basic metals (4 base metals and 4 alloys) and 8 Higher metals (4 base metals and 4 alloys)," organized into four groupings — **Physical, Mental, Temporal, and Enhancement** — each grouping containing two base metals and their alloys. — [WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind/fan-wiki content](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Allomancy)
- Push/Pull rule: "One Pushes, one Pulls — the second is usually an alloy of the first. Each elemental metal is classified as a 'Pulling' ability, whereas each alloy is classified as a 'Pushing' ability." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Allomancy)
- Confirmed metal-effect pairs from the 8 basic ("Physical"/"Mental") metals: **Iron** pulls nearby metal objects toward the user; its alloy **Steel** pushes metal objects away. **Tin** (pull) enhances senses; its alloy **Pewter** (push) enhances physical strength/speed/healing. **Zinc** (pull) "riots"/inflames emotions; its alloy **Brass** (push) soothes/dampens emotions. **Copper** (pull) conceals Allomantic use from Seekers; its alloy **Bronze** (push) detects/reveals others' Allomantic pulses. — [readingseconds.com](https://readingseconds.com/allomancy-chart-mistborn-metals-explained/); [booknookcook.com](https://booknookcook.com/metallic-arts-guide/)
- Higher-metal effects noted (Temporal/Enhancement groupings, less consistently sourced across fan summaries — flagged as lower-confidence): Atium and its alloy grant future-sight/past-revealing effects; Gold and its alloy relate to alternate-self/past-and-future perception; Aluminum "destroys" Allomantic reserves (nullifies/wipes powers); Duralumin amplifies whatever other metal is burned simultaneously with it. — [readingseconds.com](https://readingseconds.com/allomancy-chart-mistborn-metals-explained/); [booknookcook.com](https://booknookcook.com/metallic-arts-guide/) — *note: these two sources gave inconsistent/incomplete names for the Temporal and Enhancement metals (one used "Malatium/Electrum," the other left the eight Higher metals unnamed), so treat exact Higher-metal names/effects as a gap requiring Coppermind primary-page verification.*
- Design origin, in Sanderson's own words: "I want something that has a feel of a periodic table, and the feel of alchemy, but that actually works. And where I arrived was eating metal." — [Brandon Sanderson, quoted via winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-explains-how-he-came-up-with-the-mistborn-magic-system)
- Design origin of the push/pull physics: Sanderson traced the idea to wanting a version of *Star Wars* telekinesis "with the limitations that he originally thought the Force had," landing on Newton's third law (equal/opposite reaction) as the governing logic — iron pulls, steel pushes — and this reciprocal logic was then propagated across all four thematic groupings (each gets its own push/pull split, and separately an internal/external split). — [winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-explains-how-he-came-up-with-the-mistborn-magic-system); [crrowenson.com](https://crrowenson.com/analysis-of-magic-systems/allomancy-perfect-blend-of-physics-and-imagination/)
- Mass-scaling rule (a concrete, physics-grounded limitation, not just flavor): pushing on a light object (a coin) sends the object flying; pushing on a much heavier anchor (a building) instead sends the *user* flying — the same "push" verb resolves differently depending on relative mass of self vs. target, per Newton's third law. — [crrowenson.com](https://crrowenson.com/analysis-of-magic-systems/allomancy-perfect-blend-of-physics-and-imagination/)
- Access is binary, not gradient: "A Mistborn can burn 'two or more metals,' while a Misting operates with 'only one of the eight basic metals.'" — [readingseconds.com](https://readingseconds.com/allomancy-chart-mistborn-metals-explained/)
- Tabletop-adaptation framing (Mistborn Adventure Game, Crafty Games): "Mistings can use but a single metal, while Mistborn can use all the different metals, simultaneously, if desired." Each metal burns at a different rate (instantaneous up to 40 minutes of real-world duration) and has a "flare" mode that boosts potency at the cost of drastically accelerated consumption — an explicit power/resource-drain dial layered on top of the binary Misting/Mistborn access split. Because Allomancy works by *ingesting* metal, metal purity/toxic contaminants can harm the user — a physical-world tie-in cost distinct from the "limitation vs. cost vs. weakness" taxonomy noted in the Stormlight pass. — [WebSearch synthesis of Mistborn Adventure Game rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistborn_Adventure_Game)

### Inferences
- The grid is fundamentally *combinatorial but closed*: 4 groupings × (internal/external) × (push/pull) = 16 fixed slots, no more, no fewer — structurally similar to Surgebinding's "closed set of 10 Surges" discipline (Sanderson's Third Law: economy over breadth), but Allomancy achieves closure via a literal 2D chemistry-table layout rather than Surgebinding's cyclic-adjacency-pair rule.
- Because a Misting's single metal is a *fixed, permanent, lifelong trait* (not a spendable resource or unlockable tier), Mistings function like single-class characters in a class-based TTRPG with zero multiclassing, whereas Mistborn function like an "unlock everything" prestige class — this is a much starker access cliff than Surgebinding's Order structure, where every Order gets *two* Surges (never one, never all ten) via a bond that's earned/chosen by demonstrated personality, not by birth lottery.
- Unlike Surgebinding (spren bond attracted by personality/behavior), Allomantic/Feruchemical power in Mistborn is **genetic/hereditary** — access is a birth trait, not something earned by character growth or oath — which is the single biggest structural divergence between the two systems' *acquisition* fiction, even though their *execution* fictions (named discrete powers, burned/held resource) rhyme closely.

### Gaps
- The exact canonical names and effects of all 8 "Higher" metals (Temporal and Enhancement groupings) were not consistently retrieved — Coppermind's own Allomancy page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch in this session, and the two secondary-source summaries fetched disagreed on naming (one listed "Malatium/Electrum," the other left them entirely unnamed). A follow-up session should retry Coppermind directly (perhaps via a cached/mirror route) or fetch the dedicated `Allomancy/table` subpage for a verified full 16-metal table.
- Whether "Mistborn" access literally means all 16 metals or is sometimes described in-universe as limited to the 8 "basic" metals with separate rarer titles for Higher-metal users was not fully disambiguated in this pass (secondary sources used "two or more" vs. "all" inconsistently).

## How does Feruchemy work — storing vs. burning, and how does this differ *in kind* from Allomancy?

### Takeaway
Feruchemy is a storage/retrieval economy, not a consumption economy: a Feruchemist voluntarily *weakens* themselves in some attribute to charge a "metalmind" (a piece of the relevant metal), then later *taps* that same metalmind to retrieve exactly what was put in, at the cost of a period of weakness — it is explicitly zero-sum/conservation-based, in direct mechanical contrast to Allomancy, which is single-directional consumption (burn a metal, generate an effect that came from nowhere but the metal, no compensating personal weakness beforehand).

### Cited Findings
- Naming/mechanics: "Pieces of metal that have been charged by Feruchemists are referred to as metalminds in general, or more specifically as the type of metal with an added '-mind' suffix. ... The act of charging, or Investing, a metalmind is referred to as storing and the act of retrieving the stored attribute is referred to as tapping." — [WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind content](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Feruchemy)
- Specific metal-attribute examples: "Copper is used to store memories, which is why they are sometimes referred to as copperminds." "Aluminum is the internal enhancement pulling metal. Feruchemists can use aluminum to store Identity." "Tapping zinc has the odd side effect of making the user hungry" (a concrete diegetic side-cost example). — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Feruchemy)
- Tabletop-adaptation framing (Mistborn Adventure Game) states the storage/weakness trade explicitly: "While storing strength, the Feruchemist is physically weaker; later, when the Feruchemist 'taps' the metalmind, he can summon the stored strength to make him stronger than usual." — [WebSearch synthesis of Mistborn Adventure Game rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistborn_Adventure_Game)
- Design origin, in Sanderson's own words: Feruchemy grew out of his personal experience with insomnia — "Why can't I save my sleep, and then turn it on when I actually want to go to sleep?" — leading to "a magic system where you can store attributes of yourself." Feruchemy was originally designed for a separate, unpublished novel and only later folded into the Mistborn setting, where metal was retrofitted in as "a thematic tie" to Allomancy. — [Brandon Sanderson, quoted via winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-breaks-down-origin-mistborn-feruchemy-hemalurgy)
- Cosmology framing found via Hemalurgy research (useful cross-reference): "Allomancy is the art of Preservation and Feruchemy is the art of balance" — i.e., in-world metaphysics explicitly labels Feruchemy's defining trait as *balance* (conservation), reinforcing that storing and tapping must net to zero, as opposed to Allomancy's Preservation-flavored one-way release. — [WebSearch synthesis, in contrast with Hemalurgy sourcing below](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)
- Ferrings (single-metal Feruchemists, the Feruchemical analogue of a Misting) vs. full Feruchemists (all-metal analogue of Mistborn) exist as a parallel rarity structure to Allomancy's Misting/Mistborn split — this structural parallel is stated in secondary summaries but a directly quotable primary-source line naming "Ferring" specifically was not captured in this pass (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- This is the sharpest *mechanical-kind* difference between Sanderson's two flagship magic systems for TTRPG purposes: Allomancy is a **spend-a-consumable-resource-for-an-effect** loop (classic "mana burn"), while Feruchemy is a **debt/loan** loop — you must first go into a deficit (weaken yourself) before you can later draw a surplus, and the total across both phases is conserved. A TTRPG rule system wanting "two flavors of the same underlying resource logic" could map Allomancy to consumable charges/spell slots and Feruchemy to a debt-and-repayment stat-swap mechanic (temporarily lower one stat now to raise it later, net zero over time) — a genuinely different action-economy shape than a burn-resource, not just a reskin.
- Because Feruchemy's charge/discharge is *identity-locked* to the same person storing and tapping (with the notable narrative exception of unkeyed metalminds, which anyone can tap — flagged in Gaps as unconfirmed detail this pass), it also differs from Allomancy in *transferability*: Allomantic metal is consumed on burning and grants no lingering object of value, whereas a charged metalmind is itself a physical asset that (depending on keying) could be stolen, hoarded, or passed along — a distinct "banked power as loot" design lever unavailable to pure Allomancy.

### Gaps
- Coppermind's dedicated `Feruchemy` and `Feruchemy/table` pages returned HTTP 403 on direct WebFetch in this session; a full, verified 16-metal-to-attribute table (all 16 Feruchemical metals + exact stored attribute for each) was not obtained — only 3 of 16 (copper/memories, aluminum/identity, zinc/mental speed with hunger side-effect) were confirmed via secondary sources.
- The unkeyed-vs-keyed metalmind distinction (whether an unkeyed metalmind can be tapped by *anyone*, not just the original Feruchemist, which is a major loot/theft implication) was referenced in the original task's key questions but not independently confirmed with a citable source in this pass.
- Nicrosil (the Feruchemical metal associated with Compounding, per some fan sources) was not independently verified in this pass beyond the Compounding findings below.

## How does Hemalurgy work — spikes, quantified cost, and "power has a traceable, transferable cost"?

### Takeaway
Hemalurgy steals a specific Allomantic or Feruchemical power from a person (who must be killed, or at least have the spike driven through their heart) by driving a metal spike through the victim's heart and then into a recipient's body at a location that determines which power is granted; critically, the transfer is explicitly lossy — Hemalurgy is narratively defined as "the art of Ruin" precisely *because* the act of transferring power destroys some of it, making cost a first-class, in-world-labeled property of the system rather than an implicit inefficiency, though this pass could not source an exact numeric percentage for that loss.
### Cited Findings
- Mechanical procedure: "a metal spike must be driven through someone's heart, then the spike is taken and stabbed into the body of another person, the location of which determines the power transferred. The preferred method is to stab it directly through the heart into the other person, as the longer it is left out of the body the more power is lost." — [WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind/17th Shard content](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)
- Explicit cosmological cost-framing, tying the three systems to Sanderson's Shard-based metaphysics: "Allomancy is the art of Preservation and Feruchemy is the art of balance, [while] Hemalurgy is the art of Ruin, as the transfer of power destroys some of it." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)
- Lossiness is time-dependent and only partially mitigable: "Arcanists discovered a way to completely avoid Hemalurgic decay outside of the body. However, there is still an amount of Investiture lost during the creation of the spike [itself]" — i.e., there are (at least) two distinct loss points: (1) an unavoidable creation-time loss baked into the act of spiking, and (2) an additional decay-over-time loss while the spike is out-of-body between donor and recipient, which can reportedly be engineered away with sufficient skill/knowledge. — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)
- Broader consequence beyond raw power loss: "having a hemalurgic enhancement makes one susceptible to a shard's influence or even to their control" — cited examples are kandra and koloss, both Hemalurgically-enhanced beings that can be externally controlled by Investiture poured into their "damaged spirit web." This is a second, qualitatively different cost axis (loss of autonomy/vulnerability to outside control) layered on top of the raw quantitative power loss. — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy)
- Design intent, in Sanderson's own words: "I wanted to have a magic system that had some moral complications to it" — Hemalurgy was deliberately built to be the *morally compromised* system among the three, inspired by David Farland's *Runelords* series and its magic of "stealing people's strength." Sanderson also states Hemalurgy was designed as a cosmere-wide "wild card": "Hemalurgy can interact with every one of the magics," built specifically so it would transcend any single planet's local rules, unlike Allomancy/Feruchemy which (per this synthesis) are more tied to their home world. — [Brandon Sanderson, quoted via winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-breaks-down-origin-mistborn-feruchemy-hemalurgy)
- Encoding problem Sanderson had to solve mechanically: "To be able to steal both Allomancy and Feruchemy with sixteen metals meant needing a mechanism to determine which power got stolen. If you drove a pewter spike into a person who was both an Allomancer and a Feruchemist, then how would that spike know which power to suck out and grant to the one who would gain it? The location of the spike in the receiver, and how it was used to kill a person, influenced how the power was shaped." — [WebSearch synthesis, citing fan analysis (themeltingthought2000.wordpress.com) drawing on in-text Ars Arcanum material](https://themeltingthought2000.wordpress.com/2015/10/19/mistborn-reading-guide-part-viii-hemalurgic-spikes/)

### Inferences
- Hemalurgy is Sanderson's cleanest "power has a traceable, transferable, and *lossy* cost" mechanic among his major systems, and it explicitly separates **two independent cost axes** that a rule-flexibility/exception system could borrow separately: (1) a *quantitative* tax on the transfer itself (some power always evaporates — an explicit "you never get 100% of what you take" rule), and (2) a *qualitative* tax on the recipient (increased susceptibility to external control) that has nothing to do with raw power level. A homebrew "steal/exception" mechanic could charge both a numeric tax and a separate structural vulnerability, rather than just one flat penalty.
- The location-on-body + method-of-death encoding scheme is itself a designed solution to a combinatorial-explosion problem (16 metals × 2 source systems × unknown number of body locations) — worth flagging for an AI-DM system that wants a similarly "legible but rich" targeting grammar for a steal/transfer mechanic: encode *what* is stolen via *where*/*how* the exception is invoked, rather than requiring a lookup table per metal.
- The unresolved numeric-percentage question (see Gaps) matters for TTRPG translation: without a sourced number, an AI-DM system borrowing this mechanic should treat "some meaningful, undisclosed fraction is always lost" as the transferable design principle, rather than inventing a specific percentage and attributing it to Sanderson.

### Gaps
- **No specific numeric percentage for Hemalurgic power loss (e.g., "10%," "one-tenth") was found and verified in this research pass.** Multiple targeted searches (including directly for "one tenth," "10%," and general "cost"/"loss" phrasing against Coppermind and the WoB/Arcanum database) returned only qualitative confirmation that loss occurs, not a sourced quantity. Coppermind's own `Hemalurgy` page and the `wob.coppermind.net` advanced-search results for the "hemalurgy" tag were identified as the correct primary sources but returned HTTP 403 (Coppermind) or were not directly fetched (Arcanum search results page, which is a search-results index rather than a quote source) within this session's tool budget. **A follow-up session should treat this as the single highest-priority remaining gap** and attempt: (a) a direct fetch of `https://coppermind.net/wiki/Hemalurgy` via a different fetch path/proxy, (b) a direct fetch of specific WoB entries from `wob.coppermind.net` filtered to the hemalurgy tag, or (c) the "Shardcast: Hemalurgy" podcast page/transcript, which was identified in search results but not fetched.
- Exact spike-to-body-location mapping (which body part yields which power category) was referenced as existing but not itself itemized/sourced in this pass.

## What is a Twinborn, and how does combining Allomancy + Feruchemy multiply build diversity?

### Takeaway
A Twinborn is a Metalborn with exactly one Allomantic power *and* one Feruchemical power (introduced as a character type starting in *The Alloy of Law*, generations after the original trilogy, as a consequence of Terris/other-population intermarriage); critically, the two powers need not share a metal, but when they *do* share a metal the Twinborn unlocks **Compounding** — burning a charged metalmind Allomantically releases the stored Feruchemical attribute "tenfold," a quantified, named power-multiplier that exists nowhere in single-system builds.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: "A Twinborn is a new type of Allomancer/Feruchemist introduced in *The Alloy of Law*. A Twinborn is a Metalborn with at least one Allomantic power and one Feruchemical power." — [WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind/Mistborn Fandom content](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Twinborn)
- The two powers are independently rolled: "The metals a Twinborn uses for Allomancy and Feruchemy do not have to be the same. However, if a Twinborn shares a single metal with both Allomancy and Feruchemy, its power is dramatically increased." — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Twinborn)
- Compounding mechanic and quantified multiplier: "Having a shared metal allows you to compound the Feruchemical power by burning its metalmind, releasing the charge tenfold. This gives a supercharged burst of the Feruchemical attribute, rather than the typical Allomantic ability." Worked example: "A Twinborn with both abilities in iron can store weight briefly in a metalmind and then burn the metalmind to draw out an order of magnitude more weight than was originally stored (instead of receiving the usual Pulling ability associated with the Allomantic burning of iron)." — [WebSearch synthesis of Coppermind's Compounding page](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Compounding)
- Named example and consequence: "Miles 'Hundred-Lives' Dagouter Compounded gold (Health), getting near infinite amounts of Healing" — an explicit textual example of Compounding being powerful enough to functionally break the normal healing-cost curve. — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Twinborn)
- Historical/rarity gating, in two layers: (1) Twinborn as a category "only began to appear after the Final Ascension, due to the intermingling of the Terris people [Feruchemists] with the other citizens of the Final Empire [Allomancers]" — i.e., Twinborn did not exist during the original trilogy's setting because the two populations were kept genetically separate; (2) Compounding specifically "was a one-time occurrence during the time of the Lord Ruler due to the fact that Feruchemists and Allomancers did not commonly mix sexually (a strict breeding program was enforced on the Terris people) and therefore could not genetically mix the powers," meaning Compounding-capable Twinborn only became possible generations later, in the Wax and Wayne era. — [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Twinborn)

### Inferences
- Twinborn is a textbook "multiclass" build in TTRPG terms, but Sanderson adds a genuine third design layer beyond simple multiclassing: an *alignment bonus*. Rolling the same metal for both halves of the multiclass doesn't just stack two separate power lists (which two *different* metals would already do) — it unlocks a categorically different, more powerful *third* ability (Compounding) that exists in neither parent system alone. This is a strong pattern for an AI-DM combinatorial build system: reward "aligned" dual-power picks with an emergent bonus ability, not just cumulative access, to make the combinatorics *non-linear* (16 Allomantic × 16 Feruchemical = 256 raw Twinborn combos, but the 16 "matched" combos are qualitatively special, not just statistically rarer).
- The historical/genetic gating of both Twinborn and Compounding (population-mixing as prerequisite) is Sanderson using *setting history* as a rarity lever, separate from any in-scene mechanical cost — a reminder that "how rare is this build" doesn't have to be encoded as a stat or resource cost; it can be encoded as "this combination was not previously possible given the world's social structure," which is a worldbuilding-level balance tool an AI DM could use to stagger the introduction of powerful build combinations across a campaign's timeline.

### Gaps
- Precise numeric rarity statistics (e.g., "% of population that are Twinborn vs. Mistborn vs. full Feruchemist") were not found/sourced in this pass — likely because such census-style numbers may not be canonically stated, but this should be treated as unconfirmed rather than assumed absent.
- Whether Compounding is available to *any* matched-metal Twinborn or requires additional unlocking conditions beyond simply sharing the metal was not fully disambiguated (the Lord-Ruler-era "one-time occurrence" framing suggests possible additional rarity gates beyond the shared-metal condition, but this wasn't confirmed with a direct quote).

## How does this compare to Surgebinding's design — grid/chemistry structure vs. fixed-pair structure — and what does each offer that the other doesn't?

### Takeaway
Surgebinding (per the existing Stormlight note) is a *cyclic, closed pairing system* — 10 Surges arranged so each of 10 Orders gets one fixed, non-overlapping adjacent pair, with access gated by an externally-bestowed spren bond keyed to personality — while Allomancy is a *grid/table system* — 16 metals arranged in a 4-groupings × internal/external × push/pull matrix, with access gated by birth/genetics into a binary single-metal-or-all-metals split (later extended to a second, independent Feruchemical grid, then a third cross-cutting Hemalurgic theft layer). The grid format buys Allomancy far higher combinatorial *build* diversity (Twinborn × Compounding × Hemalurgic spikes stack multiplicatively) at the cost of a much less structured *narrative* growth curve than Surgebinding's oath-gated tiers.

### Cited Findings
- Surgebinding structure (carried over from the existing Stormlight note, not re-verified this pass): 10 Surges, each of the 10 Orders gets a fixed, non-repeating adjacent pair in a closed cycle; access is via a Nahel spren-bond attracted by the candidate's demonstrated personality attributes, not by birth; power growth within an Order is staged behind 5 narratively-gated Ideals (oaths), with universal Ideal 1 and Order-specific Ideals 2-5, unlocking Shardblade (Ideal 3) and Shardplate (Ideal 4) at fixed checkpoints. — [prior note, citing Coppermind: Surgebinding and Knights Radiant]
- Allomancy structure (this pass): 16 metals in a 4×2×2 grid (Physical/Mental/Temporal/Enhancement × Internal/External × Push/Pull); access is binary by birth (Misting = exactly one metal for life; Mistborn = all metals) rather than earned/staged. — [readingseconds.com](https://readingseconds.com/allomancy-chart-mistborn-metals-explained/); [WebSearch synthesis](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Allomancy)
- Layering: Feruchemy is a *second, independently-gridded* system (its own 16 metals, its own Ferring/full-Feruchemist binary), and Hemalurgy is a *third, cross-cutting* system that can graft a stolen Allomantic or Feruchemical power from either grid onto anyone via spikes — meaning Mistborn-verse combinatorics are explicitly three-layer-deep (which single metal(s) by birth × which stolen power(s) by spike × whether a shared-metal Twinborn pairing unlocks Compounding), whereas Surgebinding (per the prior note) has essentially two layers (which Order/pair by spren-bond × which Ideal-tier by oath), plus the separately-gated Shard artifacts.
- Twinborn/Compounding (this pass, see above) shows Allomancy-side combinatorics scale *multiplicatively*: 16 Allomantic × 16 Feruchemical raw pairings, with the 16 same-metal pairings additionally unlocking a distinct emergent ability (Compounding) not found in either parent system.
- Design-origin contrast, in Sanderson's own words: Allomancy was explicitly designed to feel like "a periodic table" and "alchemy, but that actually works," i.e., structured around a visual/tabular chemistry metaphor from the outset — [winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-explains-how-he-came-up-with-the-mistborn-magic-system) — whereas the prior Stormlight note found Surgebinding's defining Third-Law discipline framed instead around "expand on what you have" economy-of-powers and thematic interconnection (e.g., deriving Mistborn's own powers from "what thieves would want" thematically) rather than an explicit periodic-table visual metaphor — [prior note, citing Coppermind: Sanderson's Laws of Magic].
- Balance implication acknowledged directly by a tabletop adaptation team (Mistborn Adventure Game/Crafty Games): the designers explicitly identified "how do you let people play Mistborn and not have everybody else suck" as a core design problem, and addressed it by giving Mistborn characters deliberately "mediocre stats at the beginning of the game" and using an attribute-strength/weakness allocation system to keep Misting characters competitive despite having only 1/16th (or 1/8th) of a Mistborn's raw toolkit. — [WebSearch synthesis of Mistborn Adventure Game reception/design discussion](https://fandomwire.com/10-most-overpowered-metallic-arts-in-mistborn-ranked/)

### Inferences
- The grid (Allomancy) vs. cyclic-pair (Surgebinding) distinction maps to two different TTRPG "class design" philosophies: a **grid** naturally supports *many independent single-power specialists* (16 Misting "classes," each dead simple: one verb, one resource) plus a rare *hybrid superclass* (Mistborn) that is a strict superset — good for a game that wants lots of cheap, legible NPC power-types and a small number of exceptional PCs/villains. A **cyclic pair system** naturally supports *fewer, thematically-paired dual-specialists* (10 "classes," each already a mini-multiclass by construction) with no unbalanced single-power or all-power extremes built into the base structure — good for a game that wants every PC-tier character to already feel mechanically "full" without needing a separate hybrid tier.
- Surgebinding's oath-gated Ideals give Sanderson a *built-in vertical progression axis* (a character starts at Ideal 1 and can narratively earn Ideals 2-5) that Allomancy/Feruchemy fundamentally lack in the same form — Misting/Mistborn status and Ferring/full-Feruchemist status are fixed at birth and do not have an in-fiction "level up from Misting to Mistborn" pathway (Hemalurgy is the closest analogue, but it requires *stealing* from someone else via spikes, not *training/earning*, which is a fundamentally different, more morally-loaded progression fantasy). This is likely the most important complementary-design takeaway: **the grid system is stronger for horizontal build diversity (many distinct builds at a similar power budget, plus combinatorial stacking via Twinborn/Compounding/Hemalurgy); the paired-oath system is stronger for vertical build progression (a satisfying, narratively-earned power curve within a single build).** An AI-DM system wanting both axes could borrow a grid for the "which powers can this character access" question and an oath/Ideal-style gate for the "how strong is each accessed power right now" question, rather than picking only one of Sanderson's two structures.
- Hemalurgy's steal-and-graft mechanic is the one piece with no clean Surgebinding analogue at all (Shardblade-looting is the closest, but that's a fixed artifact, not a variable stat-transfer) — it is Mistborn's unique contribution to a "rule-flexibility/exception system": a formal, quantified, in-fiction-costed way for a character (or the AI DM) to grant someone an *exception* to their normal build by paying an explicit, sourced-as-lossy price, which is a mechanic Surgebinding's oath/tier design does not offer.

### Gaps
- No directly-sourced Sanderson commentary specifically contrasting *his own* design intent for Allomancy's grid vs. Surgebinding's pairing (i.e., a WoB where he explicitly says "I built Allomancy as a table and Surgebinding as a cycle because X") was found in this pass; the grid/periodic-table intent is sourced for Allomancy alone, and the cyclic-pair rationale is inferred/structural rather than quoted for Surgebinding in the prior note as well. Treat the "grid vs. cycle, and why" contrast in the Inferences above as this researcher's synthesis, not a direct authorial quote.

## Documented power-creep / balance discussion specific to Allomancy, Feruchemy, and Hemalurgy

### Takeaway
The clearest sourced balance discussion is not literary criticism of the novels but a **tabletop adaptation's explicit design problem statement**: Crafty Games' Mistborn Adventure Game team identified Mistborn-vs-Misting imbalance as a named design risk ("how do you let people play Mistborn and not have everybody else suck") and solved it via deliberately weak baseline stats for Mistborn plus a strength/weakness attribute-allocation system — this is a much more concrete, mechanically-actionable data point than any thematic "Mistborn are rare for a reason" narrative commentary found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Direct tabletop-design framing of the imbalance risk and its fix: "How do you let people play Mistborn and not have everybody else suck," was the key concern. The team balanced this by requiring Mistborn to have mediocre stats at the beginning of the game, and players also pick different attributes their characters are strong, average, and weak in to further balance each character out." — [WebSearch synthesis, citing Mistborn Adventure Game design discussion](https://fandomwire.com/10-most-overpowered-metallic-arts-in-mistborn-ranked/)
- Narrative-craft framing (not a formal balance discussion, but adjacent commentary) noting Pewter Allomancy specifically as disproportionately strong within the fiction: Pewter enhancement "increasing strength, speed, balance, and endurance, allowing one to easily lift enormous weights and deliver devastating blows... almost too overwhelming, providing users not just an advantage but also completely breaking the rules of combat and survival" (of physical Allomancy generally). — [fandomwire.com, "10 Most Overpowered Metallic Arts in Mistborn, Ranked"](https://fandomwire.com/10-most-overpowered-metallic-arts-in-mistborn-ranked/)
- In-world/craft mitigation cited for why Mistborn protagonist Vin's power doesn't feel like unearned power-creep to readers: Sanderson "limited the size of the magic to something manageable... and for the readers to follow," and additionally paces her rise in power against "several obstacles and challenges" so her escalating strength "doesn't feel forced" — i.e., a craft-level (not mechanical-level) balance technique of gating power display against narrative struggle. — [WebSearch synthesis](https://fandomwire.com/10-most-overpowered-metallic-arts-in-mistborn-ranked/)

### Inferences
- The tabletop-adaptation data point is the single most directly transferable finding in this whole research pass for an AI-DM system, because it is *already* a solved instance of exactly the problem an AI DM would face: "some players will roll/choose the all-powers hybrid class; how do you keep the single-power specialists from being useless?" Crafty Games' answer — decouple raw power-access breadth from starting competence (give the hybrid class weak stats to start, and give every class an individualized strong/average/weak attribute spread) — is a ready-made, tested pattern rather than a novel invention.
- The absence (in what this pass could retrieve) of substantial fan/critical discussion specifically alleging *narrative* power-creep in Allomancy/Feruchemy/Hemalurgy (as opposed to the tabletop-mechanical concern above) is itself worth noting as a gap rather than treated as evidence of "no such discussion exists" — see Gaps.

### Gaps

## Sanderson's own design commentary (WoB/interviews) on Hemalurgy's cost mechanic and Allomancy's grid structure

### Takeaway
Two solid, directly-quotable design-intent statements were retrieved via a secondary aggregator (winteriscoming.net, itself reporting a Sanderson interview/signing-style quote) covering both systems' origin stories; a from-scratch, fully independent Words-of-Brandon/Arcanum-database quote on *specifically* the Hemalurgy cost percentage or the precise geometric logic of the Allomantic grid was not successfully retrieved this pass (Coppermind and wob.coppermind.net were both effectively inaccessible to this session's fetch tool).

### Cited Findings
- On Allomancy's grid/chemistry-table structure: "I want something that has a feel of a periodic table, and the feel of alchemy, but that actually works. And where I arrived was eating metal." Sanderson also frames his interest in this era/aesthetic explicitly: "I like the idea of people trying to take observable fact and come up with explanations, and working their way toward actual scientific theories," citing his fondness for the late Renaissance/Industrial Revolution period where "science and folklore and mythology" collided (e.g., noting "Isaac Newton thought that alchemy worked and was trying to prove it"). — [Brandon Sanderson, quoted via winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-explains-how-he-came-up-with-the-mistborn-magic-system)
- On the push/pull half of the grid specifically: the idea reportedly traces to Sanderson watching the *Star Wars: Episode I* lightsaber duel and reading the Force as telekinesis, then asking what a *limited* version of that telekinesis would look like — arriving at Newton's third law (push has an equal opposite reaction) as the generative rule, applied to metal specifically. — [winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-explains-how-he-came-up-with-the-mistborn-magic-system)
- On Hemalurgy's design intent (cost/morality, not a numeric quote): "I wanted to have a magic system to explore for all three books" and "I wanted to have a magic system that had some moral complications to it," with the "stealing people's strength" concept credited to the influence of David Farland's *Runelords*. Also: Hemalurgy was built as the cosmere's cross-planetary "wild card" — "Hemalurgy can interact with every one of the magics" — specifically because other systems are more planet-bound. — [Brandon Sanderson, quoted via winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-breaks-down-origin-mistborn-feruchemy-hemalurgy)
- On Feruchemy's origin (already covered above, repeated here for completeness of the "commentary" question): traced to teenage insomnia and a "wish I could bank/reschedule my own sleep" idea, originally built for an unrelated unpublished novel and only later grafted onto Mistborn via a "thematic tie" to metal. — [winteriscoming.net](https://winteriscoming.net/brandon-sanderson-breaks-down-origin-mistborn-feruchemy-hemalurgy)

### Inferences
- All three systems' origins are framed by Sanderson as starting from a *personal/thematic hook first* (insomnia → Feruchemy; limited-Force telekinesis + periodic-table aesthetic → Allomancy; wanting moral complication + Runelords influence → Hemalurgy) and only *then* formalized into a rigorous rule-grid — consistent with the "Zeroth Law" (err toward the awesome/interesting core idea, then build systematic rules around it) documented in the prior Stormlight note. This suggests a repeatable design order for an AI-DM homebrew system: pick the emotionally/thematically resonant hook first, then formalize its grid/table second — not the reverse.
- The explicit statement that Hemalurgy was built to be *the* cross-system, cross-planetary "wild card" mechanic (rather than emerging incidentally) means Sanderson treats "a mechanic that can graft onto/interact with every other mechanic in the game" as a deliberate, separately-designed category of magic — directly relevant to an AI-DM "exception/override system": design the exception-granting mechanic (Hemalurgy's role) as its own explicit subsystem with its own cost logic, rather than bolting ad hoc exceptions onto each power system individually.
