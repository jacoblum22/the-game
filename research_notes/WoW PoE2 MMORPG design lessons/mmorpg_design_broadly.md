# MMORPG Design Beyond World of Warcraft: Balance and Build Diversity Across Three Distinctive Systems

## Final Fantasy XIV's Tightly Controlled Job Design

### Takeaway
FFXIV uses strict weapon restrictions (one weapon per job), regular potency adjustments, and encounter design that assumes specific job roles to achieve perfect numerical balance. This approach successfully prevents endgame exclusion but creates the inverse problem: high-level sameness in how jobs play, with community criticism that "everyone plays their job the same way."

### Cited Findings
- FFXIV restricts customization significantly compared to FFXI: "Each job has one, and only one weapon type. All abilities are learned by leveling up and completing job-specific quests." — [Destructoid: Why FFXIV's job system is more restrictive than XI's](https://www.destructoid.com/why-final-fantasy-xivs-job-system-is-more-restrictive-than-xis/)
- FFXIV employs a philosophy where "players who have progressed through similar levels remain on equal footing by default," keeping the community cohesive for group content. — [Destructoid: Why FFXIV's job system is more restrictive than XI's](https://www.destructoid.com/why-final-fantasy-xivs-job-system-is-more-restrictive-than-xis/)
- In Patch 7.5, "various actions received potency adjustments to achieve a better balance among jobs within their respective roles." The team refrained from adding a new healer job to maintain game balance between the three existing healers. — [FFXIV Job Patch 7.5 Guide](https://epiccarry.com/blogs/ffxiv-patch-7-5-jobs-tier-list-job-balance-guide/)
- FFXIV's development philosophy has emphasized "high homogenization in job design paired with highly specialized and specific raid fight design." However, many jobs increasingly feel interchangeable outside of visual presentation. — [FFXIV Weekly Update Breakdown](https://www.elyxir.gg/news/final-fantasy-xiv/ffxiv-weekly-update-breakdown-job-tuning-raid-balance-and-what-it-means-for-progression)
- Balance methods include potency adjustments, mechanical redesigns (e.g., removing Samurai's Kaiten button while making certain attacks auto-crit), resource economy changes (MP cost reductions for healers), and quality-of-life improvements (animation lock reduction). — [The Interesting Job Balancing Of Final Fantasy XIV Patch 6.1](https://kaylriene.com/2022/04/02/the-interesting-job-balancing-of-final-fantasy-xiv-patch-6-1/)

### Community Criticism and the Balance Tradeoff
- Players argue developers have "prioritized perfect balance at the expense of job identity" and that they "favor the cold, hard statistics over the spirit and culture surrounding each job." Examples include Dark Knight losing MP management, Astrologian's card system being oversimplified, Scholar losing DPS spells, and Samurai losing Kaiten. — [FFXIV Forum: Feedback for the devs on their over-fixation with job balance and homogenization](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/473154)
- The original complaint notes: "The devs need the jobs to be perfectly balanced. They don't want the nightmare scenario of AST or WHM or PLD or whoever else getting excluded from entire raid tiers because of underperformance." — [FFXIV Forum: Feedback for the devs on their over-fixation with job balance and homogenization](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/473154)
- However, world-first raid clears show "even the best players don't all use the same handful of classes. Every single job is viable for endgame content" due to these design decisions. — [FFXIV Online Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/39210/discussions/0/3195866053914644743)
- Players advocate for jobs with "low skill floors and high skill ceilings"—easy to learn but difficult to master—citing Black Mage as an ideal model that maintains both accessibility and depth. — [FFXIV Forum: Feedback for the devs on their over-fixation with job balance and homogenization](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/473154)

### Developer Philosophy on Job Balance
- Producer/Director Naoki Yoshida states: "what our biggest challenge though, is whenever we're thinking of or designing a new job, is 'what kind of weapon will this new job carry?'" The team looks at balance within role categories (melee DPS, magical ranged DPS, etc.) rather than across entire roles. — [Screen Rant: FFXIV Dawntrail Interview with Naoki Yoshida](https://screenrant.com/ffxiv-dawntrail-interview-naoki-yoshida/)
- Yoshida admits that "adding new jobs has become rather cumbersome, as maintaining the uniqueness of job mechanics while balancing their numbers has become extremely taxing, despite having not run out of ideas." — [Screen Rant: FFXIV Dawntrail Interview with Naoki Yoshida](https://screenrant.com/ffxiv-dawntrail-interview-naoki-yoshida/)
- FFXIV's stated goal is "not merely fewer buttons, but stronger job individuality — jobs that solve encounters in meaningfully distinct ways rather than through nearly identical rotational structures." This reflects developer awareness of the homogenization problem and intention to address it. — [FFXIV Weekly Update Breakdown](https://www.elyxir.gg/news/final-fantasy-xiv/ffxiv-weekly-update-breakdown-job-tuning-raid-balance-and-what-it-means-for-progression)

### Inferences
- FFXIV's tight control system achieves its primary goal: no job is excluded from endgame, and balance is verifiable through data. However, this same mechanism creates mechanical homogenization, where the distinctiveness of how a job *feels* declines over time.
- The tradeoff is structural: encounter design that assumes specific job roles (tank/healer/DPS) and equal damage per role means jobs within that role must converge. To have both unique rotations AND perfect balance across jobs simultaneously within this constraint would require either far more complex encounter design or acceptance of some jobs being objectively better for certain fights.
- The weapon restriction is foundational to this philosophy—it's the single most direct lever FFXIV uses to ensure jobs don't bleed into each other's design space.

### Gaps
- Specific metrics on how frequently FFXIV does major balance patches vs. minor tuning (how often do large-scale job redesigns occur vs. potency tweaks). The research found that patches are scheduled far in advance but not the exact frequency of major vs. minor adjustments.
- Player retention data or player sentiment metrics tracking whether the homogenization criticism has caused subscription changes or class popularity shifts over time.

## Guild Wars 2's Weapon-and-Trait-Based Openness

### Takeaway
GW2 uses weapon swapping (two active weapon sets, each with 5 unique skills) and a trait system (with elite specializations adding new traits and weapon access) to enable far broader build diversity than FFXIV. Balance is managed incrementally through a "living document" philosophy, and the game explicitly avoids a rigid trinity, letting all classes defend themselves. The tradeoff: meta convergence over time, and ongoing criticism that balance passes sometimes reduce build variety despite intentions.

### Cited Findings
- GW2's weapon system makes "every weapon useful on every profession that can equip said weapon. The weapon you're currently wielding is a major determining factor for how your character will play, with each weapon coming with its own set of skills." — [Guild Wars 2 Wiki: Weapon](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Weapon)
- Weapon skills are predetermined: "The main-hand weapon determines the first three weapon skills, the off-hand weapon determines the last two weapon skills, and two-handed weapons determine all weapon skills." — [Guild Wars 2 Wiki: Skill bar](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Skill_bar)
- Weapon swapping mechanics allow "all professions have the ability to switch between two weapon sets while in combat, with the exception of elementalist and engineer who can only weapon swap when out of combat." — [Guild Wars 2 Wiki: Weapon swap](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Weapon_swap)
- Elite specializations "replaced trait lines and introduce a new system to expand professions' progression, adding a full new set of traits and unlocking access to previously unavailable weapons and skill types." — [Guild Wars 2 Build diversity discussion](https://en-forum.guildwars2.com/topic/46061-weapon-and-build-diversity/)
- Weapon swap traits create mechanical incentives: "Brutality (Devastation) — Gain quickness when you swap weapons" and "Quick Draw (Skirmishing) — When swapping weapons, gain quickness and the next ranger weapon skill you use will recharge faster." — [Guild Wars 2 Wiki: Weapon swap](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Weapon_swap)

### Balance Philosophy and Process
- GW2's balance team has published a "living document" philosophy with goals to make the game "enjoyable for as many players as possible through 'a fluid and fast-paced combat experience that allows players to express their mastery of mechanics'." — [MMORPG.com: Guild Wars 2 Releases Profession Balance Change Philosophy Policy](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/guild-wars-2-releases-profession-balance-change-philosophy-policy-after-announce-2000126507)
- Key principles include "encouraging build diversity and allowing players to express their mechanics mastery" and continuing to "highlight underutilized skills with real potential to increase build versatility, flexibility, and variety." — [Massively Overpowered: Guild Wars 2 makes public principles guiding balance philosophy](https://massivelyop.com/2022/10/29/guild-wars-2-makes-public-the-principles-guiding-its-current-balance-philosophy/)
- The balance philosophy is adaptive: "ArenaNet has adopted a balance change philosophy with smaller overall scope in changes some months and major balance overhauls less frequently." The team acknowledges that "not everything in the guidelines align with the reality of the game, but promises that changes will come incrementally." — [MMORPG.com: Guild Wars 2 Releases Profession Balance Change Philosophy Policy](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/guild-wars-2-releases-profession-balance-change-philosophy-policy-after-announce-2000126507)

### Build Diversity Concerns
- With recent balance changes, "many specs have acquired similar traits and mechanics." However, "while there is a meta in GW2 like other games, there is still plenty of room for large variations in your build." — [Guild Wars 2 Forums: Build diversity](https://en-forum.guildwars2.com/discussion/30945/build-diversity)
- Community forums discuss "The Decline of Build Diversity" in GW2, suggesting that despite design intent, meta convergence is an ongoing issue. — [MMORPG.com Forums: Guild Wars 2 - The Decline of Build Diversity](https://forums.mmorpg.com/discussion/459929/guild-wars-2-the-decline-of-build-diversity-mmorpg-com)
- Recent balance efforts include "changes to classic lines and some mild buffs to non-elite base weapons, with the expectation that additional elite spec lines will help create more build diversity." — [Guild Wars 2 Forums: Build and spec variety](https://en-forum.guildwars2.com/topic/168397-build-and-spec-variety/)

### Combat System Design (Non-Trinity)
- GW2's dodge/block/attack combat system "breaks the trinity by giving every player the simple means to defend themselves," contrasting with traditional tank/healer/DPS roles. — [Massively Overpowered: Massively Overthinking: Thoughts on the holy trinity in MMOs](https://massivelyop.com/2019/03/28/massively-overthinking-thoughts-on-the-holy-trinity-in-mmos/)

### Inferences
- GW2's weapon swapping and trait system create a much larger build design space than FFXIV. A player can meaningfully change their playstyle by switching weapons or building different trait combinations.
- The "living document" balance philosophy reflects conscious choice to accept some meta convergence over time rather than pursuing perfect balance. This preserves designer flexibility and allows small buffs to underperforming specs without constant heavy-handed redesigns.
- The non-trinity system removes a fundamental source of balance rigidity: since any class can defend itself, encounters don't *require* specific roles, so the balance burden is lighter than in traditional trinity-based games.
- However, GW2 still faces the build-convergence problem that all games do: some builds are measurably better, and dedicated players will optimize toward them, reducing perceived build diversity despite mechanical availability.

### Gaps
- Specific data on how often GW2 patches compared to FFXIV (e.g., weekly, monthly, per-expansion). The research found the philosophy but not the patch schedule frequency.
- Metrics on the *actual* diversity of builds used in endgame content (raids, fractals, PvP) versus the *available* diversity. What percentage of players stick to the meta build?

## EVE Online's Time-Based Skill Training and Asymmetric Build Diversity

### Takeaway
EVE Online uses a unique real-time skill training system (training happens offline, with skills taking from days to months to train to level 5) to create build diversity through *specialization over time* rather than simultaneous choice. This removes the "everyone can have everything" pressure that affects other MMOs. The game balances dozens of ship types and thousands of fitting combinations through gradual attribute scaling and a deep item economy rather than hard job categories.

### Cited Findings
- EVE Online features a unique skill system where "you can train skills in real time, even when logged off." Skills take time to train based on "attributes, the skill's training time multiplier, and the level to which you are training the skill." — [EVE Online: Skill Training Guide](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Skills_and_learning)
- The system supports massive build diversity: "training every skill in the game to the maximum level would take around two decades of continuous training. With over 400 different options, there are premade Skill Plans available for all four Academy pathways: Explorer, Enforcer, Industrialist, and Soldier of Fortune." — [EVE Online Academy: Everyone Starts Somewhere](https://www.eveonline.com/eve-academy/everyone-starts-somewhere)
- Skills are cumulative and never lost: "once you have invested time (skill points) into a skill you will never lose them (unless you choose to extract them using a Skill Extractor)." — [EVE Online Support: Skill Plans and How They Work](https://support.eveonline.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406388028178-Skill-Plans-and-How-They-Work)
- Balance mechanisms include progressive skill levels (every skill trains to level 5), prerequisite chains that gate advanced skills, and skill extractors that allow respeccing at a cost. — [EVE Online Support: Skill Plans and How They Work](https://support.eveonline.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406388028178-Skill-Plans-and-How-They-Work)

### Distinctive Design Philosophy
- EVE Online's approach differs fundamentally from level-based progression: rather than all players reaching the same maximum level and then diverging by gear, players *begin* with divergent training paths from day one. A character trained for PvP combat cannot immediately switch to industrial gameplay—the character is locked into their training choices for months.
- The combination of real-time training, 400+ skills, and cumulative progression creates a scenario where build diversity emerges naturally: at any given moment, players have different subsets of skills trained, forcing specialization.
- The player-driven economy (ships, modules, and damage types are balanced against each other through supply/demand and iterative balance patches) provides a second-order balancing mechanism that doesn't exist in theme-park MMOs.

### Inferences
- EVE Online's real-time training system solves the "everyone converges on the optimal build" problem by making it genuinely infeasible for one character to train every combat role at maximum level in reasonable time. Specialization is baked into the progression system, not a player choice.
- This approach scales to handle very large build spaces (400+ skills across dozens of ship types) because players self-select into niches due to training time, not because the developer has to hand-balance everything.
- The cost of respeccing (through skill extractors) preserves long-term player investment in their specialization, further encouraging diversity.

### Gaps
- Specific data on whether EVE Online's approach actually produces *measurably* more build diversity in live play than FFXIV or GW2. Does the real-time training system result in a broader distribution of character builds, or does the meta still converge despite mechanical encouragement toward diversity?
- Analysis of how EVE Online's balance process differs from theme-park MMOs (patch frequency, developer philosophy, response to community feedback).
- Discussion of whether EVE Online's system creates accessibility issues for new players or returning players who want to switch roles.

## Cross-Game Comparison: Design Tradeoffs for AI-Driven Systems

### Cited Findings on Tight vs. Loose Systems
- Tight systems involve "another human broadly signals intent, capabilities and internal mental state." For MMOs, "a player dresses as a high level healer and stands in a spot where adhoc groups meet up, and there's a good chance you know what they'll do if you ask them to go adventuring together." — [Lostgarden: Building Tight Game Systems of Cause and Effect](https://lostgarden.com/2012/07/01/building-tight-game-systems-of-cause-and-effect/)
- Loose systems involve "a large number of options that must be considered," as in Go with "dozens of potential moves and hundreds of secondary moves." — [Lostgarden: Building Tight Game Systems of Cause and Effect](https://lostgarden.com/2012/07/01/building-tight-game-systems-of-cause-and-effect/)
- Auto-attack vs. rotation-based design: "Auto attack has been criticized as lazy game design that hasn't factored both skill and aesthetics into the equation, while rotation-based gameplay represents an opposite extreme, with many arguing the solution lies somewhere in between." — [MMORPG Design Guide](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/game-balance/)
- Trinity-based systems (Tank/Healer/DPS) "provide an entry point for players to understand their role in party dynamics," but non-trinity designs like GW2 "breaks the trinity by giving every player the simple means to defend themselves." — [Massively Overpowered: Massively Overthinking: Thoughts on the holy trinity in MMOs](https://massivelyop.com/2019/03/28/massively-overthinking-thoughts-on-the-holy-trinity-in-mmos/)

### Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | FFXIV | GW2 | EVE Online |
|-----------|-------|-----|-----------|
| **Progression Model** | Level-based (1-90) | Level-based (1-80) | Skill-based (real-time training, cumulative) |
| **Build Customization** | Minimal (one weapon per job) | High (weapon swap, traits, specializations) | Extreme (400+ skills, months to train all paths) |
| **Balance Approach** | Tight (perfect role parity) | Loose (incremental, living document) | Emergent (item economy + gradual patches) |
| **Combat Role System** | Strict Trinity (Tank/Healer/DPS) | Non-Trinity (self-defense for all) | None (ship types fill roles through fitting) |
| **Patch Frequency** | Tied to raid tiers | Incremental, variable cadence | Ongoing, based on player-driven economy |
| **Primary Balance Lever** | Job abilities & potency | Weapon skills & traits | Item attributes & player-driven markets |
| **Time to Respec** | Instant (free) | Instant (free) | Months (skill extractor cost or train new skills) |
| **Community Perception of Build Diversity** | Low (criticized for sameness) | Medium (meta convergence despite intent) | High (specialization forced by training time) |

### Inferences for AI-Driven Systems
- **Tight balance (FFXIV model)** works well for AI-driven systems when the AI needs to enforce strict rules: encounters can be designed assuming specific job roles, loot can be tuned precisely, and the AI can make high-confidence predictions about player power.
- **Loose balance (GW2 model)** requires more sophisticated AI: the system must track and respond to emergent builds the designer didn't explicitly anticipate. However, it creates more player agency and perceived richness.
- **Emergent systems (EVE model)** require AI that can manage second-order effects: the AI doesn't directly balance builds; instead, it influences the economy and player incentives such that balance emerges. This scales to larger build spaces but is harder to predict and debug.
- **Real-time progression (EVE)** reduces the "everyone converges on the meta" problem by making specialization economically rational (months of training committed). For an AI-driven RPG, this could mean training time or resource commitment creates natural niches without requiring the AI to enforce them.

## Key Findings for Custom AI-Driven RPG Design

### Design Philosophy Tradeoff
The core tension is between **perfect, verifiable balance** (FFXIV) and **player-perceived richness and diversity** (GW2, EVE). Perfect balance often requires architectural constraints (weapon restrictions, role categories, tight encounter design) that produce mechanical homogenization. Richer build spaces require the system to accept that some builds will be better than others and to iterate based on emergent gameplay.

### Implications for Scale
- **FFXIV's approach scales linearly**: more jobs require more encounter redesigns and more potency tuning. The weapon restriction is a scaling mechanism—it reduces permutations the system must balance.
- **GW2's approach scales quadratically**: more weapons, more traits, more elite specs means exponentially more combinations. The team accepts some convergence and patches incrementally.
- **EVE's approach scales logarithmically** relative to the total skill space: real-time training and long specialization windows mean many builds are viable at any moment because players haven't trained them all. The item economy provides second-order balancing.

### Live Patching and Community Perception
- FFXIV patches by raid tier (tied to content releases), making balance predictable but potentially inflexible.
- GW2 patches incrementally with a stated philosophy to highlight underutilized skills, accepting that balance is never perfect.
- EVE patches based on data and player-driven economy signals, often with longer iteration cycles.

For an AI-driven system, the patch process itself becomes a design lever: frequent small patches feel responsive; infrequent large patches feel more stable but potentially stale. Community acceptance depends on whether the AI is perceived as *listening* to feedback and iterating, even if total balance doesn't improve.

### Specific Gaps in Cross-Game Research
- No study comparing *actual* build diversity (measured as variance in character stats/loadouts in live play) across these three games. Claims about FFXIV's homogenization are widespread but not quantified against GW2 or EVE.
- No GDC talks specifically comparing the design philosophies of these three games or proposing the tight-vs.-loose framework as a deliberate spectrum.
- Limited discussion of how these games handle balance in PvE vs. PvP separately. GW2 and EVE have strong PvP components that may affect balance decisions differently than FFXIV's primarily PvE endgame.
