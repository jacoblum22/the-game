## 2. The philosophy (the most important part)

The project started from a ruleset that treated the language model as an unreliable component to be constrained
at every step:
- truth-gated narration;
- a second model checking every claim;
- mute NPCs;
- "the DM never chooses what is true";
- everything broken into pieces so it could be verified;
- rules delivered to the model as retrievable cards.

Two days of playtests and owner direction moved it to the stance below. It is now **constitution Principle
VIII**, which wins over older text that conflicts with it, and is also in **00-architecture** (DL-00-06).

### 2.1 Design principles
1. **Code owns the spine; the model owns language and judgment.**
   - The spine is state, dice, rules arithmetic, secrets and clue cases, and anything that must be exact.
   - **Code must not author narrative content that models then have to treat as true.** That covers prose, NPC
     lines, refusals stored as facts, labels used as penalties, and goals restated as agreements. Round 6 found
     that this was the most common hidden cause of bad play.
2. **Check by measured precision, and only what breaks the game.**
   - A check blocks only if, when it fires, it is right most of the time.
   - Low-precision checks (for example, the "was the question answered?" word overlap, about 6%) do more harm
     than good: each false alarm costs a model call and corrupts the prose.
   - Everything else is trusted, logged, or left for the player to correct.
3. **Truth only when it matters.**
   - What NPCs say is *testimony*: stored as words plus speaker, never as world fact.
   - It is settled into truth only when the player *acts on a specific claim*: travels to the place it names, uses
     it in a roll, or makes it a mechanical input.
   - A speaker's refusal or opinion is never a fact.
4. **Whole over parts.**
   - Models read whole messages and write whole passages, the way a person would.
   - Break things down only where code must decide or remember exactly: typed goals, state, clues.
   - Atomisation caused choppy dialogue, blind repairs, false "unanswered" checks, and rolls on the method instead
     of the goal.
5. **Remove friction before adding procedure.** When something feels clunky, first look for machinery to delete.
   The owner's warning is "don't get too in the weeds".
6. **Better context, not more rules.**
   - The 500k-word ruleset is now mostly the *specification the code implements*.
   - Each model call should get the situation (conversation, the relevant people with pronouns and bonds), plus
     short guidance for the judgment it actually makes.
   - Today, 70–87% of most roles' input is rule text, and the rest is the wrong context (I4).
7. **Diagnose from evidence.** See §3. Never name a cause from the surface.
8. **The player is part of error correction.** `/fix` (plus plain-words complaints) is the safety net that lets the
   system be loose. It has to be genuinely capable; F6 makes it an investigator.

**Keep the spine:** structured state, typed goals (`change {kind, what, whom}`), clue cases and casting, dice and
rubric, the event-sourced store, and the feedback loop. **Trust is model-dependent:** validate on the target model
(Gemini Flash) once the owner allows it.

### 2.2 Owner working preferences (from memory and conversation)
- **Fixes aren't sacred.** Prefer structural redesigns that retire patches. Every proposal says what it
  supersedes.
- **Look at things holistically** and challenge atomisation.
- **Implement agreed fixes before testing again**, and keep a test round focused on the fixes just made.
- **Don't get into the weeds.** Fine detail that doesn't matter to play shouldn't be tracked. Fine position, for
  example, is atmosphere; only place and range band matter.
- **The owner approves rulings and principle changes.** Quote the owner in DL entries. Present 2–3 options with a
  recommendation for real decisions. Don't ask about things with a sensible default.
- **Explain plainly and concisely.** The owner often follows along from a phone via Remote Control. Use
  `SendUserFile` for documents.
- **Haiku for engine tests; Sonnet for the player persona.** No Gemini until told. Live runs cost subscription
  quota, so keep them bounded.

## 3. The working loop (how to find and fix problems)

### 3.1 The lesson that shapes the loop
In round 5–6, about 10 of 13 first-guess causes were wrong or incomplete. Every time the actual inputs were
traced, the real cause showed up on the first proper look. **The orchestrator's surface guess is wasted time,
so skip it.** Do not state a cause, or propose a fix, until the evidence has been traced.

Examples of guesses versus traced causes:

| Symptom | Guess | Traced cause |
|---|---|---|
| NPC catchphrase ×5 | needs a freshness mechanism | One turn stitched together from three *blind* repair calls (they never saw the draft), triggered by a 6%-precision "answered?" check |
| Repeated scenery | model copying itself | The place's seeded look line was in every narrator packet |
| NPCs "a wall" | willingness too strict | Willingness said "willing" 19/19. The cause was code-written answer lines plus a narrator prompt line ("write around names or kinship") |
| Held foe keeps hitting | rules gap | The rules are fine. The engine stored the hold as *text*, not a condition, and skipped break-free |
| Reader rolled the grip, not the demand | model too small | Sonnet split the same 7/8 messages. The prompt, `normalise` and the golden policy all *required* the split |

### 3.2 The loop
1. **Playtest.**
   - Run 2–3 personas on Haiku, usually talker and fighter, plus one more when breadth is needed (explorer,
     newcomer or breaker):
     ```
     py -3.12 evals/playtest.py --engine claude --player claude --engine-model haiku --persona <p> --turns 15 --quick-setup
     ```
   - Transcripts and findings go to `evals/playtests/`, saves to `saves/`.
   - Also run `evals/longhorizon.py --mode scripted` (offline, free) for engine errors and drift.
2. **Collect symptoms.** Read the `.findings.jsonl` player notes and stats. List the symptoms. **Do not diagnose
   yet.** Also check what the targets of the last fix round did, and write that down.
3. **Trace with parallel read-only investigators.** Spawn 3–4 Opus subagents, split by area: narration and text,
   NPCs and dialogue, mechanics and combat, feedback, context. Each gets:
   - **the method:** for every symptom, find the instances; show what the player saw, *every model call's packet
     sections and response*, the turn record, and the code path; separate model behaviour from what our
     scaffolding fed the model or did to its output; quantify frequency across all runs;
   - **the evidence locations** (§7);
   - **the proposed structural fixes to test against:** removes the cause, partly, or misses it, and what's needed
     instead;
   - **constraints:** read-only, a report file in `research_notes/…/roundN-investigation/`, a final reply of at
     most 250 words.
4. **Synthesise.**
   - Put the reports side by side and look for **cross-cutting causes**, one cause behind several symptoms.
   - Check against the **known anti-pattern catalogue** (§3.3).
   - Write `ROUNDn-ANALYSIS.md` / `ROUNDn-PLAN.md`: each symptom with its traced cause and evidence; the
     cross-cutting causes; and one structural fix per real problem, with what it retires. Minor bugs are listed as
     patches.
5. **Owner review.** Present the plan in plain words. Wait for approval of anything that changes a principle or
   rules. The owner often pushes back usefully; take that seriously and re-trace if needed.
6. **Rules wave.** One `rules-worker` agent per wave encodes the approved changes:
   - revisions tagged `[revision: R2-x]`;
   - DL entries quoting the owner;
   - re-assembled appendix, anchors, `cards.json` compiled **last**, validator 0/0;
   - a report with an **engine checklist per spec**.

   **Rules come before the engine:** the engine cites rule ids.
7. **Engine specs.** One spec-kit feature per structural fix:
   - specify → plan → tasks → analyze → implement; skip clarify and record assumptions;
   - each in its own worktree, with explicit **file lanes**;
   - at most 2 in parallel, because of usage limits and merge conflicts.

   Each brief includes:
   - `git reset --hard milestone-1-playable` first;
   - the evidence to test against;
   - real gates (tests from real saves; live runs on Haiku with a turn budget);
   - "report real numbers, don't fake a pass";
   - what to retire.
8. **Merge and gate.** See §5. Then go back to step 1.

### 3.3 Anti-pattern catalogue (check for these every round)
| Anti-pattern | Seen as |
|---|---|
| **Code authoring narrative truth** | Refusals stored as facts; scene labels ("straining", "on guard") used as snags; complaint text pinned as facts; the player's goal restated as an NPC agreement; code-written answer lines; found clues re-delivered; internal fields printed raw (the Reader's `method` in "Then:") |
| **Atomisation** | Sentence-array narration; act-by-act beats; per-clause "answered" bookkeeping; per-fact levers; complaints settled one at a time |
| **Over-triggering rules** | Settlement on 64% of turns; "every hindering fact is a snag"; "every part must be answered"; the answer ladder's Oracle on every ordinary question (before S1) |
| **Low-precision checks that block** | The answered-question overlap (6%); the old full-extraction Checker; pronoun checks without a certain subject |
| **Blind model calls** | Repairs that don't see the draft; testimony conversion without pronouns; roles whose prompts claim conversation they don't get |
| **Wrong context** | Rule-text bloat; id-sorted situation cards; missing pronouns and bonds for absent people; a lead line in every packet |
| **Mechanics stored as text** | The hold as words; a coin never deducted; a silent take-back |
| **Mandatory texture** | Activities, looks and details pushed as must-name, so the narrator repeats them |
| **Test and measurement traps** | Prompts tuned on the scoring set (always keep a held-out set, labelled by the rule *before* seeing answers); a golden-set policy that encodes the anti-pattern you are fixing (B5); harness artefacts (the persona sees the last 9,000 characters, so an old hint looked repeated) |

## 5. Merge procedure (the orchestrator does this; agents commit on their worktree branches)

```bash
git merge --no-ff --no-commit <branch>
py -3.12 evals/orchestration/resolve_rules_merge.py      # resolves fragments, ledger rows, generated files; prints MANUAL: for the rest
# resolve any MANUAL: code conflicts by hand (typical: role lists: keep both new roles, drop retired ones; CONVENTIONS sections: keep both)
bash evals/orchestration/regen_and_gate.sh               # assemble, anchors, cards (last), validator, re-record replay, full pytest
git commit -m "Merge … (gate numbers)\n\nCo-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>"
```

**If tests fail after a merge of two specs:**
1. `git checkout -b integrate-x-y` and commit there.
2. Spawn a reconcile agent on that branch.
3. Fast-forward `milestone-1-playable` when it is green.

Update `OVERNIGHT-LOG.md` after each merge.

### The fixes
- **F1. Holistic narration:**
  - one passage, plus a passage-level side list;
  - only precise blocking checks, run on the whole passage;
  - at most one revision, which sees the whole draft;
  - a quote-mark check;
  - retire the sentence array, the per-sentence repair, coverage adds and the answered check.
- **F2. Goal-first Reader:**
  - goals per target (what, toward whom, how) plus out-of-character flags;
  - split only on different goals;
  - change the prompt, `normalise` *and* golden policy B5 (relabel blind, by rule);
  - OFFER titles and "Then:" come from the typed goal;
  - test across every goal and method shape:
    - force in service of words;
    - movement in service of an act;
    - an object as the method;
    - deception;
    - stealth;
    - two real goals;
    - conversation only;
    - mixed messages.
- **F3. Context per role:**
  - drop the core and the id-sorted cards; give each role a short guidance digest (about −73% input);
  - give conversation to Director, World and Checker;
  - include pronouns and bonds for anyone named;
  - show the lead line only when it is relevant;
  - remove "write around names or kinship".
- **F4. Code stops authoring narrative truth:**
  - testimony stored as words, settled only when the player acts on a specific claim, never from a refusal or
    opinion, with pronouns in scope;
  - scene labels never levers or snags (01.6.8: mechanical hindrances only);
  - answer lines only when they carry a real fact; failed lookups write nothing; no re-delivered clues;
  - `change.py` summaries go to RESULT only;
  - complaint text is never a fact.
- **F5. Mechanics as state:**
  - a hold is a condition, and `foe_beat` runs break-free first;
  - payments deduct coin;
  - every restore prints "Now:".
- **F6. `/fix` as an investigator on Sonnet:**
  - read-only tools: turn record, entity state and provenance, transcript search, recompute a frame, the
    ledger, testimony versus record;
  - a bounded investigation of about 3–6 steps;
  - a broad set of proposed corrections, validated by code;
  - complaints handled together;
  - a reply of "found → did → Now:".
- **F7. Small items:**
  - the quest-giver's reaction roll respects his seeded stance;
  - the harness shows the persona only new output.
