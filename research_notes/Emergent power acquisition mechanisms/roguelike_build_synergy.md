# Roguelike/Roguelite Build-Synergy Systems: Encoding Emergent Combinations

## Hades: the Duo Boon system — comprehensive table or sparse curated subset?

### Takeaway
Hades' Duo Boons are a small, hand-curated set of named combinations (roughly 30, by the most detailed data source found) layered on top of ~8 gods' worth of independently-authored regular boons, gated by simple prerequisite rules ("own at least one specific boon from each of two gods"), not a comprehensive pairwise table generated from the full combinatorial space. This is the "sparse curated table over an independent effect pool" model referenced in the research objective.

### Cited Findings
- Duo Boons are boons that combine the powers of two different gods for a unique effect, and are drawn from the original Hades game's roster of deities — Zeus, Poseidon, Athena, Aphrodite, Ares, Artemis, Dionysus, and Demeter (8 gods) — per multiple guide sites. — [CBR: Every Hades Duo Boon & How to Get Them](https://www.cbr.com/hades-duo-boon-guide/)
- A data-extraction tool built directly from the game's boon data lists roughly 30 distinct duo boons across these god pairings (exact count somewhat ambiguous in the rendered chart, but in that range rather than in the hundreds a full pairwise table over 8 gods would require — C(8,2) = 28 possible god-pairs, and each pair typically has only 1 duo boon, occasionally more). — [Hades Duo Boons data tool](https://orlp.github.io/hades-boons/duo_boons.html)
- The prerequisite rule is: "you must possess boons from two specific gods" — typically one specific named boon from each of the two gods involved — for the Duo Boon to become available as a pickup option. Most Duo Boons require exactly one prerequisite boon from each god; one boon (Vengeful Mood) is a documented exception requiring three prerequisite boons instead of two. — [CBR: Every Hades Duo Boon & How to Get Them](https://www.cbr.com/hades-duo-boon-guide/)
- Boons occupy exclusive ability slots (Attack/"Strike", Special/"Flourish", Cast/"Shot", Dash, and Call/Aid); the prerequisite boons for a given Duo Boon are drawn from specific slot categories rather than requiring an exact slot match between the two gods (i.e., it is not simply "any Attack boon from God A + any Special boon from God B" as a generic rule — each Duo Boon names its own specific prerequisite boons). — [Hades Duo Boons data tool](https://orlp.github.io/hades-boons/duo_boons.html); [TheGamer: Hades Duo Boons List](https://www.thegamer.com/hades-duo-boons-list-how-to-get/)
- There are also explicit hand-authored incompatibility rules layered on top of the prerequisite system — e.g., certain boon pairs are flagged as mutually exclusive (the data tool cites "Cold Embrace and Crystal Clarity can't be combined" as an example) — showing the designers hand-tune both what unlocks together and what is blocked from co-occurring. — [Hades Duo Boons data tool](https://orlp.github.io/hades-boons/duo_boons.html)
- Hades II (the sequel) uses the same architecture at a larger scale: 37 Duo Boons across 9 gods (Aphrodite, Apollo, Ares, Demeter, Hephaestus, Hera, Hestia, Poseidon, Zeus), again a curated subset rather than a full pairwise table (C(9,2) = 36 possible pairs, roughly matching the 37 duo boon count — suggesting Hades II moved closer to ~1 duo boon per possible god-pair, but this is still a named/curated entry per pair, not an emergent rules-composition system). — [RogueRanker: Hades 2 Duo Boons: All 37 and the Best Combos](https://rogueranker.com/hades-2-duo-boons/)

### Inferences
- The near-1:1 ratio of god-pairs to Duo Boons in Hades II (36 possible pairs vs. 37 duo boons) suggests Supergiant's design process is "author exactly one flagship combo per god-pair," which is tractable because the number of *god* pairings (not item/boon pairings) stays small even as the roster of individual boons grows — the combinatorial explosion is avoided by keying the special-case table off gods (a small, fixed set) rather than off individual boons (a large, growing set).
- This means Hades' "emergent combination" system is not emergent in the strict sense used in the research question (interactions falling out of shared rules) — it's a sparse, entirely hand-authored named-combination layer, with the "emergence" being in variety of *when* a Duo Boon becomes available (governed by run RNG and player choice of which boons to pick up) rather than in *what* the combination does.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm an exact, undisputed total count of Duo Boons in Hades I from a single authoritative source (estimates ranged from ~10 shown in a single article to ~30 in the data-tool listing); the Hades Fandom wiki page itself could not be fetched directly (429/402 errors on repeated attempts) so the canonical wiki table was not directly verified.
- No developer interview/GDC talk from Supergiant specifically explaining the design rationale or authoring workflow behind Duo Boon selection was found in this pass.
- Could not confirm whether Duo Boon prerequisite checks are keyed by boon slot, by exact boon identity, or by an underlying trait/tag system in the actual game code (no source with decompiled/datamined boon-prerequisite logic was found beyond the community data tool's rendered summary).

## Slay the Spire: emergent synergy from independent rules vs. explicit combination table

### Takeaway
Slay the Spire's card/relic synergies are emergent in the strict sense: cards and relics are independently-authored objects that each subscribe to a small, shared set of combat event hooks (e.g., "on card played," "on damage dealt," "on turn start"), and synergy arises when multiple independently-written effects key off the same trigger or shared resource — there is no explicit combination lookup table.

### Cited Findings
- Community "synergy" documentation explicitly frames Slay the Spire's synergies as compositional: "A synergy page groups resources around a shared mechanic, then shows how that mechanic appears across cards, relics, potions, and build directions. A synergy is two or more pieces that make each other better, with the cleanest synergies having one engine piece (that repeats, like extra energy or draw) and one payoff piece (that turns repetition into kills or value)." — [SpireGenius: Slay the Spire 2 Synergies](https://spiregenius.com/synergies/)
- Design commentary characterizes the combat system itself as simple and fixed ("the same five or six rules every turn"), with the real design surface being the card/relic pool that plugs into that fixed rules engine: "relics change the rules underneath every fight for the whole run... The deck-building game requires players to develop a strategy based on cards obtained in synergy with the relics they collect." — [Summer Engine: How to Make a Deckbuilder Like Slay the Spire](https://www.summerengine.com/blog/make-a-deckbuilder-like-slay-the-spire)
- Mega Crit's lead designer Anthony Giovannetti gave a GDC 2019 talk, "Slay the Spire: Metrics Driven Design and Balance," describing how the team used play-metrics/telemetry (rather than a hand-designed combination catalog) to observe which emergent card/relic combinations were over- or under-performing and rebalance from there — implying the combination space was discovered through data, not pre-specified. — [GDC Vault: 'Slay the Spire': Metrics Driven Design and Balance](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025731/-Slay-the-Spire-Metrics)
- The modding ecosystem (BaseMod, the most widely used Slay the Spire modding framework) exposes a shared set of event hooks that mirror the base game's own callback architecture, including `OnCardUse` (a card is played), `OnPlayerDamaged`, `OnPowersModified` (Powers/buffs applied, modified, or removed), `PostBattle`, `PostDraw`, `PostExhaust`, and `RelicGet`. Cards, relics, and Powers each independently subscribe to whichever hooks are relevant to their effect, and combinations emerge because unrelated objects can react to the same event without knowing about each other. — [BaseMod Wiki: Hooks](https://github.com/daviscook477/BaseMod/wiki/Hooks)

### Inferences
- Slay the Spire is the clean contrast case the research objective anticipated: it is architecture (b) — a small set of shared hooks/pipelines (turn start, card played, damage dealt, power applied) that many independently-authored cards/relics plug into — rather than architecture (a) (Hades' curated named-pair table). No "if card X and relic Y, then special effect Z" table exists; two objects that both key off "whenever you play an Attack" will compose automatically the moment both are in play, which is exactly the "emergent from independent rules" pattern named in the research question.
- Because BaseMod's hooks are built to intercept and replicate the base game's own callback points (this is how a mod overlay framework can add new cards that behave like native ones), the hook list found here is a reasonable proxy for the actual `AbstractCard`/`AbstractRelic`/`AbstractPlayer` callback methods in Mega Crit's own engine, though this equivalence is inferred rather than confirmed by an official source.

### Gaps
- No official Mega Crit developer statement (interview, blog post, or the GDC talk's actual slide content — only a description of the talk's existence was found, not a transcript) explicitly confirms "cards and relics are implemented as independent hook subscribers" in those terms; this is inferred from third-party modding documentation, not primary developer commentary.
- Could not access the full GDC talk content (only metadata/description), so specific postmortem commentary on synergy design philosophy from Mega Crit is not directly quoted here.

## Binding of Isaac: thousands of item synergies — hand-authored per-pair, or emergent from shared tags/pipelines?

### Takeaway
Isaac uses a hybrid: the great majority of item interactions emerge for free from a shared "stat cache" pipeline that all passive items feed into (additive/multiplicative stat modifiers recompute automatically whenever any item is picked up), but a smaller category of mutually-exclusive "tear replacer" items (Brimstone, Mom's Knife, Technology, Ludovico Technique, etc.) require explicit, hand-authored priority rules per conflicting pair, because only one can visually/mechanically own the "what does the attack look like" slot at a time.

### Cited Findings
- The game exposes a `MC_EVALUATE_CACHE` callback: "many stats in Isaac are handled using a cache system... The callback runs right after a stat evaluation happens, providing the relevant player and a CacheFlag variable indicating which stat has updated." Items add stat modifiers that get folded into this shared recalculation rather than each item needing to know about every other item. — [Binding of Isaac: Rebirth Lua API Docs, ModCallbacks](https://wofsauge.github.io/IsaacDocs/rep/enums/ModCallbacks.html); [Isaac Blueprints: Stats cache tutorial](https://isaacblueprints.com/tutorials/basics/stats_cache/)
- The API exposes `AddCacheFlags` (mark which stat categories need recalculating) and `EvaluateItems` (trigger the actual recalculation), and states "the stats for vanilla items and effects are applied before the callback is fired for any modded effects" — confirming stat-modifying items are resolved through a single shared evaluation pass rather than pairwise logic. — [Isaac Blueprints: Adding stats tutorial](https://isaacblueprints.com/tutorials/repentogon/adding_stats/)
- Item "tags" exist in the game data (e.g. the `tearsup` tag marking all items that increase tear rate) and are used at minimum for unlock-tracking purposes (e.g., contributing toward the "Lachryphagy" trinket unlock condition tied to collecting enough tear-rate-up items in a run). — [Binding of Isaac: Rebirth Wiki, Item Tags tearsup](https://bindingofisaacrebirth.wiki.gg/wiki/Item_Tags_tearsup)
- For the subset of items that fully replace how Isaac's attack behaves (tear-replacer items like Brimstone, Mom's Knife, Technology, Haemolacria, The Ludovico Technique), the wiki documents an explicit, hand-specified priority order for every conflicting pair rather than a general rule: "Brimstone + Mom's Knife: Mom's Knife has priority... Brimstone + Haemolacria: Haemolacria has priority... Technology + Brimstone: the lasers coil the beams, purely visual, and Technology does not grant its damage multiplier... The Ludovico Technique + Mom's Knife overrides Brimstone." — [community synergy summary compiled from Binding of Isaac: Rebirth Wiki, Brimstone/Haemolacria/Technology/Mom's Knife pages]
- Community synergy documentation (720+ items across all expansions) is maintained as per-item and per-pair wiki entries (e.g., "isaacguru.com," "tboi.com/all-items," "thebindingofisaacrebirth.wiki/items/synergies") rather than as a single authoritative in-game combination table — the wiki content itself is curated/discovered by the community after the fact, describing interactions the underlying code produces, not interactions the code explicitly enumerates. — [tboi.com: Every Item in The Binding of Isaac: Repentance](https://www.tboi.com/all-items); [The Binding of Isaac Rebirth Wiki: Item Synergies Guide](https://thebindingofisaacrebirth.wiki/items/synergies/)

### Inferences
- Isaac best supports the research objective's "small set of shared tags/hooks/pipelines" model (b) for the bulk of its item pool: ordinary stat-modifying passives (damage up, tears up, range up, speed up, luck up, etc.) all compose for free because they write into the same small set of cache flags/stat variables, and the "thousands of documented synergies" the wiki catalogs for these items are mostly just arithmetic stacking discovered and written up after the fact — not designer-authored pairs.
- However, the *most famous, build-defining* synergies (the tear-replacer interactions) sit outside that clean pipeline precisely because they compete for control of the same single "what does an attack look like" slot, and Isaac's developers had to fall back to architecture (a) — hand-specified per-pair priority — for that narrow but high-visibility category. This is a useful nuance for the tabletop-RPG design problem: a shared pipeline scales cleanly for *additive/stacking* effects, but *mutually-exclusive, slot-claiming* effects re-introduce the O(n²) hand-authoring problem and may need a different structural solution (e.g. an explicit priority/rank field on each competing effect, rather than a full pairwise table — Isaac's designers effectively rank-order the tear-replacers rather than writing a rule for every pair, which is a partial mitigation).

### Gaps
- No official developer (Edmund McMillen / Nicalis) interview or postmortem specifically explaining the internal architecture decision (cache/pipeline vs. hand-authored) was found in this pass — the cache-flag mechanism was documented via community modding API docs (which mirror, but are not identical to, McMillen's original closed-source implementation before the Repentance/Repentance+ engine rewrite), so some detail may not perfectly reflect the original codebase.
- Could not determine what fraction of the "thousands of documented synergies" are pipeline-emergent stacking vs. genuinely hand-tuned special-cases beyond the tear-replacer category (e.g., whether items like familiars, active items, or trinkets have their own separate hand-authored interaction rules) — this would need a deeper pass through modding decompilation resources.

## Risk of Rain 2: stacking items, proc coefficients, and trigger resolution

### Takeaway
Risk of Rain 2 avoids both a combination table and a Path-of-Exile-style priority-ordered resolution system: every on-hit/proc item independently rolls its own trigger chance, scaled by a single shared "proc coefficient" number attached to whatever skill/attack landed the hit, and stacking uses one of two shared mathematical formulas (linear or hyperbolic) rather than per-item custom scaling curves. Combinations emerge for free because coefficients and stacking formulas are generic multipliers, not because any priority/ordering logic reconciles competing items.

### Cited Findings
- "The proc coefficient is the factor by which an item's effect, trigger chance, duration, or any combination of the three is scaled (multiplied). Every ability and item in the game has a proc coefficient between 0 and 1... Effective Trigger Chance = Item Chance × Proc Coefficient." — [Risk of Rain 2 Wiki: Proc Coefficient](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Proc_Coefficient)
- Stacking uses two shared formula families across the whole item pool rather than bespoke per-item curves: "Linear Stacking: f(x) = 1 + a·x... For many items that use percentages... linear stacking would eventually lead to a 100% chance to trigger or 100% reduction, which would lead to wild imbalance. For this reason, these items use hyperbolic stacking." — [Deltia's Gaming: RoR2 Formulas Guide](https://deltiasgaming.com/risk-of-rain-2-ror2-formulas-guide-how-item-stacking-actually-works/); [Risk of Rain 2 Wiki: Item Stacking](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Item_Stacking)
- On-hit items can chain off each other's procs ("proc chains"): "when one proc triggers another, each step calculates its damage based on the previous step, however the original hit's bonuses do not carry over," and damage-over-time effects (Bleed, Burn) have a proc coefficient of 0.0 and thus cannot themselves re-trigger on-hit items — a single global rule preventing infinite proc chains rather than a hand-authored exception per DOT/on-hit item pair. — [GameRant: How Do Proc Coefficients Work In Risk Of Rain 2?](https://gamerant.com/risk-of-rain-2-ror-2-proc-chain-coefficients-explained/)
- No central priority/ordering system for simultaneous procs was found in any source: each on-hit item independently rolls its own activation chance using the same shared coefficient multiplier for that specific attack — e.g., a 10% base bleed chance becomes 6% when triggered by an attack with proc coefficient 0.6, or 30% with coefficient 3.0 — with the resulting build diversity ("the same item set produces different tactical outcomes depending on which survivor uses it") coming purely from which skills a character has, not from any conflict-resolution logic between items. — [Risk of Rain 2 Wiki: Proc Coefficient](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Proc_Coefficient); [GameRant: How Do Proc Coefficients Work In Risk Of Rain 2?](https://gamerant.com/risk-of-rain-2-ror-2-proc-chain-coefficients-explained/)
- Items also carry a separate `ItemTag` metadata system (Damage, Healing, Utility, AIBlacklist, BrotherBlacklist, OnKillEffect, CannotCopy, and others) used for categorization purposes such as which items are eligible to drop from Category Chests (Damage/Healing/Utility categories) and which items are excluded from being granted to certain enemies/AI or copied by certain effects (AIBlacklist, BrotherBlacklist, CannotCopy). — [Risk of Rain 2 Wiki: Item Tags](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Item_Tags)

### Inferences
- Risk of Rain 2's `ItemTag` system is analogous to Isaac's tags in that it is metadata for *classification/eligibility* (loot pools, AI restrictions, copy-restrictions) rather than a mechanism that itself resolves trigger order between items — the actual "which effects can chain and how strongly" question is answered entirely by the proc-coefficient scalar and the two stacking-formula families, not by ItemTag priority.
- This directly answers the research question about Path-of-Exile-style priority resolution: no evidence was found that Risk of Rain 2 uses priority-ordered conflict resolution for on-hit triggers. Instead it sidesteps the conflict-resolution problem altogether by making on-hit effects independently probabilistic (each item "rolls its own dice" scaled by a shared coefficient) rather than deterministic and slot-claiming — which avoids needing any ordering rule in the first place. This is architecturally the cleanest of the four case studies for scaling to a large item pool, precisely because it never needs a priority table: a new on-hit item just needs a trigger chance and it will compose correctly with every other on-hit item and every skill's coefficient with zero additional authoring.

### Gaps
- No developer (Hopoo Games) interview, GDC talk, or postmortem specifically discussing the proc-coefficient/stacking-formula design rationale was found in this research pass — all findings here come from community wiki documentation and derived guides, not primary developer sources.
- Could not confirm whether any items in Risk of Rain 2 do have explicit hand-authored pairwise interactions (analogous to Isaac's tear-replacer conflicts) for items that claim an exclusive "replace this skill/behavior" slot (e.g., certain item-granted "on death" or equipment-replacement effects) — this would be useful to check further, since RoR2 does have some items that override default behavior (e.g., certain equipment items), and whether those need per-pair authored rules the way Isaac's tear-replacers do is unresolved here.

## Cross-case comparison: which pattern dominates, and which scales better?

### Takeaway
The four games split roughly 1-and-a-partial vs. 2-and-a-partial between the two patterns named in the research objective: Hades is architecture (a) (sparse hand-curated named-combination table, keyed off the small "gods" dimension rather than the larger "boons" dimension to stay tractable); Slay the Spire and Risk of Rain 2 are cleanly architecture (b) (shared hooks/coefficients that any new item plugs into for free); and Binding of Isaac is a hybrid — mostly (b) for ordinary stacking passives via its stat-cache pipeline, but forced into (a) for the narrower category of mutually-exclusive "slot-claiming" effects (tear replacers). No direct developer postmortem comparing these approaches across studios was found, but the structural evidence strongly favors (b)-style shared-pipeline architectures as the scalable pattern, with (a)-style curated tables reserved for a deliberately small, closed dimension (Hades keys its table off ~8-9 gods, not off the much larger boon count) or for narrow special-case conflicts (Isaac's tear replacers).

### Cited Findings
- See per-section findings above; the comparison itself is a synthesis rather than a separately-sourced claim, since no single source discusses all four games' architectures side by side.
- Hades' Duo Boon table scales with C(gods, 2), which stays small (28 pairs for 8 gods, 36 for 9) even as the roster of individual boons within each god grows — this is the concrete mechanism by which Supergiant avoids an unmanageable table size: they key the special-case lookup off a low-cardinality dimension (gods) rather than a high-cardinality one (individual boons or boon-instances). — [Hades Duo Boons data tool](https://orlp.github.io/hades-boons/duo_boons.html); [RogueRanker: Hades 2 Duo Boons](https://rogueranker.com/hades-2-duo-boons/)
- Slay the Spire's and Risk of Rain 2's shared-pipeline architectures both let a brand-new item/card be added with zero additional pairwise authoring and still combine correctly with the entire existing pool, because the "interaction surface" for any object is only the small fixed set of hooks/coefficients it participates in, not every other object. — [BaseMod Wiki: Hooks](https://github.com/daviscook477/BaseMod/wiki/Hooks); [Risk of Rain 2 Wiki: Proc Coefficient](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Proc_Coefficient)
- Isaac's stat-cache pipeline (`CacheFlag`/`MC_EVALUATE_CACHE`) demonstrates that the (b) pattern can absorb a very large item count (700+ items) for additive/multiplicative effects, but the tear-replacer conflicts show that (b) breaks down specifically when multiple items want exclusive ownership of the same discrete "which behavior is active" slot rather than contributing a scalar to a shared total — at that point the designers reintroduced explicit, hand-ranked priority. — [Isaac Blueprints: Stats cache tutorial](https://isaacblueprints.com/tutorials/basics/stats_cache/); Brimstone/Mom's Knife/Technology/Haemolacria priority findings above.

### Inferences
- For the tabletop RPG design problem this research is meant to inform: the scalable takeaway is to design a small, fixed set of shared "hooks" or "stat channels" (e.g., "on hit," "on take damage," "on turn start," a shared numeric pool like "extra actions" or "damage type") that every new ability/item plugs into, rather than authoring a rule for every new pair. Reserve an explicit hand-curated combination table (Hades' model) only for a deliberately small, closed dimension of the design (e.g., a fixed small set of "classes" or "elements," not the full item/ability pool) if a "named flagship combo" flavor payoff is wanted. Expect that any effects which are mutually-exclusive/slot-claiming (rather than additive/stacking) will need an explicit priority-rank field per effect (cheaper than a full pairwise table, since it's O(n) authoring, one rank per effect, rather than O(n²)) — this is the compromise Isaac uses for tear replacers and is likely the right pattern for any "this ability overrides how basic attacks/actions work" category in the tabletop system.
- The Risk of Rain 2 proc-coefficient model is arguably the strongest single mechanism found across all four cases for the stated goal (recombination without pairwise authoring), because it replaces "does X interact specially with Y" with "does X have a trigger-chance number and does Y (the attack) have a coefficient number" — reducing an O(n²) authoring problem to O(n) (one number per item) plus O(1) (one shared formula).

### Gaps
- No primary-source developer commentary (interviews, GDC talks with accessible transcripts, or postmortems) directly comparing "curated table" vs. "shared pipeline" tradeoffs was located for any of the four studios in this pass; the comparison and scaling claims above are inferences from the structural/mechanical evidence gathered, not a direct citation of developer reasoning. This should be flagged to the report writer as synthesis rather than sourced fact where marked.
- Could not verify from primary sources whether Supergiant, Mega Crit, or Hopoo Games have ever explicitly framed their own design in "table vs. pipeline" terms as this research task does; that framing is the research task's own analytical lens applied to the sourced mechanical facts.
