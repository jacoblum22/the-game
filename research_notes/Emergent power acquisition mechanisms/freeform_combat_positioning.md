# Theater of the Mind / No-Grid Tactical Combat Resolution in TTRPGs

## How does Blades in the Dark's Position/Effect system implicitly handle "where is everyone" without tracking coordinates? Are there explicit rules for closing distance, retreating, or being cornered?

### Takeaway
Position (Controlled/Risky/Desperate) and Effect (Limited/Standard/Great) are both set by the GM, at the same moment, purely from the fictional description of the action and situation — there is no map, distance, or coordinate input at all. Spatial/tactical questions like "can I reach them" or "am I cornered" are resolved narratively (the GM decides what's fictionally plausible) and then translated directly into the Position/Effect axes rather than into movement rules; the one quasi-mechanical spatial tool the game does use for chases/cornering is a pair of opposed progress clocks, not a positional grid.

### Cited Findings
- The GM sets Position and Effect together, "after the player says what they're doing and chooses their action" — both are determined by the GM from the fictional context, not rolled or chosen by the player. — [Setting Position & Effect](https://bladesinthedark.com/setting-position-effect)
- The default/baseline combination for a roll is Risky/Standard, and it is then "modified by the action being used, the strength of the opposition, and the effect factors" — three fictional inputs, none of them spatial coordinates. — [Setting Position & Effect](https://bladesinthedark.com/setting-position-effect)
- Position and Effect together form a 3×3 matrix (Controlled/Risky/Desperate × Limited/Standard/Great); Desperate means "if this doesn't go perfectly, they'll be utterly screwed," Controlled means "they'll have a chance to reassess," Risky is between the two; Limited/Standard/Great describe how far short of, exactly, or beyond the stated goal the outcome falls. — [Blades in the Dark: Action Rolls Explained](https://lagoon83.medium.com/blades-in-the-dark-action-rolls-explained-b9e5ab4b4b46)
- Position is explicitly framed as "how bad can things go" (danger to the PC), not distance; things that would be spatial/tactical factors on a grid — being outnumbered, lacking cover, fighting in the open vs. a narrow alley/choke point, having a sniper's vantage — are folded directly into worsening or improving Position rather than tracked as coordinates. — [Setting Position & Effect](https://bladesinthedark.com/setting-position-effect)
- Players can spend Stress to "Push" for better Effect, or trade Position for Effect (worsen one a step to improve the other a step) — these are the only "adjustment" levers, and neither involves movement or distance. — [Setting Position & Effect](https://bladesinthedark.com/setting-position-effect)
- "Fictional positioning" is described as the defining FitD (Forged in the Dark) mechanic: identical actions by two different squads (a stealth-trained squad vs. shock troops both crossing an exposed clearing) can get different Position/Effect purely because of who they fictionally are, with no bonus dice or map difference — the GM must "write the world" clearly enough that the fictional stakes of a spatial situation (an exposed clearing, a choke point) are legible without measuring it. — [Fictional Positioning when Forging in the Dark](https://offguardgames.com/2019/07/08/fictional-positioning-in-fitd/)
- For chase/cornering scenarios specifically, Blades uses opposed progress clocks rather than a spatial mechanic: e.g., PCs have an "Escape" clock and pursuers have a "Cornered" clock running in parallel; if the PCs fill their clock first they get away, otherwise they are cornered and can't flee. This is the closest thing the system has to an explicit "closing distance" or "being trapped" mechanic, and it is a race-of-clocks abstraction, not a position-tracking one. — [Fictional Positioning when Forging in the Dark](https://offguardgames.com/2019/07/08/fictional-positioning-in-fitd/)
- Partial success on a positioning-flavored action (e.g., trying to corner an enemy) is resolved narratively rather than negated outright — GM guidance explicitly warns against saying "the enemy escapes" on a 4/5 result; instead the enemy ends up cornered but the PC pays a complication (e.g., a scuffle in which the enemy grabs the PC's gun), keeping the "where is everyone" question fictional rather than re-litigated via distance math. — [Setting Position & Effect](https://bladesinthedark.com/setting-position-effect)

### Inferences
- Blades in the Dark essentially eliminates the need for grid-style tactical resolution by making position/effect a direct GM judgment call on danger and outcome-scope, deferring all "can I reach/flank/retreat" questions to prose description and precedent-setting narration, with progress clocks standing in for anything that needs to unfold "over time" (like a chase) rather than "over space."
- This places a very high narration burden on the GM (must improvise consistent fictional positioning cues) rather than a bookkeeping burden — relevant for an AI GM, since an LLM narrating in prose is well-suited to improvising this kind of judgment call but needs guidance on what factors should worsen/improve Position/Effect.

### Gaps
- No source found gives an explicit, named "closing distance" or "retreating" action/move analogous to D&D's Disengage or 5e's movement rules — Blades appears to treat this entirely as freeform narration folded into Position/Effect and (for extended chases) progress clocks, rather than a discrete rule. This may simply not exist as a formal rule in the system.

## How many zones does a typical Fate Core/Fate Condensed scene have, what determines cross-zone action, and how does this differ from a grid while being more concrete than pure narration?

### Takeaway
Fate uses a small number of abstract "zones" (2–4 typical, rarely more than ~6) as a coarse map layer: melee ("Fight") only works within a zone, ranged ("Shoot") can reach up to two zones away, and moving between zones costs an action (or a skill roll if a Situation Aspect makes movement contested) — giving Fate a lightweight, sketch-level positional layer that a grid replaces with exact squares/distances and pure theater-of-the-mind lacks entirely.

### Cited Findings
- "A conflict should rarely involve more than a handful of zones, with two to four probably being sufficient, save for really big conflicts... more than six or so zones tends to get cumbersome." — [Zones in FATE CORE](https://www.worldanvil.com/w/fate-core-moose2181/a/zones-article)
- As a rule of thumb for mass combat, the number of zones should equal one more than the number of players (e.g., a GM + 3 players scene would use 5 zones). — [This Means War: Mass Combat, Fate System Toolkit](https://fate-srd.com/fate-system-toolkit/means-war-mass-combat)
- A zone is defined as an abstract representation of physical space — "close enough that you can interact directly with someone (in other words, walk up to and punch them in the face)." — [Zones in FATE CORE](https://www.worldanvil.com/w/fate-core-moose2181/a/zones-article)
- Movement: "if there's nothing preventing you from doing so, you can move one zone in addition to your action for the exchange. If you want to move more than one zone or if a situation aspect suggests it might be difficult to move freely, you must make an overcome action using Athletics to move." — [Movement, Fate Core](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/movement)
- Melee (Fight skill) only works against a target in the same zone, while the Shoot skill "can make [attacks] from up to two zones away," making ranged weapons mechanically distinct from melee purely via zone distance rather than exact range numbers. — [Shoot, Fate Core](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/shoot); community summary from search of Fate Core weapon-range discussion.
- Fate does not have an explicit "firing into melee" penalty rule; instead the system relies on narrative logic and Aspects — e.g., a "Shotgun" Aspect might be invoked for bonuses at close range and compelled for penalties at long range — leaving edge cases like shooting at a target in melee with an ally to GM/table narrative judgment rather than codified mechanics. — [Fate Core Weapon Properties discussion](https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/fate-core-weapon-properties.757132/); ["But there aren't rules for that!"](https://ironbombs.wordpress.com/2014/01/22/fate-but-there-arent-rules-for-that/)

### Inferences
- Zones are best understood as a middle ground: coarser and cheaper to track than a grid (no coordinates, no exact distances, only adjacency/"how many zones away"), but more concrete than pure prose narration because they give a countable unit ("2 zones away") that determines legality of an action (melee vs. ranged) and the cost of movement (free 1-zone move vs. a roll for more).
- For a prose-narrating AI GM, an internal zone-like counter (even unnamed to the player) could resolve "can I reach them this turn" questions cheaply without exposing any grid/coordinate language in the narration.

### Gaps
- Could not find a canonical, official-text quote from the Fate Core core rulebook itself (as opposed to secondary summaries/wikis) giving the "2-4 zones typical" guidance verbatim — the figure is corroborated by two independent secondary sources (a Fate wiki article and the Fate System Toolkit's mass-combat rule of thumb) but a primary-source citation from the core rulebook PDF was not directly fetched in this pass.

## How do PbtA games (Dungeon World and others) handle "can I hit this enemy," positioning, and multiple-enemy engagement without turn-by-turn initiative and a grid?

### Takeaway
Dungeon World replaces exact distance with five named descriptive range bands (Hand/Close/Reach/Near/Far) tied to what a given move or weapon needs, uses no turn order or action economy (players act when the fiction calls for it, GM interjects with "moves" in response), and resolves multi-enemy/positioning problems as emergent narrative consequences of partial success rather than as a separate spatial subsystem; Apocalypse World's generic GM moves toolkit (e.g., "separate them") is the mechanism the GM uses to reshape the tactical situation.

### Cited Findings
- "Dungeon World is designed well for theater of mind combat and has no distances given for anything." Instead it defines range with descriptive categories: Hand (attacking something within reach), Close (arm's reach plus a foot or two), Reach (attacking something several feet away, maybe as far as ten), Near (seeing the whites of their eyes), and Far (shouting distance). — [I'm running Dungeon World/PbtA wrong, please help me run it right](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/im-running-dungeon-world-pbta-wrong-please-help-me-run-it-right.854513/)
- "There is no action economy or initiative order, and the transitions between combat and not-combat elements are seamless. Combat doesn't take place in strict turns" — players describe actions and jump in as the fiction dictates. — [Dungeon World (A Powered by the Apocalypse Review)](https://dmtales.com/2023/05/23/dungeon-world-a-powered-by-the-apocalypse-review/)
- Positioning consequences emerge from partial successes rather than being separately tracked: e.g., if a player tries to duck out of an orc's way and only partially succeeds, the GM can narrate that the orc ends up between the PC and the rest of the party — turning a dodge roll into a positioning shift purely through narration. — [I'm running Dungeon World/PbtA wrong](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/im-running-dungeon-world-pbta-wrong-please-help-me-run-it-right.854513/)
- "Separate them" is one of Apocalypse World's basic MC (GM) moves — a generic tool the GM can invoke (typically as a consequence of a missed/partial roll) to physically divide the PCs, which is how multi-enemy or split-the-party tactical situations get introduced without any grid mechanic. — [50. MC Moves, The Daily Apocalypse](https://daily-apocalypse.com/daily-apocalypse/50-mc-moves)
- General PbtA design philosophy: all GM moves "put the characters in a position to make a choice and act," and each move creates a reaction the MC responds to in turn — meaning positioning/spatial pressure in PbtA is one flavor of the same generic "moves" currency used for everything else (threats, complications, costs), not a distinct tactical layer. — [50. MC Moves, The Daily Apocalypse](https://daily-apocalypse.com/daily-apocalypse/50-mc-moves)
- In Monster of the Week specifically (an Apocalypse-engine game), any pre-roll storytelling about getting the right weapon or positioning happens narratively, and combat itself "often boils down to a single action roll to see if you win and what price you paid" — the Keeper (GM) does not roll for monster actions but reacts to the hunters' rolls, again keeping position as fictional framing rather than a subsystem. — [Combat example for Apocalypse engine games?, rpg.net forum](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/combat-example-for-apocalypse-engine-games-monster-of-the-week-apocalypse-world.649053/)

### Inferences
- PbtA's approach effectively dissolves "positioning" as a distinct concern: it's just one of many things a partial-success consequence or an MC move can introduce narratively, which is philosophically the closest of all systems surveyed to what a prose-only AI GM already does by default — the risk is consistency (the GM/AI needs some memory of "who's near whom" to avoid contradicting itself across turns), which PbtA's actual-play advice doesn't formally solve either (see Gap below).

### Gaps
- Did not find explicit rules text (only secondary discussion) on how PbtA GMs are advised to keep persistent track of "who's near whom" across a whole fight to stay consistent turn-to-turn, if at all — this may simply be left to GM improvisation/memory rather than codified, which is itself a relevant (negative) data point for an AI GM design.

## How does Ironsworn / Ironsworn: Starforged's move-based combat handle position/range without a grid?

### Takeaway
Ironsworn/Starforged resolve range almost entirely through the choice of which move/stat to use rather than tracked distance: "Enter the Fray" sets who has initiative based on the fictional approach (face-off, sneak, ambush), and "Strike" simply asks whether you are fighting "in close quarters" (roll +iron) or "at range" (roll +edge) — the player/GM declare the fictional range category per move rather than consulting a map.

### Cited Findings
- Enter the Fray, the move that starts a fight, triggers differently depending on fictional approach: "Facing off against your foe: Roll +heart. Moving into position against an unaware foe, or striking without warning: Roll +shadow. Ambushed: Roll +wits." On a strong hit you gain +2 momentum and take initiative. — [Ironsworn Moves (gameplay) wiki](https://dirty20gaming-plays-ironsworn.fandom.com/wiki/Ironsworn_Moves_(gameplay))
- Strike: "When you have initiative and attack in close quarters, roll +iron. When you have initiative and attack at range, roll +edge." Strong hit inflicts +1 harm and retains initiative; weak hit inflicts harm but loses initiative. — [Strike, Ironsworn RPG wiki](https://ironsworn-rpg.fandom.com/wiki/Strike); [Combat Moves, Ironsworn: Starforged SRD](https://rsek.github.io/starforged-srd/moves/combat)
- Ironsworn's overall design philosophy (per its own marketing/community framing) is to have "less rules than D&D" and explicitly encourage players to "not be afraid to break out of the rules if needed or do things the game doesn't explicitly tell you to do" — reinforcing that spatial/positional nuance is meant to be handled by table fiat, not a subsystem. — [Ironsworn: Starforged tips, monkeyslunch.com](https://monkeyslunch.com/ironsworn-starforged-tips/)

### Inferences
- Ironsworn essentially collapses "position" into a binary (close quarters vs. range) selected per-move based on narrative description, plus an "initiative" token that shifts between combatants based on hit/miss quality — this is one of the lightest-weight solutions surveyed and might be a good minimal template for an AI GM: instead of tracking coordinates, just track a discrete state per combatant (e.g., "engaged in melee" vs. "at range" vs. "fleeing") that individual narrated actions can read and update.

### Gaps
- Did not find explicit Starforged-specific combat-range moves beyond the shared "Strike"/"Enter the Fray" core moves in the time budget for this pass; a deeper dive into Starforged's vehicle/starship combat (which likely has its own range abstraction) was out of scope for this lighter pass.

## What other notable narrative-first systems are praised/criticized for grid-less combat positioning?

### Takeaway
Mörk Borg is a clear, well-documented example of an OSR-adjacent game praised for deliberately abstracting away distance/movement almost entirely, using a flat situational modifier (+3/-3) for things like flanking or high ground instead of any spatial calculation; it's frequently cited as ideal for theater-of-the-mind and online play specifically because of this.

### Cited Findings
- "Mörk Borg is much more abstracted than typical OSR games... intended to be played theater of the mind with a very loose play style, requiring very little of the tactical rule set used in even B/X D&D," and "the game doesn't pay too much attention to things like movement, distance, etc." — [Welcome to the Deathtrap: Game Review: Mörk Borg](https://deathtrap-games.blogspot.com/2022/09/game-review-mork-borg.html)
- Positional tactical factors (flanking, higher ground) are handled via a flat "Advantage/Disadvantage inside combat" modifier of +3 or -3 to attack/dodge rolls, rather than a grid-derived bonus. — [Welcome to the Deathtrap: Game Review: Mörk Borg](https://deathtrap-games.blogspot.com/2022/09/game-review-mork-borg.html)
- Mörk Borg uses a "skirmish" initiative system where the whole party acts in the same round against the opposition, with the party group discussing internally who acts first — removing strict turn-order/positioning interplay found in grid-based initiative systems. — [Welcome to the Deathtrap: Game Review: Mörk Borg](https://deathtrap-games.blogspot.com/2022/09/game-review-mork-borg.html)
- Reviewers explicitly praise this abstraction as suited to the "current trend of online play" (i.e., theater-of-the-mind/no-grid is treated as a feature, not a limitation, in the context of remote/text-based play). — [Welcome to the Deathtrap: Game Review: Mörk Borg](https://deathtrap-games.blogspot.com/2022/09/game-review-mork-borg.html)

### Inferences
- The one flat modifier (+3/-3) approach in Mörk Borg is arguably the simplest possible translation of "positional advantage" into a number, and is a plausible fallback for an AI GM that wants to occasionally reward good tactical narration (e.g., a player narrating a flanking maneuver) without any persistent spatial state.

### Gaps
- Could not find substantive, citable material specifically on Root RPG's combat-positioning design within the research budget for this pass (searches surfaced Mörk Borg content but not Root RPG specifics) — this is a gap for a system explicitly named in the assignment's example list.
- Similarly did not reach primary Monster of the Week rulebook text beyond secondary commentary already cited above.

## Has any AI-driven/LLM-driven text RPG project documented how it resolves spatial/tactical combat questions in pure prose?

### Takeaway
At least one published research system (ChatRPG, arXiv 2502.19519) documents this problem directly: its first version had no spatial reasoning at all and produced "non-sensical outcomes" (e.g., narrating a kick as a self-inflicted "trip," or attacks resolving against the wrong target) when players didn't over-specify their actions; its fix was not to add spatial tracking but to abstract combat into a structured "Battle tool" that takes hit-chance/damage-severity parameters directly, sidestepping positional reasoning entirely rather than solving it. Commercial AI-GM products are split between adding an explicit visual grid (abandoning theater-of-the-mind) versus keeping prose-only narration paired with a separate deterministic rules engine that tracks state the LLM doesn't have to reason about spatially.

### Cited Findings
- ChatRPG v1 (pilot study): "unless [participants] meticulously described their actions, v1 often resulted in non-sensical outcomes, such as characters stumbling or self-injuring when the system failed to identify an appropriate target." One participant reported that a simple kick action was narrated as an unjustified "tripping" — i.e., a fundamental misinterpretation of intent absent any spatial/target reasoning. — [Static Vs. Agentic Game Master AI for Facilitating Solo Role-Playing Games, arXiv 2502.19519v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v2)
- The v2 fix was NOT to add spatial state tracking; instead the paper describes: (1) a "Battle tool" that "simulates combat between two characters by specifying attack order, hit chances, and damage severity for each exchange" via structured JSON parameters rather than freeform spatial interpretation; (2) few-shot prompting examples baked into the tool description to keep combat logic consistent; (3) a specialized "Narrator" agent dedicated to combat decisions to reduce inconsistency. The paper explicitly does not report building any dedicated spatial state-management or distance-tracking system, and flags this as an unaddressed limitation for future work. — [Static Vs. Agentic Game Master AI, arXiv 2502.19519v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v2)
- Among commercial/hobbyist AI-GM products, at least one (Friends & Fables) has instead gone the opposite direction and reintroduced a visual layer — described as using "hex battlemaps, token movement, and spatial combat structure" for players who want traditional tabletop tactical structure rather than pure narrative freeform. — [11 Best LLM RPG Games You Can Play Inside ChatGPT, Claude & Gemini, arcanumrpgs.com](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/best-llm-rpg-games/)
- Other products (RoleForge, DungeonsDeep.ai) instead keep prose-only narration but separate the LLM "narrator" from a deterministic rules/mechanics engine that handles combat, skill checks, and progression state — i.e., the LLM never has to do spatial reasoning itself; a non-LLM system tracks whatever positional/mechanical state exists and the LLM only narrates the results. — [5 Best AI Game Masters Compared & Ranked, dungeonsdeep.ai](https://dungeonsdeep.ai/blog/the-best-ai-game-masters-compared-in-2026)

### Inferences
- The one rigorously documented case (ChatRPG) supports a strong design conclusion: giving an LLM freeform narrative control over "what happened" in combat without ANY structured backstop (not necessarily a grid, but at minimum structured hit/target/state parameters) produces incoherent results in practice, not just in theory. The fix that worked was structuring the *resolution* (hit chance, damage, valid targets) as tool calls with explicit parameters — while still leaving the actual prose narration free — rather than tracking literal spatial coordinates. This maps well onto the Blades/Ironsworn pattern of "abstract discrete state (position/effect, or close-vs-range) resolved by GM/tool judgment" rather than either literal grid coordinates or pure unconstrained prose.
- The commercial product landscape shows a real fork in the road for this project's own design choice: add a lightweight visual/structured layer (Friends & Fables' approach) vs. keep pure prose but push spatial/tactical state into a separate deterministic subsystem the LLM queries or calls into (RoleForge/DungeonsDeep.ai approach) rather than trying to have the LLM reason about space unaided.

### Gaps
- Beyond the one arXiv paper, could not find other academic or rigorously documented (as opposed to marketing-page-level) case studies of LLM text-RPG projects that specifically analyze the spatial-positioning problem in prose combat within this pass's search budget; the commercial-product claims (Friends & Fables, RoleForge, DungeonsDeep.ai) are sourced from aggregator/review blog posts rather than each product's own technical documentation, so should be treated as lower-confidence, secondary-source claims about product features rather than verified technical design details.
