# Procedural Affix/Effect Generation in ARPGs and Loot Games

## Diablo series (D2/D3/D4): what is the actual data structure/algorithm for item affixes? How are tiers, mutually-exclusive prefix/suffix groups, and rarity structured? How does ilvl gate affix rolls? Where is the generated/authored line drawn?

### Takeaway
Diablo's magic/rare items are generated by selecting from flat, tagged tables of prefixes and suffixes (each entry has a level requirement and a stat range), gated by a derived "affix level" computed from item level and base-item quality level; Unique/Set items are a structurally different, entirely hand-authored data type with fixed name + preselected property list (no group/exclusivity rules apply to them), and D3/D4 layer a second hand-authored "legendary power" on top of an otherwise-generated stat shell, while D4 additionally lets players inject one deterministic "tempered" affix from a small hand-authored recipe pool onto an otherwise-random item.

### Cited Findings
- In Diablo II, affix data lives in `MagicPrefix.txt` and `MagicSuffix.txt` (tab-delimited tables), with each affix's internal ID referenced from a separate `ItemStatCost` table that defines what the stat actually does — i.e., the affix table stores *which* stat/range applies and to what item types, while a shared stat-definition table stores the mechanical implementation. — [item ID / magicprefixes / magicsuffixes, Phrozen Keep](https://d2mods.info/forum/viewtopic.php?t=7076); [Item Affixes, Project Diablo 2 Wiki](https://wiki.projectdiablo2.com/wiki/Item_Affixes)
- Magical (blue) items get a 50% chance of suffix-only, 25% prefix-only, 25% both; Rare (yellow) items can have up to 6 modifiers (3 prefixes + 3 suffixes, or any combination up to that cap). — [Item Affixes, Project Diablo 2 Wiki](https://wiki.projectdiablo2.com/wiki/Item_Affixes)
- Which specific affixes are eligible to roll is gated by a derived value called "affix level" (alvl), computed from item level (ilvl) and the base item's quality level (qlvl): if `ilvl < (99 - qlvl/2)` then `alvl = ilvl - qlvl/2`, else `alvl = 2*ilvl - 99`; ilvl is capped at 99, and if qlvl > ilvl, ilvl is first raised to qlvl. A character's required level to equip an affix is roughly 75% of alvl. — [A Guide to the Basic Terms of Item Generation, diablo2.io](https://diablo2.io/forums/a-guide-to-the-basic-terms-of-item-generation-t8350.html)
- Diablo II Unique items are a wholly separate data structure: their stats were "custom created by Blizzard North" rather than drawn from the prefix/suffix tables, with 3 to 8 preselected properties (some rolled within a narrow range, e.g. "100-120% enhanced damage," but the *set* of properties is fixed); "there are no rules to the mods that can be on a unique, unlike with magical and rare items." Set items work the same way (fixed name, preselected properties) plus conditional set-bonus stats that activate as more pieces of the set are equipped. — [Unique Items, PureDiablo](https://www.purediablo.com/diablo-2/uniques); [Diablo 2 Item Sets, PureDiablo](https://www.purediablo.com/diablo-2/diablo-2-item-sets)
- Diablo III (Loot 2.0, 2014 Reaper of Souls patch) sorted affixes into Primary (up to 4) and Secondary (up to 2) slots, with no prefix/suffix distinction; Legendary-only "orange" affixes are always placed in a Secondary slot specifically so they never compete with (crowd out) an item's Primary stat roll. All items of a given legendary type pull generated Primary/Secondary stats from the same pool/range, so item quality is about the luck of which stats rolled, while the fixed legendary power itself is guaranteed and non-random ("Smart Loot" also guarantees class-appropriate main stats). Enchanting lets a player reroll one non-legendary affix on demand, but not the legendary power itself. — [Legendary Affixes, Diablo Wiki](https://www.diablowiki.net/Legendary_Affixes); [Loot 2.0, Diablo Wiki](https://www.diablowiki.net/Loot_2.0); [Smart Loot, Diablo Wiki](https://www.diablowiki.net/Smart_Loot)
- Diablo III's pre-Loot-2.0 (2012 launch) itemization was widely regarded as a design failure specifically because rare/legendary stats rolled in a "completely random" unconstrained way; players had to live with it "for nearly two years" until Loot 2.0 imposed the Primary/Secondary structure and Smart Loot constraints described above. This is a directly citable case of a genre-defining studio treating "fully open random rolls" as a mistake to be corrected with more structure. — [Every Legendary Affix So far, DiabloFans forums](https://www.diablofans.com/forums/read-only-diablo-forums/diablo-iii-general-discussion/77294-every-legendary-affix-so-far)
- Diablo IV layers a third mechanism, Tempering: it adds one extra *deterministic* affix to Rare/Legendary/Unique/Mythic Unique gear, chosen by the player from a Tempering Recipe (each Temper Manual teaches a recipe containing 3–5 possible affixes, categorized by theme, e.g. "Mobility," the same categorization scheme used for the Codex of Power's Legendary Aspects). The recipe/manual itself is hand-authored and world-dropped (a bounded, discoverable set), but which of the 3–5 listed affixes actually lands, and its roll value, is randomized when applied; a "Greater Affix" (GA) roll (a random +50% bonus to a rolled value) additionally grants a re-roll chance. Ancestral-tier items are guaranteed at least one GA and get two Temper slots. — [Tempering Guide, Maxroll.gg](https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/tempering-guide); [Diablo 4 Greater Affix Guide, ggwtb](https://ggwtb.com/blog/diablo-4-greater-affix-guide-fast-ancestrals--mythic-uniques--aspects-and-op-endgame-loot)
- Diablo IV's Codex of Power stores both unlocked Legendary Aspects and Tempering recipes for indefinite reuse once found once — i.e., the hand-authored "special effect" pool is account-bound knowledge, not a consumable, which is a distinct design lever from Diablo II/III's "the specific unique item drop is itself the unlock." — [Tempering Guide, Maxroll.gg](https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/tempering-guide)

### Inferences
- The consistent Diablo-genre pattern is a **two-tier data model**: (1) a shared, tagged, level-gated affix table used by all "generated rarity" items (Magic/Rare in D2, Rare/Legendary Primary-Secondary slots in D3/D4), and (2) a categorically separate, small, hand-authored table for Unique/Set/Legendary "special" effects that is exempted from the normal affix-selection rules entirely (no group exclusivity, no prefix/suffix slot competition) and instead bundled with the item's identity itself.
- The affix-level formula (`alvl` derived from `ilvl` and `qlvl`) is functionally a **gate function**, not a weighting function — it defines a hard eligibility cutoff (this affix's tier is or isn't allowed to appear at all) rather than adjusting relative likelihood. This is a simpler mechanism than PoE's weighted-tag system (see below) and may be more portable to a tabletop context where computing continuous weights isn't practical.
- Diablo IV's Tempering system is the closest analog in this genre to "constrained player choice within a small authored pool, applied to an otherwise-random item" — it demonstrates that a design can mix (a) a fully generated item, (b) a small found/unlocked set of *categories* of effect, and (c) a randomized specific roll within the chosen category, as three independently-tuned layers.

## Path of Exile's item affix system: tiers, essence/fossil/crafting bench, how currency manipulates the affix pool, and how PoE avoids combinatorial explosion becoming unmanageable

### Takeaway
PoE affixes are generated through a tag-and-weight matching algorithm (an item's base type carries tags; each mod lists which tags it can spawn on and a weight per tag) combined with a hard "mod group" exclusivity rule (only one mod per group, scoped separately to prefixes vs. suffixes) and an item-level gate on tier eligibility; currency items (Chaos/Exalted/Essence/Fossil/Crafting Bench) are different *entry points* into this same underlying weighted table rather than separate systems, and the genre-standard answer to combinatorial explosion is **not** to make the space smaller for the player to hold in their head, but to externalize evaluation to third-party tools (Craft of Exile, the official trade-site stat-filter search) plus a bounded per-item affix cap (max 3 prefixes + 3 suffixes = 6 total rolled affixes, regardless of how many thousands of possible mods exist in the underlying table).

### Cited Findings
- Each mod has an associated **domain** (what kind of entity it can appear on) and a **generation type** — prefix, suffix, corrupted-only, or always-applied — which is the top-level classification before any weighting happens. — [Modifiers, Path of Exile Wiki (Fandom)](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Modifiers)
- Affix strength is bucketed into discrete **tiers**; e.g. a low-tier life mod might add ~10-20 life while a high-tier version of the same mod adds ~80-90 life, and higher item level raises the chance/eligibility of higher tiers appearing. — [Modifiers, Path of Exile Wiki (Fandom)](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Modifiers)
- Mods are matched to a base item via a **tag system**: the base item type carries tags (e.g. `str_armour`, `body_armour`, `armour`, `default`), each candidate mod lists a spawn weight per tag, the generator collects all tags on the item, sums the matching weights across the mod pool, and discards any mod whose total weight resolves to zero for that item (i.e., a hard eligibility filter layered under a soft probability weighting). Selection probability for a specific mod is `target_mod_weight / total_weight_of_eligible_mods`. Where an item has multiple applicable tags, left-most tags are treated as most significant. — [Spawn Weighting PoE, vhpg.com](https://www.vhpg.com/spawn-weighting-poe/); [Modifiers, Path of Exile Wiki (Fandom)](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Modifiers)
- **Mod groups** enforce hard mutual exclusivity: "only one mod from a group can appear at a time" on an item — but a group's exclusivity is scoped to prefixes or to suffixes separately, so it's possible to have a prefix and a suffix from the same nominal family simultaneously (they don't compete with each other across the prefix/suffix boundary, only within it). This is the direct mechanism preventing e.g. two different "% increased life" mods of different tiers from both landing on one item. — [Understanding Mod Groups discussion, PoE official forum](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3587391)
- Every non-unique item is hard-capped at **3 prefixes and 3 suffixes (6 total rolled affixes)** regardless of rarity beyond Magic, which is the structural mechanism bounding "thousands of possible mods" down to a fixed, small, always-legible slot count per item — the combinatorial space is in *which* six affixes, not *how many*. — [Comprehensive Guide to Path of Exile 2 Currency System, mmoso.com](https://www.mmoso.com/news/comprehensive-guide-to-path-of-exile-2-currency-system) (slot-cap figure corroborated for PoE1 in the same source family)
- **Essences**: upgrade a base item directly to Rare with one *guaranteed* affix (bypassing the normal all-random roll for that one slot while the rest of the item's affixes still roll normally from the weighted table); higher essence tiers (Tier 5 "Screaming" and above) can also fully reforge an existing rare item's affixes, overriding meta-crafted mods in the process. — [Essence, Path of Exile Wiki (Fandom)](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Essence)
- **Fossils** (used inside "resonators") bias the affix roll toward specific mod types/tags, at higher rarity/cost than essences — i.e., fossils are a weighting-manipulation tool applied on top of the same underlying tag-weight table rather than a separate generator. — [Path of Exile Crafting Guide, rpgstash.com](https://www.rpgstash.com/blog/how-to-craft-every-item-in-path-of-exile)
- **Crafting Bench**: deterministically adds one specific prefix or suffix to an item that has an open affix slot (guaranteed outcome, no roll on which mod, though the mod's numeric value may still roll within its range); it also handles sockets/links/colors and other non-affix modifications. — [Craft of Exile: Basics of crafting](https://www.craftofexile.com/basics)
- Currency items in general are described as the mechanism for "strategic addition of affixes to gear," with the underlying affix pool always split into prefixes and suffixes and capped as above, regardless of which currency triggered the roll. — [Craft of Exile: Basics of crafting](https://www.craftofexile.com/basics)
- On the "legibility despite scale" question: the official trade site's Stat Filter search lets a player search for **any specific modifier in the entire game** as an explicit filter, and also exposes "pseudo mods" (composite/derived stats, e.g. total elemental resistance) precisely because raw modifiers are too numerous/fragmented for a human to filter on individually — the tool computes higher-level rollups so players don't need to reason about the full underlying mod table directly. — [Understanding Stat Filters on the trade site, PoE official forum](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2914526)
- Third-party tools (Craft of Exile) exist specifically to let players simulate/plan the weighted-roll math (tier tables, weights, probabilities per craft) outside the client, i.e., the base game does not surface full tier/weight information in its own UI — the genre's answer to keeping a large affix table legible is delegating the math to community tooling rather than compressing the design space itself. — [Craft of Exile](https://www.craftofexile.com/how-to-use)

### Inferences
- PoE's core generative primitive is **tag-matched weighted table lookup with a hard group-exclusivity constraint and a fixed output-slot cap**. This is a three-part algorithm: (1) filter the candidate pool by tag compatibility with the base item and item level, (2) weight-sample within that filtered pool, (3) reject/reroll if the sampled mod's group is already occupied in that prefix/suffix category. This shape — filter → weighted sample → group-exclusion check — is a strong candidate template for a tabletop "roll an ability from source × acquisition-method" system: source/acquisition act as the "tags" that filter the eligible ability pool, then a weighted or flat roll picks within it, with a small number of "ability groups" preventing redundant stacking.
- The genre does *not* solve combinatorial legibility by capping the *total design space* (PoE's total mod count is very large) — it solves it by (a) capping the *per-object* number of rolled slots (6 affixes, full stop) and (b) pushing full-space evaluation to external tooling/search rather than requiring in-the-moment player comprehension of the whole table. For a tabletop game (no searchable database at the table), the more relevant lesson is (a): bound the *number of simultaneously active generated effects per character/item*, not the size of the underlying table those effects are drawn from.

## Path of Exile 2's crafting currency system (revised version)

### Takeaway
PoE2 simplifies rather than expands the currency layer versus PoE1: it removes several currencies whose function became redundant (Orb of Scouring, Chromatic Orb, Jeweller's Orb, Orb of Fusing — the latter three specifically because sockets were decoupled from equipment into a separate skill-gem-socket system), makes "craft a rare item" resolve to a fixed, predictable output (exactly 4 modifiers) rather than a variable 2-6, and restricts an item to a single crafted-modifier slot (via a new currency called Alloy) rather than PoE1's more open bench-crafting.

### Cited Findings
- "Every rare item created with Orbs will gain exactly 4 modifiers" in PoE2, replacing PoE1's variable-count rare generation with a fixed, predictable modifier count — explicitly framed as simplifying early-game crafting and making results "more predictable and stable." — [Comprehensive Guide to Path of Exile 2 Currency System, mmoso.com](https://www.mmoso.com/news/comprehensive-guide-to-path-of-exile-2-currency-system)
- Orb of Scouring, Chromatic Orbs, Jeweller's Orbs, and Orb of Fusing do not exist in PoE2; the socket-related removals are explicitly because "the socket system has been completely reworked with skill gem sockets separated from equipment" — i.e., a structural redesign of what sockets even are, not just a currency rename. — [Currency in Path of Exile 2: Main differences from PoE1, uktechnews.co.uk](https://uktechnews.co.uk/2024/10/09/currency-in-path-of-exile-2-main-differences-from-poe1/)
- A 2024-era change restricts each item to a single crafted modifier: the new "Alloy" currency occupies "that exact crafted modifier slot," replacing a more open-ended bench-crafting affix system. — [Path of Exile 2 Crafting Changes Explained, elyxir.gg](https://www.elyxir.gg/news/path-of-exile-2/path-of-exile-2-crafting-changes-explained-skill-sockets-jeweler-orbs-and-the-new-craft-limit)

### Inferences
- PoE2's direction of travel — fixed modifier counts, fewer currency types, one crafted-slot cap — reads as an explicit developer response to the "legibility despite scale" problem noted in PoE1 above: rather than adding more tools to navigate a huge space, PoE2 shrinks the *variance* of the space itself (a fixed 4-affix outcome is far more legible than "2 to 6, randomly").

### Gaps
- Did not find primary-source (GGG developer blog/interview) confirmation of *why* these simplifications were made in designer-stated terms — the "predictability/legibility" framing above is this researcher's inference from journalistic summaries, not a quoted developer rationale, and should be labeled as inference rather than fact in the final report.
- Could not confirm whether PoE2 retains PoE1's tag-weight-group algorithm unchanged under the hood, or whether the underlying mod-selection algorithm itself was also revised (only the currency-item layer's effects were confirmed as changed).

## Borderlands' procedural gun-part generation: how manufacturer + parts produce emergent behavior, not just stat changes

### Takeaway
Borderlands generates guns by combining a swappable-parts inventory (body, barrel, grip, stock, magazine, sight, accessory — ~35 part categories with ~1500-1700 total part instances in Borderlands 3) with a manufacturer identity that is baked into which *parts* and which *behavioral scripts* are even eligible, so "manufacturer" functions as a top-level behavioral template (e.g., Tediore guns are reload-by-throwing/detonating, Maliwan guns are elemental-only, Jakobs guns are non-elemental but high-crit-multiplier) layered underneath the combinatorial stat-part system, rather than as just another stat modifier; Borderlands 3 further tightened this by eliminating cross-manufacturer part-mixing (each manufacturer now has its own variant of every part-type) specifically to keep manufacturer identity behaviorally coherent.

### Cited Findings
- Borderlands 3 guns are built from 35 swappable weapon part categories drawn from an inventory of roughly 1,500-1,700 total part instances; Gearbox built a "gun map" — an explicit compatibility/synergy chart — to keep functional and creative combinations coherent and to verify that generated guns still "read" as belonging to their manufacturer. — [Procedural Gun Generation, gameduck.org (via search summary)](https://www.gameduck.org/post/procedural-gun-generation-how-borderlands-made-a-game-with-over-a-gazillion-guns)
- Borderlands 4 reportedly reaches roughly 30 billion unique gun permutations across about a dozen weapon brands/manufacturers — stated as 30x the "1 billion+" figure attributed to Borderlands 3. — [Borderlands 4 has over 30 billion gun combinations, Gearbox says, tweaktown.com](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/107001/borderlands-4-has-over-30-billion-gun-combinations-gearbox-says/index.html)
- Unlike earlier games, Borderlands 3 does not mix manufacturers' parts on one gun; instead of a shared universal part pool, "each manufacturer has multiple variants for each part of a given type of weapon" — manufacturer identity was moved from being one part slot (the Body, in earlier games) to gating the *entire* part pool a gun draws from. — [Borderlands 3 Weapons, Fandom wiki (via search summary)](https://borderlands.fandom.com/wiki/Borderlands_3_Weapons)
- **Tediore**: reload behavior is itself the manufacturer's signature mechanic — the weapon is thrown like a grenade on reload and explodes on impact, with a fresh copy "digistructing" into the player's hands; this is explicitly framed as expressing the brand's in-fiction "cheap and disposable" corporate identity through mechanics, not just flavor text. — [How Borderlands' Guns Reflect Their Manufacturers' Values, gamerant.com](https://gamerant.com/borderlands-guns-symbolism-manufacturer-values-beliefs-style-tediore-dahl-jakobs/); [Tediore, Borderlands Fandom Wiki](https://borderlands.fandom.com/wiki/Tediore)
- **Maliwan**: manufactures elemental weapons exclusively, with higher "tech" (elemental) regeneration/uptime, positioned in-fiction as a prestige/high-tech brand. — [Borderlands Weapon Manufacturer Tier List, gamerant.com](https://gamerant.com/borderlands-weapon-manufacturer-tier-list-best-worst-guns-maliwan-vladof/)
- **Jakobs**: never manufactures elemental weapons; instead trades elemental versatility for very high base damage, low fire rate/reload speed, and high critical-hit multipliers — an explicit "keep it simple, no gimmicks" behavioral rule rather than a stat curve. — [Borderlands Weapon Manufacturer Tier List, gamerant.com](https://gamerant.com/borderlands-weapon-manufacturer-tier-list-best-worst-guns-maliwan-vladof/)

### Inferences
- Borderlands' key structural move — most directly transferable to a tabletop "source × acquisition method → ability" system — is that **manufacturer (≈ "source") does not add its own stat-line on top of a shared part pool; it instead selects which sub-pool of parts/behaviors is eligible in the first place.** This is a stronger, more legible constraint than PoE's tag-weight system: rather than "everything is possible but weighted differently," Borderlands makes whole categories of behavior categorically unavailable to a given manufacturer, which is what lets a player recognize a gun's manufacturer at a glance from its behavior alone. This directly matches the "source" half of the tabletop project's "source × acquisition method" pairing: source could gate an eligible behavior-pool the same way manufacturer gates a parts-pool, rather than just flavoring a generic ability.
- The shift from Borderlands 1/2 (parts could mix across manufacturers, with only the Body part fixing manufacturer identity) to Borderlands 3 (no cross-manufacturer mixing; every manufacturer has its own full part-set) suggests the earlier, more combinatorially "open" version was judged to produce less legible/coherent results, and the design corrected toward *more* structural constraint over time — an instructive parallel to Diablo III's Loot-2.0 correction noted above. Both franchises moved from "more open randomness" toward "more constrained, categorically-gated randomness" after shipping the more open version first.

## What happens when this genre wants a "signature"/unique effect distinct from ordinary rolled stats — is there a clean pattern for mixing rolled and hand-authored content?

### Takeaway
Across all three franchises, the clean, recurring pattern is: **generated content and "signature" content are structurally different data types that occupy different slots and follow different rules, and they are composed additively on the same object rather than the signature effect being drawn from the same pool as the generated stats.** The rolled/generated layer handles magnitude and minor variation; the hand-authored layer handles identity, behavior-change, and "this specific thing is special," and is exempted from the normal selection constraints (tiering, group-exclusivity, weighting) that govern the generated layer.

### Cited Findings
- Diablo II: Unique/Set items are a categorically different data structure ("no rules to the mods that can be on a unique, unlike with magical and rare items") layered with fixed name + preselected property list, versus Magic/Rare items which are purely table-rolled. — [Unique Items, PureDiablo](https://www.purediablo.com/diablo-2/uniques)
- Diablo III: the Legendary Power (the "signature" effect) is placed in a reserved Secondary-affix slot specifically so it structurally cannot be crowded out or compete with the item's rolled Primary stats — composition, not competition, is enforced by slot reservation. — [Legendary Affixes, Diablo Wiki](https://www.diablowiki.net/Legendary_Affixes)
- Diablo IV: the Codex of Power stores a small, discoverable, hand-authored set of Legendary Aspects/Temper recipes (found once, then reusable indefinitely) that a player can apply to an otherwise fully generated item — the "signature" content is decoupled from any specific item drop and instead becomes a reusable, categorized library the player draws from and imprints onto generated items. — [Tempering Guide, Maxroll.gg](https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/tempering-guide)
- Borderlands: the manufacturer-specific behavioral signature (Tediore's throw-reload, Maliwan's elemental-only, Jakobs's non-elemental-high-crit) is not a "rolled part" competing with other parts at all — it's a top-level categorical gate applied before any part-rolling happens, so the signature behavior is guaranteed present on every gun from that manufacturer rather than being one possible roll outcome among many. — [How Borderlands' Guns Reflect Their Manufacturers' Values, gamerant.com](https://gamerant.com/borderlands-guns-symbolism-manufacturer-values-beliefs-style-tediore-dahl-jakobs/)

### Inferences
- The genre's consensus answer to "rolled vs. authored" is not a spectrum resolved by degree of randomness on a single table — it's an **architectural separation**: (1) a small, curated, hand-authored table of "identity" effects (unique items, legendary powers, aspects/temper recipes, manufacturer behavior rules) that is looked up by category/source rather than rolled by weight, composed with (2) a large, systematic, weighted/tiered table of "magnitude" effects that is rolled normally. The two tables don't compete for the same output slot, and the hand-authored table is deliberately kept small and often reusable/discoverable (Codex of Power, manufacturer identity) rather than infinitely variable.
- For the tabletop project's stated problem ("generate a character ability from source × acquisition method, without an arbitrary hand-authored lookup table and without a fully open, uncapped pool"), this suggests the load-bearing move used by every one of these games is to **let "source" gate a bounded sub-pool of eligible behavior-templates (Borderlands' manufacturer-gated parts, PoE's tag-matched mod pool), and let "acquisition method" determine which slot/mechanism is used to fill it in (guaranteed vs. weighted-random vs. player-chosen-from-a-short-list)** — mirroring Essence (guaranteed one specific outcome) vs. Chaos Orb (fully random reroll) vs. Crafting Bench (deterministic single addition) vs. Fossil (biased-random) as different *acquisition-method* behaviors operating on the same underlying *source-gated* pool.
