# Class-from-Playstyle / Hidden Class Systems in LitRPG & Progression Fantasy

## Which specific novels/series implement a class-from-playstyle or "hidden class" system?

### Takeaway
At least five distinct RoyalRoad/published LitRPG series implement classes that emerge from an aggregate history of actions rather than a menu pick: **Delve** (by Div, aka "bestspellever" wiki subject) has the most explicit, numeric, formalized version (tracked skill ranks, kill counts, mana spent, stat ratios); **The Wandering Inn** has the best-known and most narratively developed version (classes triggered by sustained behavior, with explicit hybrid "consolidation"); **Threadbare** has a character who accumulates multiple job-like classes through repeated task-specific actions; **Defiance of the Fall** grants a first class at a level threshold "mainly based on achievements and experiences" rather than choice; and **Azarinth Healer** has a class label that can diverge from actual moment-to-moment playstyle, which is itself a useful contrast case. **The Legend of Randidly Ghosthound** is a weaker/partial fit — its "classless vs classed" tradeoff is a strategic skill-learning mechanic, not an aggregate-playstyle-determines-class-identity mechanic — included here mainly to explain why it was set aside.

### Cited Findings
- **Delve** (RoyalRoad, ongoing/completed serial): hidden classes are unlocked by meeting "Primary Requirements" (skill-rank thresholds in a category, plus a stat ratio) and "Secondary Requirements" (cumulative action counts). Example: **Dynamo** (Rare) requires Clarity to be at least 5 times greater than Focus, plus secondary requirements around recovering mana and having diverse kill types; **Legendary Dynamo** requires holding the Dynamo class with all stat points in Clarity, plus recovering at least 1,000,000 total mana points cumulatively. — [Delve, "052: Specialization"](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/25225/delve/chapter/412697/052-specialization) (via search-indexed content)
- Delve's "Beacon" hidden-class family shows the same primary/secondary pattern applied to combat-support playstyles: **Remedial Scourge Beacon** requires at least 4 skills at rank 5+ in the "Offensive Auras" skill category (primary) and at least 50 kills scored using Offensive Auras (secondary); **Remedial Warder Beacon** requires 4 skills at rank 5+ in "Defensive Auras" (primary) and mitigating at least 1,000 total damage points for self or allies (secondary); **Remedial Meta-Beacon** requires 4 skills at rank 5+ in "Aura Metamagic" (primary) and at least 5,000 mana spent through Aura Metamagic enhancements (secondary). — [Delve Wiki, "Class"](https://bestspellever.fandom.com/wiki/Class) (via search-indexed content)
- Delve explicitly names "Remedial" and "High" tiers of the same class family, indicating classes are re-evaluated/upgraded as the underlying tracked stats climb further past the initial threshold, rather than being a one-time static grant. — [Delve Wiki, "Class"](https://bestspellever.fandom.com/wiki/Class) (via search-indexed content)
- In Delve, class evolutions are shown as a selection step once thresholds are met — the chapter index includes "012: Class" and "052: Specialization" as dedicated chapters where the system presents unlocked options — implying the threshold-crossing is transparent/legible to the character (the system surfaces exact numeric options), even though which classes exist to be unlocked is not necessarily known in advance. — [Delve, "012: Class"](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/25225/delve/chapter/369623/012-class); [Delve, "052: Specialization"](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/25225/delve/chapter/412697/052-specialization)
- **The Wandering Inn** (RoyalRoad/self-published, by pirateaba): classes are acquired primarily through **action-based acquisition** — sustained behavior matching a profession triggers a class grant. The page example: Erin Solstice earned the [Innkeeper] class after cleaning up and running an abandoned inn (a repeated-behavior trigger, not a single event or menu choice). — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- The Wandering Inn also has an **authority-based** class grant path (a ruler with the right class/authority can hand a class directly to someone, e.g., an Emperor granting [General] to a character with no prior action-history prerequisite), which the source notes is a separate mechanism from playstyle-driven emergence — important for distinguishing "aggregate behavior" grants from "authority conferral" grants in the same setting. — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- The Wandering Inn's system is described as **semi-transparent**: characters know classes exist and can recognize when they've gained one, but cannot always predict exactly when/how a class will change — i.e., partially opaque rather than a fully legible formula. — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- **Threadbare** (RoyalRoad, by Andrew Seiple): rather than being classless, the titular character (a golem) accumulates a large roster of classes through repeated actions matching each — cited classes include Greater Toy Golem, Cave Bear, Ruler, Tailor, Model, Necromancer, Duelist, Animator, Enchanter, Golemist, Scout, Smith, Sculptor, and Shaman — consistent with "do the thing repeatedly, get the matching class" rather than a menu pick. — [Threadbare chapter index / search-indexed summary](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/15130/threadbare/chapter/638272/awakening)
- **Defiance of the Fall** (RoyalRoad/published, by TheFirstDefier): a character's *first* class is granted at character level 25 and is described as "mainly based on achievements and experiences" rather than a free menu choice; some classes are rare/hidden and only a small fraction of the setting's total class list is public knowledge, meaning even NPCs/other characters don't know what class a given accumulated history will produce. — [Defiance of the Fall Wiki, "Known Classes"](https://defiance-of-the-fall.fandom.com/wiki/Known_Classes); [Defiance of the Fall Wiki, "Classes"](https://defiance-of-the-fall.fandom.com/wiki/Classes) (via search-indexed content)
- **Azarinth Healer** (RoyalRoad/published, by Rhaegar): the protagonist holds a class literally named "Healer," but her actual moment-to-moment playstyle is high-mobility melee skirmishing (walk into melee range, absorb damage, self-regenerate, counter-attack) — the class label and lived playstyle are shown as divergent, which is a useful negative/contrast case for "does the label track the aggregate behavior." The series uses a **dual-class-slot** system where "every skill, class, and ability can evolve," implying classes are not static once granted. — [Azarinth Healer Fandom Wiki](https://azarinth-healer.fandom.com/wiki/Azarinth_Healer)
- **The Legend of Randidly Ghosthound** (RoyalRoad, by Noret Flood): the salient class-related mechanic is a **classed-vs-classless tradeoff** — a character with a class can level up but is capped in the number of skills they can learn, while a classless character (like the protagonist for stretches of the story) can learn unlimited skills but cannot level up conventionally. This is a strategic build choice about *whether* to hold a class at all, not a mechanism where accumulated playstyle history algorithmically determines *which* class title you get — it is included here to explicitly rule it out as a strong match for the "aggregate playstyle → class title" pattern. — [Randidly Ghosthound Wiki, "Class"](https://randidly-ghosthound.fandom.com/wiki/Class)

### Inferences
- Across these series there appear to be (at least) three distinct sub-patterns that all get called "hidden class" or "classless" in fan/marketing shorthand, and a report synthesizing this should keep them separate: (1) **numeric-threshold unlocking** (Delve: literal tracked stats/ratios/counts cross a published-in-story threshold), (2) **behavioral-trigger narrative grant** (Wandering Inn, Threadbare: the System notices a sustained pattern of action and grants a thematically matching class, without the character seeing the exact counter), and (3) **level-gated retrospective grant** (Defiance of the Fall: at a fixed level milestone, the system looks backward over "achievements and experience" and issues a class, again without a visible formula).
- Delve is the only series in this set where the underlying mechanism is close to "an implementable game rule" as originally written (explicit ratios and cumulative counters) — the others describe the *existence* of an aggregate-tracking mechanism narratively but do not expose the counters/formulas on the page, meaning an implementer would have to invent the specific thresholds if adapting them.

## For each system found: what actions/stats are tracked, what threshold/algorithm assigns the class, and is it transparent or opaque?

### Takeaway
Delve is the clearest, most mechanically explicit system (dual-tier "Primary Requirement" stat/skill thresholds plus "Secondary Requirement" cumulative-action counters, presented to the player as legible numbers); Wandering Inn and Defiance of the Fall are narratively opaque (the existence of tracking is stated but the counters are never shown on-page); Threadbare is action-triggered per discrete job/skillset rather than a single unified axis.

### Cited Findings
- Delve primary requirements are **skill-rank counts within a category** (e.g., "at least 4 skills at or above rank 5" in a named skill category such as Offensive Auras, Defensive Auras, or Aura Metamagic) combined in some classes with a **stat ratio** (Clarity ≥ 5× Focus for Dynamo). Secondary requirements are **cumulative action counters**: kill counts (≥50 kills via Offensive Auras), cumulative damage mitigated (≥1,000 points), cumulative mana spent through a specific mechanism (≥5,000 mana via Aura Metamagic), or extreme cumulative totals for higher tiers (≥1,000,000 total mana recovered for Legendary Dynamo). — [Delve Wiki, "Class"](https://bestspellever.fandom.com/wiki/Class); [Delve, "052: Specialization"](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/25225/delve/chapter/412697/052-specialization) (via search-indexed content)
- Delve's mechanism reads as **deterministic and transparent once unlocked** — thresholds are numeric and the system surfaces the unlocked option to the character by name with the qualifying numbers implied — but **opaque in advance**, since a character doesn't necessarily know a given hidden class exists or what its thresholds are until requirements are already met or nearly met. — [Delve, "012: Class"](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/25225/delve/chapter/369623/012-class)
- Wandering Inn is **partially opaque**: the system (in-story "the System") grants classes off of sustained behavior, and while characters can retrospectively recognize *why* they got a class (e.g., innkeeping behavior → [Innkeeper]), they cannot forward-predict the exact trigger point, and class change intervals are usually tied to level milestones ("every ten levels") with off-schedule "off-classes" as an exception — meaning there is a hidden cadence/threshold logic operating that is not exposed to the character. — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- Defiance of the Fall's grant is **opaque/retrospective**: reaching level 25 triggers a class grant that looks back over "achievements and experiences," but the wiki-sourced summary gives no explicit formula, weighting, or visible counter — consistent with a "surprise reveal" mechanism rather than a transparent one. — [Defiance of the Fall Wiki, "Known Classes"](https://defiance-of-the-fall.fandom.com/wiki/Known_Classes) (via search-indexed content)
- Threadbare's mechanism is **action-triggered per discrete activity** rather than a single blended-axis score — performing tailoring work yields a Tailor-type class progression, performing combat yields Duelist/Cave-Bear-type progression, etc. — implying the underlying tracker (if formalized) would need one counter per candidate class rather than one shared melee/magic axis. — [Threadbare summary via search index](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/15130/threadbare/chapter/638272/awakening)

### Inferences
- The genre pattern splits along a spectrum from "shown-your-work" (Delve) to "trust the narrator" (Wandering Inn, Defiance of the Fall): the more a series leans into explicit inventory/stat-sheet chapters (Delve is known for these), the more likely the class-grant mechanism is also spelled out numerically; series that foreground character drama over system transparency (Wandering Inn) tend to keep the exact trigger a mystery even to the protagonist.

### Gaps
- No source found gives Azarinth Healer's specific numeric thresholds for how the dual-class-slot evolution mechanism selects or reveals new classes/evolutions — only that "every skill, class, and ability can evolve" and that class benefits include flat stat bonus packages (e.g., +10 Vitality, +5 Strength, +5 Dexterity, +10 Intelligence, +10 Wisdom for the base Healer class, per the Azarinth Healer wiki), which describes *what a class grants*, not *how it gets assigned*. — [Azarinth Healer Fandom Wiki](https://azarinth-healer.fandom.com/wiki/Azarinth_Healer)

## How do hybrid/multiclass outcomes get handled when history is genuinely mixed?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented hybrid mechanism is The Wandering Inn's named "consolidation," where two sufficiently-advanced classes merge into a new named hybrid class; Delve's category-based unlocking implies multiple simultaneous hidden classes can be held/pursued in parallel rather than forcing a single verdict, though no source confirms an explicit "50/50 mixed history" tie-breaking rule in either series.

### Cited Findings
- The Wandering Inn: **class consolidation** — "[Warrior] and [Strategist] class can merge to form a [Commander] class," and a more extreme multi-class merge is illustrated by a character whose [Princess] + [Warrior] + [Barmaid] + [Beast Tamer] + [Carer] + [Tactician] classes consolidated into a single named hybrid, [Worldly Princess]. Consolidation is described as requiring a secondary class to be advanced "sufficiently to inform" the primary one — i.e., a plurality/threshold-based merge rather than an even 50/50 split rule. — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- Delve's hidden-class list includes multiple parallel categories (Scourge Beacon from Offensive Auras, Warder Beacon from Defensive Auras, Meta-Beacon from Aura Metamagic, Dynamo from a Clarity/Focus stat ratio) that a character could in principle qualify for more than one of; the source material available does not state what happens if a character qualifies for two at once (no explicit tie-break, refusal, or forced-hybrid rule was found). — [Delve Wiki, "Class"](https://bestspellever.fandom.com/wiki/Class)

### Inferences
- "Named hybrid class" (Wandering Inn's [Commander], [Worldly Princess]) appears to be the dominant genre solution when it's addressed at all — authors seem to prefer minting a new evocative title for a mixed history over either refusing classification or picking a plurality winner and discarding the rest.

### Gaps
- No source found specifies an explicit numeric tie-breaking rule (e.g., "within 10% of each other triggers hybrid, otherwise majority wins") in any series researched — this appears to be left as authorial narrative judgment rather than a stated formula in every case examined, including Delve despite its otherwise-explicit numeric thresholds.
- Could not confirm whether Defiance of the Fall, Threadbare, or Azarinth Healer have any explicit hybrid-class rule at all (only Wandering Inn's consolidation mechanic was documented with specifics).

## What happens when playstyle shifts significantly after a class is already assigned?

### Takeaway
Wandering Inn and Delve both explicitly support post-grant evolution/change (Wandering Inn via "specialization," "advancement," and "circumstantial shift" sub-types; Delve via "Remedial" → "High" tiers gated on continued accumulation) — neither series treats an initially granted class as permanently locked, though Wandering Inn also shows classes becoming maladaptive labels that persist until circumstances force a relabel.

### Cited Findings
- Wandering Inn documents three re-evaluation pathways after initial grant: **specialization** (a [Warrior] who focuses exclusively on spear combat can become [Spearmaster]), **advancement** (a [Tactician] progressing to [Strategist]), and **circumstantial shift** (a [Warrior] permanently injured becomes [Wounded Warrior]) — all three show the class label tracking a *change* in aggregate behavior/circumstance after the original grant, not a one-time lock. — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- Wandering Inn class changes "usually happen at ten-level intervals," with exceptions called "off-classes" for changes that occur outside that cadence — implying the system re-checks/re-derives the class at fixed checkpoints by default, with an escape valve for sufficiently dramatic shifts. — [The Wandering Inn Wiki, "Classes"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- Delve shows the same base class family recurring at "Remedial" and "High" tiers (e.g., Remedial Scourge Beacon vs. an implied High Scourge Beacon), consistent with continued accumulation past the original threshold triggering an upgraded version of the same class rather than a brand-new unrelated title. — [Delve Wiki, "Class"](https://bestspellever.fandom.com/wiki/Class)

### Inferences
- None of the sources describe a class becoming permanently locked in a way that actively resists updating — the closest to "lock-in" found is Wandering Inn's ten-level cadence, which delays re-evaluation rather than preventing it outright.

### Gaps
- Defiance of the Fall, Threadbare, and Azarinth Healer sources retrieved did not specify whether/how a granted class re-evaluates after a major playstyle shift (Azarinth Healer's "every skill, class, and ability can evolve" implies *some* post-grant change mechanism exists, but no threshold or trigger detail was found).
- No source quantifies exactly how much playstyle deviation is needed to trigger Wandering Inn's off-schedule "off-class" changes.

## Author/fan meta-discussion: why choose a hidden/emergent class system over menu-choice, and what problems does it solve?

### Takeaway
Direct on-record author interviews specifically framing hidden/classless systems as a solution to power-gaming, decision paralysis, or pacing were not found in the time available; the strongest available meta-commentary is a TV Tropes analysis-page observation (via search index) that class systems "don't save time or allow anyone more choices" and "also can't help with choice paralysis when they force you into making choices all the same" — i.e., a genre-critical framing suggesting emergent/hidden systems exist partly *because* menu-driven class choice doesn't actually solve the problems it's assumed to solve.

### Cited Findings
- A TV Tropes "Character Class System" analysis-page passage, surfaced via search index, argues that traditional menu-driven class systems "don't save time or allow anyone more choices, but also can't help with choice paralysis when they force you into making choices all the same" — framing rigid class menus as a design problem rather than a solution, which is the closest found to an explicit rationale for why a story might instead use an emergent/hidden system. — [TV Tropes, Analysis/Character Class System](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Analysis/CharacterClassSystem) (via search-indexed content; direct page fetch returned HTTP 403)
- A RoyalRoad forum thread titled "Guide: How to make a litrpg [system]" exists and is likely to contain author-side design discussion of class-system tradeoffs, but I was unable to retrieve its content (HTTP 403 on direct fetch) to extract specific quotes. — [RoyalRoad Forums, "Guide: How to make a litrpg [system]"](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/120806)
- The RoyalRoad-hosted story "Unbound Ascension" is explicitly subtitled/marketed as a "classless system apocalypse" and is described (via search index) as featuring a protagonist who "doesn't blindly accept everything the system presents" and instead "questions the information received, and consider[s] possible consequences before making important decisions" — suggesting at least one author uses a classless/emergent framing specifically to enable a more skeptical, agency-driven protagonist rather than a menu-picking one, as a narrative-pacing/characterization choice. — [Unbound Ascension (RoyalRoad)](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/155037/unbound-ascension-classless-system-apocalypse)

### Inferences
- Given the difficulty accessing TV Tropes and RoyalRoad forum threads directly (repeated 403s), the genre-meta-discussion layer of this research question is the weakest-sourced section of this report; a follow-up pass using a tool that can render RoyalRoad/TV Tropes pages directly (rather than relying on search-engine snippet extraction) would likely surface much more author-quoted material, since both sites are known hubs for exactly this kind of craft discussion.

### Gaps
- No direct author-interview quote (blog, podcast, AMA) was located and verified specifically addressing hidden/emergent class design as a deliberate answer to power-gaming or decision paralysis. This should be flagged to the report writer as an open gap rather than papered over.
