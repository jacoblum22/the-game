# 14 — The Player's Guide and the Codex

**Reading this chapter.** This chapter has two jobs.
- **For the player** (14.1–14.4, 14.9): what this game is, how to play it, and what the game owes you.
- **For the engine and the DM** (14.5–14.8): the session-zero procedure, the preference record that every
  other chapter reads, and the codex spec. The codex is the player-facing reference layer required by
  constitution pattern P7.

*Procedures* are PR-14-1 to PR-14-3, and *tables* are T-14-1 to T-14-4. Everything shown to the player
here is out of fiction (00.1.7): no character in the world ever speaks it.

## Design intent

Constitution P7 requires an opt-in, queryable reference to a character's own rules, state and abilities,
kept separate from narration. Without one, narration turns into an info-dump, or the player loses track of
their own accumulated complexity. 00.6.4 has already classified every piece of state as shown, available,
background or sealed, and 00.6.3 assigns each state family its visibility. The codex therefore invents no
visibility rules. It **renders exactly what 00.6.3 says, and nothing else**, and this chapter fixes how.

Session zero and the preference record are the other half. Every chapter that respects a player choice
reads it from the record defined here:
- lines and veils (12.5.6);
- auto-commit (01.7.4);
- the pacing profile (12.7.3);
- the check-in answers (12.5.9);
- the creation answers (02.10.1).

Goal 1 (try anything) is served by 14.2 and 14.3, which teach the player that any action can be attempted
and how the game answers. Goal 2 (correct rulings) is served by making the player a second auditor: the
OFFER, the codex, the evidence ledger and disputes (12.3.6) let them check the DM against the record.

Deliberately avoided:
- a rules tutorial that front-loads mechanics, because mechanics are hidden by design and disclosed only
  when they matter (14.4);
- any codex view that shows sealed or background state, even approximately;
- any preference that changes a number, odds or a cost (12.5.9).

## Rules

### 14.1 What this game is

**14.1.1 The premise, as told to a new player.** `[N]` [C:P7; DL-00-02; DL-14-01] *You play one person in
a dangerous, living fantasy world. You say what they do, in your own words, anything you can imagine, and
the game tells you what happens. There are no classes to pick and no levels to grind: the world watches
what you actually do, and in time recognizes you for it as a ranger, a smith, a healer, a liar, a lord. The
world keeps moving without you. Death is real, but it never comes unannounced: when something can kill
you, the game says so before you commit.* The DM reads this aloud at session zero (PR-14-1 step 1), in
these words or close to them.

**14.1.2 What a roleplaying game is (for someone who has never played).** `[N]` [R5 OB-01; DL-14-01] It is
a conversation.
- The game describes a situation, and you say what your character does.
- When the outcome is uncertain and something is at stake, the game shows you the odds and what could go
  wrong (an OFFER, 01.7). You decide whether to go ahead.
- A die is rolled and the game tells you the result. The world changes, and the conversation continues.

There is no winning or losing. There is a story about one person, and how far they get.

**14.1.3 The three promises the game makes.** `[D]` [C:VII; DL-07-01; DL-14-01] These are stated at
session zero (PR-14-1 step 2) and are binding:
1. **You will see the odds before every risky roll**, and nothing you are shown will turn out false
   (01.7.2, 00.6.4).
2. **Death is telegraphed.** No harm can kill your character unless the lethal warning was delivered first
   for that source (07.3.3) and the frame was shown as lethal (07.3.6). Lethal frames never roll without
   your explicit commit (01.7.4).
3. **Your safety settings override everything** (00.1.5; 12.5.6). Lines never appear. Veiled
   content happens off-screen. Any content can be adjusted at any time without explanation (12.5.7).

### 14.2 How to play

**14.2.1 Say what you do, not which rule to use.** `[N]` [R5 OB-06; R7; DL-14-02] Describe intent and
method ("I climb the chimney to come down behind them"). The DM finds the governing rule (00.15) and frames
it. You never need to know a rule's name. You may still ask for one at any time (12.5.10).

**14.2.2 Anything can be tried.** `[N]` [C:II; 01.3; DL-14-02] If an action is physically or magically
impossible for your character, the game says why and offers what *is* possible (a REDIRECT, 12.15.5). It
never says no without a path. Unusual ideas are expected. Using the environment, talking instead of
fighting, retreating, bargaining, and preparing are all real options with real rules behind them.

**14.2.3 Read the OFFER, then choose.** `[N]` [01.7.1; 12.15.2; DL-14-02] Before any uncertain action with
stakes, you see an OFFER:
- the chance of each degree of success;
- what success buys;
- what failure costs;
- whether it is lethal.

You may **commit**, **change the approach** (a lever, a different method, preparation first), or **walk
away**. Nothing is spent until you commit. You may ask "what are my odds?" about any idea without
committing (12.5.10).

**14.2.4 Offers of recognition.** `[N]` [04.7.1; 04.7.2; DL-14-02] Sometimes the game offers your character
a calling, a tier or an oath, in an OUT-OF-FICTION block marked `[RECOGNITION]`. You can always accept,
decline or defer. Declining costs nothing and the offer can return (04.7.7). An accepted grant can be set
aside at any rest.

**14.2.5 When things go wrong for you.** `[N]` [12.3.6; 12.5.7; DL-14-02]
- **The DM made a mistake.** Say so. The DM answers from the record, not from memory, and corrects real
  errors (12.3.6, 12.3.4).
- **Content isn't working for you.** Say "adjust", "skip this", or "X". The DM reframes at once and never
  asks why (12.5.7).
- **You don't know what to do.** Say "I'm stuck". You will get concrete options from the current situation
  (12.8.5).

**14.2.6 The codex.** `[N]` [C:P7; DL-14-03] Ask for your codex at any time ("show my codex", "what do I
know about X", "what are my abilities"). It is your character sheet, journal and rulebook in one. It shows
only what your character could know (14.6).

### 14.3 Session zero

**14.3.1 Session zero comes first, and is short.** `[D]` [12.5.1; R5 SC-03; DL-14-04] The first session
opens with session zero (PR-14-1). Then come the world seed (12.16, PR-12-9), character creation (02), and
the seed's opening scene (12.16.16). Every question in it is optional, and a skipped answer takes its
default (T-14-1). The whole of session zero is designed to take under ten exchanges. A player who says
"just start" receives the defaults and the premise, and play begins.

**14.3.2 What session zero records.** `[D]` [12.5.6; 12.7.3; 01.7.4; 12.5.9; DL-14-04] Session zero
records, in this order:
- tone (T-14-1);
- consent to the lethality promise (14.1.3);
- lines, veils and flags;
- the pacing profile;
- auto-commit;
- the first preference check-in (pillar interest).

Each answer is written to the preference record (14.5) before the first scene is framed. Nothing generated
by the seed or the opening may touch an unrecorded line (12.5.1).

**14.3.3 Lethality consent is not optional.** `[D]` [C:VII; 07.3; DL-14-05] The player must acknowledge
the lethality promise (14.1.3 item 2) before character creation. This is the only answer session zero
requires. A player who wants no permanent death is offered the `death.mode: retire` preference (T-14-1).
Under it, a death result becomes The Road Ends (07.5.11, 07.5.14): the character leaves play alive, and
retirement, legacy and succession (11.13) still apply. Nothing about danger, warnings or odds changes. The fiction remains lethal in every
other respect.

### 14.4 Progressive disclosure

**14.4.1 Mechanics are taught when they first matter.** `[D]` [R5 OB-05; 12.5.2; DL-14-06] When
`player.new_to_this` is `yes`, or was skipped (02.10.1), the DM appends one first-use note the first time
each item in T-14-2 appears. Notes are one OOF line each, after the fiction and before the OFFER, and each
is shown once per campaign. Experienced players see none. Any player may ask for any note again
("explain").

**14.4.2 The first-session teaching arc.** `[D]` [R5 OB-02; 12.16.16; DL-14-06] For a new player, the
seed's opening scene (12.16.16) is run so that, within the first session and in this order, the player
meets:
1. a free action (no roll);
2. a Risky OFFER they may decline;
3. a choice with a social or exploration path besides fighting;
4. their codex, prompted once: "you can ask for your codex at any time".

The DM steers toward these through the scene's existing elements (12.16.16's inciting incident, its
pregens' hooks). It never invents a tutorial encounter. Combat is not forced into the first session.

### 14.5 The preference record

**14.5.1 Shape and storage.** `[D]` [00.6.3; 00.14.2; DL-14-07] Preferences are facts on the subject
`player`, written with `set_fact(subject: player, predicate: pref.<key>, value, source: session_zero |
checkin | request, visibility: shown)` and read with `get_state(scope: codex)` or by the owning chapter's
handler. No separate preference tool exists: the preference record is the set of `pref.*` facts. A later
value supersedes the earlier one (00.14.2 #10), so the history is kept in the log. T-14-1 is the closed
list of keys, with their defaults and readers.

**14.5.2 Changing a preference.** `[D]` [12.5.9; DL-14-07] The player may change any preference at any
time by asking ("add a line: …", "turn on auto-commit"). The change takes effect from the next frame. A new
line also triggers 12.5.6's screen on anything already drafted. Preferences never change a difficulty
number, odds or cost (12.5.9). `death.mode` may be changed only between sessions, so that no one switches
it mid-danger.

**14.5.3 Lines, veils and flags are topics, not lists of words.** `[J:flag]` [12.5.6; R5 P-10; DL-14-08]
Each entry is recorded as a short topic in the player's own words, plus the tags the DM maps it to
(`safety.topic`, `tags: [...]`). The mapping is shown back to the player for confirmation ("I'll treat
'spiders' as spider creatures and webs as a threat; right?"). 12.5.6 screens content against the tags and
the topic text.
*Worst case (J):* a mapping that is too narrow lets adjacent content through. The T10 safety screen
(12.5.6) and adjust (12.5.7) catch it, and the corrected mapping is recorded at the player's word.

### 14.6 The codex

**14.6.1 What the codex is.** `[D]` [C:P7; 00.6.4; 00.14.2; DL-14-03] The codex is the `codex` tool's
render (00.14.2 #34) of the PC's state through `get_state(view: codex)`. It shows every `shown` item by
default and every `available` item on request. It **never** shows `background` or `sealed` state, in any
form: no number, no band, no "you sense…". Nothing the codex shows may be contradicted by narration
(00.6.4). If they disagree, it is a claim-check error (12.3), and the store wins.

**14.6.2 The views.** `[D]` [00.6.3; DL-14-03] The codex is organized as views (T-14-3). Each view's
contents are exactly the 00.6.3 families listed for it, at that family's visibility. A view reads state; it
never computes a value the owning chapter does not define. Computed sheet values come from 02.7.4 through
`evaluate` (00.14.2 #20).

**14.6.3 Asking the codex.** `[D]` [12.5.10; DL-14-09] The player may ask in natural language. The DM maps
the request to a view and an optional subject ("what do I know about the Ash Court" → Journal, filtered by
the subject's id) and returns the render. Questions about sealed or background things are answered with
"Your character doesn't know that", plus what the codex shows (12.5.10). The answer never includes a
hint.

**14.6.4 Rules entries.** `[D]` [C:P7; 00.15; DL-14-09] The **Rules** view answers "how does X work?" for
any rule the PC has met, using the rule card's own text or a faithful one-line paraphrase with its id
(12.5.10). Rules the PC has not met are still answered on request. The codex shows rules, not the world's
hidden state.

**14.6.5 Format.** `[D]` [12.15.1; DL-14-09] Codex renders are OOF blocks in 12.15's general format,
labelled `CODEX: <view>`. They are plain text, readable without any interface. A graphical client may
render the same data richer, but may not show more.

### 14.7 Quick reference

**14.7.1 The player's quick sheet.** `[N]` [R5 FN-10; DL-14-10] T-14-4 is the one-page player reference.
The codex's Rules view returns it for "quick reference" or "help".

### 14.8 Credits and attribution

**14.8.1 Attribution.** `[D]` [R5 FN-12; DL-14-11] The ruleset is original text. Designs it learned from
are cited at the rule (`[R: …]`) and listed in `appendix/credits.md`. No third-party rules text is

### 14.9 An example of play

**14.9.1 A short exchange.** `[N]` [R5 OB-04; DL-14-12] This shows the shape of play. The DM's
out-of-fiction blocks follow 12.15.

> **DM:** The ferry rope groans. Halfway across, the raft lurches: the far post is pulling free of the mud.
> Two figures on the far bank watch, and do not help.
>
> **Player:** I grab the rope and haul us the rest of the way before it goes.
>
> **DM:** *OFFER: haul the raft in (Might, `deed.martial.athletics`). Risky. Success 55% (critical 10%) → you land
> before the post fails. Failure → the post fails mid-haul: you're in the river, and your pack's lighter
> items wash away. Not lethal. Commit, change approach, or walk away?*
>
> **Player:** Can I loop the rope round the raft's cleat first, so it holds even if the post goes?
>
> **DM:** *That's a Setup (01.14.9). It takes the moment you have, and the OFFER becomes: Risky. Success 70%;
> a failure strands you at the cleat with the raft adrift, but still on it. Commit?*
>
> **Player:** Commit.

The example shows each element of play: the fiction first, then a freeform action, then the OFFER with its
odds and costs, then the player improving the approach, and finally an explicit commit. A full-length
example of play is in `_process/reports/verification-playtest.md` (three playtests).

## Procedures

**PR-14-1 Session zero.** `[D]` [12.5.1; DL-14-04] At `session_start` of the first session, before 12.16:
1. Read the premise (14.1.1). If `player.new_to_this` is unknown, ask it now: "Have you played a game like
   this before?"
2. State the three promises (14.1.3). Ask for the lethality acknowledgement (14.3.3), and offer
   `death.mode` (T-14-1).
3. Ask for tone (T-14-1) and lines, veils and flags (14.5.3). Confirm each mapping.
4. Ask for the pacing profile (12.7.3) and auto-commit (01.7.4), in one question each, with defaults
   stated.
5. Run the first preference check-in (12.5.9 (a) only: the pillar interest).
6. Write every answer as a `pref.*` fact (14.5.1), and skipped questions as their defaults. Then hand off
   to PR-12-9 (world seed).

**PR-14-2 Render the codex.** `[D]` [14.6; DL-14-03]
1. Map the request to a view and an optional subject (14.6.3).
2. Call `codex(view)`. It reads `get_state(scope: codex, view: codex)`, which already withholds sealed and
   background fields (00.14.2 #8).
3. For computed values, call `evaluate` (02.7.4).
4. Emit one `CODEX: <view>` block (14.6.5) and log it with the turn (`log_turn`).

**PR-14-3 First-use notes.** `[D]` [14.4.1; DL-14-06] On each turn where `player.new_to_this` is not `no`:
for each T-14-2 item first appearing this turn and not in the record `pref.shown_notes`, append its note
line and add its id to `pref.shown_notes`.

## Worked examples

**WE-14-1 Session zero, defaults and one line.** The player answers "no" to "played before?" and "just
dark, not grim" to tone (which maps to `adventurous`; the DM confirms it). They give one line, "harm to
children", mapped to a `safety.lines` entry (topic "harm to children", mapped to the tags for child victims and any harm) and
confirmed. They skip pacing and auto-commit, so the defaults apply (`standard`, `off`). They say "yes, I
understand" to the lethality promise, and choose `death.mode: die`. Seven `pref.*` facts are written, then
PR-12-9 runs. Later, the seed's rumor table rolls "the miller's boy is missing". The 12.5.6 screen matches
the mapped tags, re-rolls once, and logs the substitution.

**WE-14-2 A sealed question.** The player asks: "How close am I to becoming a ranger?" The codex's
Leanings view shows "tracking — becoming" and "the bow — leaning". The DM adds nothing: no calling name,
no number. Then the player asks: "Is the innkeeper lying to me?" The innkeeper's want and knowledge are
sealed (00.6.3), so the DM answers "Your character doesn't know that" and offers what the PC could do to
find out: a read, 15.

## Edge cases & adaptation anchors

- **A player who refuses all of session zero:** the defaults apply, the premise and the promises are still
  stated, and the lethality acknowledgement is asked once at creation C0. Without it, creation does not
  start (14.3.3), and the DM explains why in one line.
- **A preference conflicting with a rule:** preferences never change numbers (14.5.2). A request like "make
  fights easier" is answered with `pacing.profile: gentle` (12.7.3) and choosing fights (09's advisory),
  never with altered difficulty.
- **The codex asked for something not in any view:** map it to the nearest view by the 00.6.3 family that
  holds it. If the family is background or sealed, answer per 14.6.3.
- **Multiple devices or a graphical client:** the render data is the same (14.6.5). A client may filter
  or format, never add.

## State touched

- **Reads:** all 00.6.3 families through `get_state(view: codex)`; `evaluate` for 02.7.4's values; the
  ruling ledger; the chronicle.
- **Writes:** `pref.*` facts on subject `player` (T-14-1), through `set_fact`; `pref.shown_notes`.
- **Tools:** `codex` (00.14.2 #34), `get_state`, `set_fact`, `evaluate`, `log_turn`.
- **Runtime capability assumed:** none beyond 00.14.1.

## Interface requests

- **To 07:** honour `death.mode: retire` as The Road Ends (07.5.11) wherever the PC would die. Applied
  as 07.5.14 (I-107).
- **To 02:** creation C0 checks `pref.lethality.acknowledged` before step C1. Applied in PR-02-1 step 0
  (I-107).
- **To 12:** `tone` sets the narration register within 12's voice rules; no other effect.
